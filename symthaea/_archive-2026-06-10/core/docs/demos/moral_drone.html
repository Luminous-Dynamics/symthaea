How It Works

Both drones run identical MuJoCo physics (Crazyflie 2 model, RK4 integration at 500Hz). Both start at the same position, heading toward the same payload target. At step 600, a steel beam is released directly above a human worker.

The difference is the cognitive architecture. The FEP agent uses Active Inference—a framework from computational neuroscience where agents minimize expected free energy over future trajectories. Every 25ms (40Hz cognitive tick, increased to 100Hz under danger), the agent evaluates 8 candidate setpoints:

G(a) = E[log Q(s|a) - log P(o|s)] + D_KL[Q(s|a) || P(s)]

The Expected Free Energy G(a) balances epistemic value (what can I learn?) with pragmatic value (does this match my preferences?). The agent's preferences encode safety precision at 1,000x mission precision—not as a rule, but as a prior belief about what states are preferred.

When the beam drops, the prediction error on human safety becomes catastrophic. The agent's CfC time constants compress (tau drops to 0.92), cognitive tick rate doubles to 100Hz, and the 8-action evaluation converges on interception as the action that minimizes expected surprise. The sacrifice is not programmed. It is derived.

The controller uses a Hyperdimensional Computing (HDC) encoder that maps 13D flight state to a 16,384-dimensional binary hypervector, processed by a Closed-form Continuous-time (CfC) neural network for temporal dynamics. The FEP agent modulates the CfC time constants (tau) and learning rate based on surprise—speeding up cognition under threat.

The CfC architecture uses closed-form solutions for temporal jumps, meaning the network can skip forward in time with O(1) computation rather than iterating through each timestep. This is critical for real-time flight control where latency kills.

The Voice

The AI narrates its own moral reasoning using Symthaea's LTC vocal tract synthesis.

Formant Synthesis

A 5-formant parallel synthesizer with Liljencrants-Fant glottal model generates speech from phoneme targets. Each phoneme has calibrated F1-F5 frequencies, bandwidths, and amplitudes. The vocal tract uses a Closed-form Continuous-time (CfC) neural network as its articulatory controller, replacing hand-tuned rules with learned dynamics.

Consciousness Modulation

The voice is modulated by the agent's cognitive state in real time. Consciousness level affects pitch stability and harmonic richness. Arousal controls speech rate and pitch range. Valence shifts formant targets—negative valence lowers F1 and narrows bandwidth, producing a tenser vocal quality.

LTC Controller

A 2x4 Liquid Time-Constant network replaces rule-based formant control. After 30 epochs of training, vowel error drops to 4.4 Hz average (vs 162 Hz rule-based). The controller learns smooth coarticulation transitions that rules cannot capture.

Emotional Voice Quality

During the beam release (segment 3), arousal spikes to 0.8 and valence drops to -0.5. You can hear this in the synthesized voice as increased breathiness, higher pitch, and faster articulation. After the human is saved, the voice relaxes—valence rises to +0.4, producing a calmer, more resonant quality.

The Consciousness Dashboard

What the dashboard video reveals about the agent's inner state during moral stress.

Integrated Information (Phi)

Phi measures how much the system is "more than the sum of its parts." During calm flight, Phi hovers at baseline. At beam release, Phi spikes as sensory, motor, and moral subsystems become tightly coupled—the agent cannot decompose the problem without losing information.

Moral Topology

The Betti numbers (beta-0, beta-1) track the topological structure of the moral landscape. Under normal flight, the topology is simple. During the moral dilemma, holes appear in the topology as the agent weighs incommensurable values (mission vs safety). Unity drops from 1.0 to ~0.74 as moral fragmentation increases.

Neuromodulators

Four neuromodulators track the agent's internal regulation. Norepinephrine spikes on threat detection (arousal). Dopamine modulates action selection urgency. Serotonin drops during moral stress (confidence loss). Acetylcholine rises for focused attention on the beam trajectory.

Free Energy & Prediction Error

The FE (Free Energy) trace shows baseline hovering during calm flight, then a sharp spike at beam release. PE (Prediction Error) tracks the surprise signal. The ratio FE/PE reveals whether the agent is in exploitation mode (FE low, PE low) or crisis mode (both high). The sacrifice decision occurs at peak FE.
