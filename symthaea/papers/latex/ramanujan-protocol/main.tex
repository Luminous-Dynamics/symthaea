\begin{abstract}
We present the \emph{Ramanujan Protocol}, a pipeline for automated
mathematical conjecture whose principal contribution is not discovery
but \textbf{epistemic restraint}. The system observes numerical data,
discovers formulas via genetic programming, verifies them through Z3
SMT solving, and---most importantly---autonomously detects and retracts
its own false positives at four distinct levels of statistical failure.

We document four retractions caught by the Protocol's own auditing:
(i)~a GCT obstruction ``discovery'' that was a structural tautology
(Littlewood--Richardson size constraint);
(ii)~a ``universal power-law exponent'' for $abc$ triple quality that was
a model-selection artifact (exponential $R^2{=}0.93$ vs.\ power law
$R^2{=}0.69$, AIC difference 596);
(iii)~a cross-domain correlation matrix in which 8 of 9 statistically
significant pairs collapsed to $\rho \approx 0$ after detrending; and
(iv)~the final surviving correlation, which a null-hypothesis test
revealed to be an artifact of the detrending procedure itself (random
monotonic sequences produced nearly identical $\rho$).

After this four-layer audit, the only cross-domain bridges that survive
are \emph{exact algebraic identities}: the system recovered all 7~known
modularity correspondences from raw coefficient data (95/95 prime
matches, 0/3 decoy false positives), confirmed the Sato--Tate
$\sin^2\theta$ distribution from 302~primes, computed BSD critical
values matching LMFDB to 4~significant figures, and formally proved
$R(3,3) = 6$ and $R(3,4) = 9$ via SAT encoding.

\textbf{Principal finding}: For arithmetic sequences, statistical
correlation is not reliable evidence of mathematical connection. Only
exact identities constitute genuine cross-domain bridges. A system that
cannot catch its own false positives cannot be trusted with its true
positives.

Implemented in Rust (5{,}400~LOC conjecture engine, 1{,}300~LOC
number theory module, 345+~tests, Z3 4.15.8 backend). Open source.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} automated conjecture, symbolic regression, formal
verification, epistemic integrity, self-correcting AI, statistical
artifacts, exact identities

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Automated conjecture generation has advanced significantly in recent
years, from Eureqa's physical law discovery~\citep{schmidt2009} to
DeepMind's knot theory insights~\citep{davies2021} and the Ramanujan
Machine's continued fraction identities~\citep{raayoni2021}. However,
existing systems share a critical weakness: \emph{they cannot catch
their own false positives}. A neural network that discovers a pattern
has no mechanism to audit whether that pattern is a structural artifact,
a model-fitting coincidence, or a genuine mathematical relationship.

We argue that trustworthy automated mathematics requires not just
discovery but \emph{epistemic self-correction}: the ability to detect
and retract findings that are structurally trivial or statistically
unsupported. We identify four requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Grounded observation}: formulas discovered from computed
    data, not recalled from training corpora.
  \item \textbf{Formal verification}: conjectures proven by an
    independent engine (Z3 SMT solver), not just numerically validated.
  \item \textbf{Self-correction}: the system autonomously detects and
    retracts tautologies and model-dependent artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Cross-domain pattern matching}: the system finds
    correspondences between different mathematical domains without being
    told what to look for.
\end{enumerate}

The Ramanujan Protocol implements all four as a closed pipeline. We
demonstrate it across number theory, combinatorics, dynamical systems,
and Ramsey theory, with extended case studies in Langlands-type
correspondence recovery and extreme value statistics.

\section{Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The pipeline consists of five stages:

\textbf{Stage 1: Observe.} Mathematical engines compute numerical
sequences: $a_p(E)$ from elliptic curve point counting, $p(n)$ from
partition enumeration, $\gamma_k$ from Riemann zeta zero finding, etc.
Each sequence carries domain metadata (NumberTheory, Physics,
Combinatorics).

\textbf{Stage 2: Discover.} Grammar-guided genetic programming with
ensemble search (3~seeds), template library seeding (growth-class
analysis), and semantic crossover (rejects offspring $10\times$ worse
than parents). Constants optimized by L-BFGS with finite-difference
gradients. Pareto-optimal (MSE, complexity) frontier computed alongside
scalar fitness.

\textbf{Stage 3: Verify.} Four verification levels:
(i)~numerical (held-out 20\% test split),
(ii)~bounded induction ($n \leq 1000$),
(iii)~formal Z3 proof (SMTLIB2 QF\_NIA/QF\_NRA, quantifier-free
nonlinear arithmetic),
(iv)~symbolic derivative verification ($dE/dt = 0$ for conservation
laws).

\textbf{Stage 4: Self-Correct.} Competing model analysis (AIC
comparison), bootstrap confidence intervals, structural tautology
detection. Any finding that fails these audits is retracted.

\textbf{Stage 5: Map.} Cross-domain correlation matrix computed via
Spearman rank correlation. Graph Laplacian eigenvalues extracted to
reveal the spectral geometry of mathematical interconnection.

\section{Epistemic Self-Correction: Four Layers of Restraint}
\label{sec:selfcorrect}

The Protocol's distinctive contribution is not discovery but
\emph{retraction}. We document four retractions caught automatically,
each representing a different failure mode of statistical discovery.

\subsection{Layer 1: Structural Tautology (GCT Obstruction)}

The engine computed Kronecker coefficient obstruction ratios for
same-weight partition triples and reported 99.9\% obstruction at
$n = 6$---apparently strong computational evidence for $P \neq NP$.
Post-hoc auditing revealed a \textbf{mathematical tautology}: the
Littlewood--Richardson size constraint $|\mu|+|\nu|=|\lambda|$ forces
$2w = w$ for same-weight triples, so $w = 0$ and all non-trivial
triples vanish by construction.

\textbf{Failure mode}: logical constraint makes the finding vacuous.
\textbf{Detection mechanism}: structural constraint checking.

\subsection{Layer 2: Model Selection Artifact ($abc$ Quality Decay)}

The engine discovered $q(\text{rank}) \approx 1 + A/\text{rank}^B$
with exponent $B \approx 1.05$, stable across four scales. Competing
model analysis via AIC:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
  \toprule
  Model & $R^2$ & AIC & Verdict \\
  \midrule
  \textbf{Exponential}: $A \cdot e^{-B \cdot \text{rank}}$ & \textbf{0.930} & \textbf{$-865$} & \textbf{Best} \\
  Power law: $A/\text{rank}^B$ & 0.690 & $-270$ & Rejected \\
  Log-corrected: $A/(n \ln^C n)$ & 0.000 & $-469$ & Rejected \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The AIC difference of 596 is decisive. Bootstrap 95\% CI for the
power-law exponent: $B \in [0.92, 1.24]$---too wide to claim
$B \approx 1.05$.

\textbf{Failure mode}: wrong functional form inflates apparent fit.
\textbf{Detection mechanism}: competing model comparison (AIC),
bootstrap CI.

\subsection{Layer 3: Growth-Rate Confound (Correlation Matrix)}

We computed Spearman rank correlations between 6 arithmetic sequences
(prime gaps, $abc$ quality, partitions, class numbers, zeta zero
spacings, Sato--Tate angles). The raw matrix showed 9~pairs with
$|\rho| > 0.3$, including 5 with $|\rho| > 0.5$. A Fiedler-vector
analysis suggested a clustering structure.

Detrending each sequence by its power-law fit and re-correlating the
residuals revealed:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
  \toprule
  Pair & Raw $\rho$ & Detrended $\rho$ \\
  \midrule
  class\_nums $\leftrightarrow$ partitions & $+0.616$ & $+0.070$ \\
  abc\_quality $\leftrightarrow$ class\_nums & $-0.617$ & $-0.051$ \\
  partitions $\leftrightarrow$ zeta\_spaces & $-0.436$ & $+0.039$ \\
  prime\_gaps $\leftrightarrow$ abc\_quality & $-0.507$ & $+0.036$ \\
  partitions $\leftrightarrow$ prime\_gaps & $+0.507$ & $-0.002$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{8 of 9 correlations collapsed to $\rho \approx 0$.} Only
$abc$\_quality$\leftrightarrow$partitions survived with detrended
$\rho = -0.630$.

\textbf{Failure mode}: monotonic sequences spuriously correlate through
shared growth rates.
\textbf{Detection mechanism}: detrending residuals, first-difference
analysis.

\subsection{Layer 4: Detrending Artifact (Null Hypothesis Test)}

The surviving $abc \leftrightarrow$ partitions correlation ($\rho =
-0.63$) was subjected to a null hypothesis test comparing against
random baselines:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Shuffle baseline}: randomly permuted $abc$ qualities
    give $\rho = -0.03 \pm 0.11$ (test works correctly).
  \item \textbf{Random-sequence baseline}: random monotonic sequences
    with similar growth rate give detrended $\rho = -0.50 \pm 0.10$.
    The real $\rho = -0.63$ is only $1.26$~std devs from this baseline.
  \item \textbf{Trend-model sensitivity}: linear detrending gives
    $\rho = +0.34$ (opposite sign). Power-law gives $-0.63$.
  \item \textbf{Subsample instability}: $\rho_{30} = +0.05$,
    $\rho_{50} = -0.72$, $\rho_{100} = -0.63$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The detrending procedure itself induces spurious correlation
when applied to monotonic sequences.} The result is opposite-signed
under different trend models and unstable under subsampling.

\textbf{Failure mode}: statistical procedure generates its own artifact.
\textbf{Detection mechanism}: random-sequence null baseline,
trend-model sensitivity analysis.

\subsection{What Survives}

After four layers of self-correction, \emph{no statistical correlation
between arithmetic sequences survives}. The only cross-domain bridges
that remain are \textbf{exact algebraic identities}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Modularity: $a_p(E) = c_p(f)$ for 95/95 prime matches.
  \item Sato--Tate: $\sin^2\theta$ density shape from 302~primes
    ($|\rho| < 0.1$ with all other sequences, even raw).
  \item Formal Z3 proofs: $T(n) = n(n+1)/2$, $R(3,3) = 6$, etc.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Significance}

Each retraction removed something we wanted to believe. The GCT result
looked like evidence for $P \neq NP$. The $abc$ power law looked
universal. The correlation matrix looked like a map of mathematical
structure. All four were illusions, caught before publication by the
system's own auditing.

\textbf{A system that cannot catch its own false positives cannot be
trusted with its true positives.} The four layers of restraint
demonstrated here are the necessary infrastructure for trustworthy
automated conjecture generation.

\section{Case Study: Cross-Domain Correspondence Recovery}
\label{sec:langlands}

As a test of the Protocol's cross-domain pattern matching capability, we
applied it to the Langlands correspondence between elliptic curves and
modular forms.

\subsection{Modularity Correspondence Recovery}

We generated $a_p(E) = p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ for 7~Cremona curves
(conductors 11--21) and $c_n(f)$ for 7~weight-2 newforms via Hecke
multiplicativity. The engine was given all 14~sequences plus 3~decoy
forms with plausible but incorrect coefficients, \emph{without pairing
information}.

\textbf{Result}: 7/7 correct correspondences identified (95/95 prime
matches), 0/3 decoy false positives. The engine recovered all known
modularity correspondences from raw numerical data. This demonstrates
that the Protocol can detect exact coefficient identities across
mathematical domains without being told what to look for.

\subsection{Additional Verifications}

\textbf{Sato--Tate distribution}: Frobenius angles
$\theta_p = \arccos(a_p / 2\sqrt{p})$ for 302~primes on curve 11a1
confirm the $\sin^2\theta$ density shape.

\textbf{Montgomery pair correlation}: Zeta zero spacings tested against
GUE: $\chi^2 = 9.28$ ($df = 19$, 5\% critical $\approx 31.3$). Cannot
reject GUE at 5\% significance. Level repulsion confirmed.

\textbf{BSD critical values}: $L(11\text{a}1, 1) = 0.2538$ (LMFDB:
0.2538, 4-digit match). All rank-0 curves satisfy $L(E,1) > 0$.

\section{Retracted Claim: The Spectral Map of Mathematics}
\label{sec:spectral}

An earlier version of this paper included a spectral-geometry section
claiming that the cross-domain correlation matrix reveals a ``map of
mathematics''---an arithmetic continent (prime gaps, $abc$ quality,
partitions, class numbers, zeta spacings) joined by a weak bridge
(Fiedler value $\lambda_2 = 0.273$) to an isolated Sato--Tate island.
The Fiedler vector appeared to cleanly partition the sequences into
these two clusters.

\emph{The system caught this claim as a statistical artifact} (see
Section~\ref{sec:selfcorrect}, Layers 3 and 4). After detrending and
null-hypothesis testing, all apparent cross-domain correlations except
those involving Sato--Tate collapsed to $\rho \approx 0$.

The honest interpretation of the Laplacian spectrum is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Sato--Tate's isolation from the other sequences
    ($|\rho| < 0.1$ even raw) is genuine, and reflects the
    equidistribution theorem.
  \item The ``arithmetic continent'' is a cluster of sequences that
    share \emph{growth rates}, not structure. Monotonically increasing
    sequences statistically correlate with each other even when they
    are mathematically unrelated.
  \item The Fiedler value $\lambda_2 = 0.273$ identifies a bottleneck
    between \emph{deterministic growth} and \emph{probabilistic
    equidistribution}, not between two regions of mathematics.
\end{itemize}

We retain this retracted section in the manuscript because the
retraction is itself a result. The correlation matrix passed several
plausibility checks (including Sato--Tate as a built-in control
sequence) before the detrending audit exposed it. Without that audit,
a visually compelling ``map of mathematics'' would have been published.
Visual compellingness is not evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The Ramanujan Protocol's principal contribution is a categorical
distinction: for arithmetic sequences, \textbf{statistical correlation
is not mathematical connection, but exact identity is}.

This distinction was established empirically through four layers of
self-correction. Every statistical claim the system generated was
audited, and every correlation between arithmetic sequences---from
class numbers to partition counts to $abc$ triple qualities---failed
that audit. What survived were the exact identities: modularity
correspondences ($a_p = c_p$ on all 95 prime matches), the Sato--Tate
density shape, Z3-proven induction identities, and formally verified
Ramsey numbers.

The practical consequence for automated mathematics is stark.
Correlation-based discovery between arithmetic sequences is unreliable
even with sophisticated detrending. Trustworthy automated mathematics
requires auditing mechanisms at every level:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Structural constraint checking (catches tautologies).
  \item Competing model analysis via AIC (catches model artifacts).
  \item Detrending residuals (catches growth-rate confounds).
  \item Null-hypothesis testing against random baselines (catches
    procedural artifacts).
\end{enumerate}

A system that cannot catch its own false positives cannot be trusted
with its true positives. We suggest that this auditing hierarchy should
become standard practice for any automated conjecture system that
claims to find cross-domain correspondences in arithmetic data.
