# Cover Letter: CPT: Pharmacometrics & Systems Pharmacology

Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration an Original Article entitled "Quantifying Pharmacogenomic Health Inequity: An Open-Source Equity Scoring Engine for Ancestry-Aware Drug Dosing."

CPIC guidelines represent a major advance toward precision drug therapy, yet they are predominantly validated in European-ancestry populations. No existing open-source tool systematically quantifies the population-level equity gap between the populations for which guidelines are calibrated and those to which they are applied. A 2025 scoping review identified 40 published pharmacogenomic analysis tools; none included systematic equity assessment.

We present the Pharmacogenomic Health Equity Engine, an open-source tool that computes per-drug, per-gene, per-population equity gap scores from allele frequency data. Across 11 CPIC Level A drug-gene pairs spanning 6 pharmacogenes, 7 clinical domains, and 32 guideline recommendations, we identify multiple underserved population-drug combinations where guideline applicability falls below 85%. The largest inter-ancestry disparities emerge for tacrolimus/CYP3A5 (CYP3A5*3 frequency: 94% European vs. 18% African) and efavirenz/CYP2B6 (CYP2B6*6: 38% African vs. 26% European). Using US Census data, we estimate tens of millions of individuals carry actionable phenotypes that may be inadequately addressed by European-calibrated guidelines.

The engine extends to admixed population modeling for four clinically relevant populations using linear allele blending with HWE. External validation against gnomAD v4 confirms 26/32 allele-population comparisons within 30% relative agreement. Sensitivity analysis demonstrates perfect rank stability across +/-20% allele frequency perturbations.

All code, reference data, and allele frequency tables are released under AGPL-3.0. We believe CPT: Pharmacometrics & Systems Pharmacology is the ideal venue given the journal's focus on quantitative pharmacology tools with clinical translation potential and its commitment to open science.

We confirm this manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration at another journal. No conflicts of interest exist.
