## Abstract (draft)

We investigate social dynamics in a multi-agent physics engine where agents maintain an integration metric (Φ) at thermodynamic cost. Using Symtropy, an open-source engine with five Φ-physics coupling channels, we run 9 experiment suites (2,000+ simulation runs) exploring cooperation, conflict, altruism, resource economics, and group formation. Key findings: (1) Physical bonds between resonant agents improve survival from 57% to 97% (p=0.001, d=2.44). (2) A sharp phase transition at 0.20 J/tick maintenance separates cooperative and collapsed regimes, with bistable dynamics above threshold. (3) Adversarial agents at 25% fraction collapse cooperation; cooperators under adversarial pressure evolve solidarity (resonance 0.86→1.0) rather than tribalism. (4) Algorithm-era information topology devastates cooperation vs pre-internet baselines. (5) Costly altruism [result pending]. (6) Natural group sizes emerge from thermodynamic constraints [result pending]. (7) Shared resource overcrowding [result pending]. We argue these emergent social patterns arise from the thermodynamic cost structure, not the specific consciousness metric — a universality finding for computational social physics.
