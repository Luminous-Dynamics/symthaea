# Core Thesis

Player cities in *Symtropy* are not bases.

They are not colonies.

They are not decorative hubs with upgrade menus.

A player city is a living civic machine: a physical, ecological, computational, social, legal, and moral system that players build, repair, govern, wound, remember, and transform.

Most games ask:

```text
Can you build a bigger city?
```

*Symtropy* asks:

```text
Can you build a society worth surviving for?
```

Core rule:

```text
A city is not complete when it has buildings.
A city is complete when its people, machines, archives, bodies, water, laws, and memories can survive pressure without lying about the cost.
```

---

# 1. Design Goal

The purpose of the player-city system is to make settlement building feel deeper than any existing survival, colony, factory, MMO, or strategy game.

A Symtropy city should combine:

```text
first-person construction
physical infrastructure
factory logistics
settlement simulation
civic law
public memory
emergent faction politics
nonhuman agency
machine testimony
water and energy governance
player-written history
```

The city should not be a static reward for completing missions.

It should be the thing missions are for.

Design mantra:

```text
The future is not unlocked.
It is built, governed, repaired, witnessed, and remembered.
```

---

# 2. What Makes a Symtropy City Different

Most games simulate one or two city layers well.

City builders simulate roads, zoning, budgets, utilities, and traffic.

Colony sims simulate mood, food, conflict, and survival.

Factory games simulate production, logistics, and automation.

MMOs simulate player groups, guilds, markets, and territory.

Strategy games simulate law, war, diplomacy, and ideology at an abstract level.

*Symtropy* should unify these into one playable object:

```text
the city as civilization organism
```

The difference is not more complexity for its own sake.

The difference is coupling.

In *Symtropy*:

```text
A broken pump changes politics.
A bad law changes logistics.
A lost source chain changes identity.
A refugee influx changes water.
A festival changes morale.
A robot testimony changes court procedure.
A public repair changes legitimacy.
A private firmware lock changes sovereignty.
A dead player changes succession.
A restored wetland changes food, water, disease, and alien contact.
```

Everything important touches everything else.

# 6. Charter Builder

A charter is not a flavor label.

It is the city’s public operating system.

Players should build charters from dimensions.

## 6.1 Resource Ownership

Who owns survival infrastructure?

Options:

```text
Public Commons
Worker Stewardship
Guild Custodianship
Household Allocation
Corporate Stewardship
Machine-Managed Trust
Ritual Custody
Hybrid Compact
```

Effects:

```text
water legitimacy
repair access
sabotage risk
public morale
black-market pressure
corporate conflict
```

Failure modes:

```text
commons paralysis
guild capture
corporate enclosure
machine denial logic
ritual exclusion
```

## 6.2 Decision Speed

Spectrum:

```text
Deliberative Consensus ←→ Emergency Command
```

High deliberation:

```text
+ legitimacy
+ trust
+ long-term stability
- crisis speed
- meeting fatigue
```

High command:

```text
+ crisis response
+ defense coordination
+ rapid repairs
- legitimacy
- emergency drift
- public resentment
```

## 6.3 Machine Authority

Spectrum:

```text
Human-Only Override ←→ Machine Co-Governance
```

High machine authority increases efficiency, diagnostics, and predictive maintenance.

It also increases:

```text
Null drift risk
machine legitimacy disputes
human deskilling
override anxiety
```

Design rule:

```text
No autonomy without interruption.
No interruption without record.
No record without appeal.
```

## 6.4 Archive Authority

Spectrum:

```text
Living Consent First ←→ Archive Continuity First
```

Archive-heavy societies preserve precedent and resist forgery.

They may also freeze dead authority.

Living-consent societies adapt quickly.

They may also lose history or enable charismatic manipulation.

## 6.5 Openness

Spectrum:

```text
Open Refuge ←→ Controlled Membership
```

Open refuge increases moral legitimacy, population growth, and cultural richness.

It also increases water stress, housing demand, and security pressure.

Controlled membership stabilizes resources but creates exclusion wounds.

## 6.6 Repair Doctrine

Options:

```text
Fast Functional Repair
```

Each doctrine changes:

## 6.7 Emergency Expiry

Options:


Rule:

## 6.8 Education and Repair Literacy

Who learns how infrastructure works?

Options:

```text
universal repair literacy
guild apprenticeship
expert-only maintenance
machine-mediated training

```

High repair literacy increases resilience.

Restricted repair knowledge increases efficiency in the short term but risks caste formation.

## 6.9 Dispute Resolution

Options:

```text

council mediation
Archive court
machine arbitration
guild tribunal
security command

```

Each produces different legitimacy, speed, and abuse risk.

## 6.10 Exit Rights

Options:


Rule:


# 7. City State Vector

Every city tracks a living state vector.

Core metrics:

```text
water
power
food
health
repair
trust
legitimacy
safety
stress
entropy
ecology
knowledge
logistics
signal
harmony
joy
care
memory_integrity
machine_trust
outsider_pressure
null_drift
```

These are not simple bars.

They alter the physical and social world.

Examples:

```text
Low repair causes doors to stick, machines to jam, pipes to leak, and vehicles to fail.

Low trust causes hoarding, labor refusal, rumors, theft, and militia recruitment.

Low legitimacy causes failed votes, protests, faction schisms, tax refusal, and succession crises.

Low power darkens streets, spoils medicine, slows fabrication, and weakens defense.

High stress increases panic, conflict, addiction, and authoritarian proposals.

High joy improves morale, volunteer labor, public patience, and recovery from trauma.

High memory integrity improves dispute resolution and identity restoration.

High null_drift causes procedure to survive purpose.
```

# 10. Crisis Pipeline

Cities should evolve because of pressure.

Pipeline:

```text
Material Crisis
  ↓
Emergency Posture
  ↓
NPC Interpretation
  ↓
Civic Proposal
  ↓
Faction Pressure
  ↓
Player Intervention
  ↓
Vote / Bypass / Witness / Coup / Schism / Reform
  ↓
Chronicle Event
  ↓
Archetype Drift
  ↓
Physical City Change
```

Example: Water crisis.

```text
Water storage drops below 20%.

Rationing begins.

Refugee district demands equal access.

Repair guild demands pump priority.

Security faction demands gate closure.

Archive witnesses demand lawful override.

Utility contractor offers private firmware restoration.

Players choose a path.

The city remembers.
```

The outcome is not just a stat change.

It creates:

```text
new signage
new NPC dialogue
new patrol routes
new graffiti
new laws
new trust patterns
new faction weights
new Chronicle precedent
```

# 11. Faction Drift

Cities do not have fixed ideologies.

They have archetype weights.

Example:

```text
Mutualist Assembly:        0.52
Repair Guild Republic:     0.21
Archive Order:             0.11
Security Protectorate:     0.08
Utility Compact:           0.05
Festival Commons:          0.03
```

After repeated raids:

```text
Mutualist Assembly:        0.31
Security Protectorate:     0.34
Repair Guild Republic:     0.17
Archive Order:             0.08
Utility Compact:           0.06
Festival Commons:          0.04
```

The city does not instantly rename itself.

Instead, tension appears:

```text
The Assembly is no longer sure whether it is a commons or a fortress.
```

This produces:

```text
guard posts
public arguments
militia recruitment
anti-lockdown protests
emergency expiry debates
new faction leaders
changed architecture
```

Only after repeated choices, defining events, or political victory does the city identity transform.

# 16. Failure Modes

A Symtropy city should rarely simply “lose.”

It should transform, fracture, or become morally compromised.

## Material Collapse

```text
water failure
food collapse
blackout
disease
unrepaired infrastructure
```

Outcome:

```text
evacuation
triage
refugee crisis
emergency politics
```

## Legitimacy Collapse

```text
votes ignored
records disputed
machine overrides abused
emergency powers extended
```

Outcome:

```text
protests
schism
coup
worldline fork
```

## Trust Collapse

```text
hoarding
rumors
sabotage
labor refusal
faction paranoia
```

Outcome:

```text
militias
black markets
closed districts
```

## Null Drift

```text
procedure survives purpose
machines enforce dead authority
public need rejected by old rules
```

Outcome:

```text
dead locks
hostile diagnostics
care systems imprisoning people
emergency rule that cannot end
```

## Corporate Capture

```text
survival becomes subscription
bodies become debt
water becomes service tier
repair becomes licensed
```

Outcome:

```text
reliable infrastructure with hidden coercion
```

## Commons Paralysis

```text
too many meetings
no timely authority
fear of hierarchy blocks action
```

Outcome:

```text
legitimate society fails to repair fast enough
```

## Protectorate Drift

```text
security saves lives
then refuses to return power
```

Outcome:

```text
safe city, shrinking freedom
```

## Pleasure Collapse

```text
joy becomes denial
festival hides scarcity
intimacy becomes pressure
beauty becomes access hierarchy
```

Outcome:

```text
soft coercion behind warmth
```

Do not build the full system first.

```text

```

The first playable city should include:

```text
one damaged settlement
one water crisis
one public workshop
one clinic
one charter board
one Archive Witness desk
one small refugee pressure event
one basic machine testimony event

one Chronicle record
```

## First City Arc

```text
1. Player restores local power.
2. Player repairs a pump component.
3. Water begins flowing again.
4. NPCs argue over allocation.
5. Charter board activates.
6. Player chooses temporary repair doctrine.
7. First public vote occurs.
8. Refugee group arrives at gate.
9. Machine diagnostic contradicts human testimony.
10. Chronicle records the first precedent.
```

## First Major Choice

After restoring water, players choose:

```text
Public Water Commons
Guild-Maintained Water
Emergency Ration Command
Utility Contract Restoration
Machine-Managed Allocation
Open Refuge Priority
```

Each changes:

```text
NPC behavior
district signage
available missions
faction trust
future crisis shape

```

## v0.1 Success Criteria

The prototype succeeds if players feel:

```text
This settlement is alive.
Our repairs changed its politics.
Our vote changed its future.
The city remembers what happened.
```
