# SYMTROPY_SPACECRAFT_DESIGN_BIBLE_V0_1

## Working Title

**The Ship Is a Society Under Acceleration**

## Core Thesis

A spacecraft in **Symtropy** is not merely a vehicle.

It is a moving pressure vessel, jurisdiction, life-support agreement, cargo claim, repair ecology, memory system, and moral argument traveling through vacuum.

A spacecraft always reveals:

```text
who paid for the delta-v
who maintains the air
who owns the cargo
who can stop the burn
what happens if the reactor lies
what bodies the ship was designed for
what risks were hidden in the manifest
what society is being carried inside the hull
```

Core line:

```text
A spacecraft is a civilization temporarily pretending to be a machine.
```

## Design Mantra

```text
The hull is not the boundary of the ship.
The boundary is everything the ship is responsible for not killing.
```

# 0. Relationship to Existing Symtropy Space Design

This design bible connects three major layers:

```text
Spaceports:
  where Earth argues about what may leave.

Orbital Habitats:
  where arrivals become air, rooms, water, and law.

Spacecraft:
  the fragile moving agreements between them.
```

A launch can be legitimate and still deliver a dishonest ship.

A docking can be safe and still inherit a moral debt from launch.

A spacecraft can be functional and still be socially corrupt.

Design rule:

```text
Never draw a spacecraft without asking what it owes.
```

# 1. Spacecraft Design Principles

## 1.1 Human Spacecraft Are Maintenance Cultures With Engines

Human spacecraft should not look like frictionless luxury objects.

Even advanced human ships carry maintenance memory.

They should show:

```text
patched thermal blankets
replaceable panels
external handholds
radiator politics
cargo provenance seals
crew-added markings
source-chain black boxes
pressure patch scars
tool stowage
emergency oxygen lanes
visible life-support trunks
worker stop placards
dock compatibility adapters
jury-rigged upgrades
family privacy curtains
memorial tokens
```

Human craft should feel like some mixture of:

```text
submarine
clinic
courthouse
workshop
apartment block
lifeboat
cargo ledger
confession booth
```

## 1.2 Alien Spacecraft Are Not Human Ships With Better Engines

Alien craft may not be “ships” in human terms.

They may be:

```text
habitats
grown instruments
migratory bodies
seeded ecologies
translation organs
boundary structures
archive shells
sensor swarms
ritual membranes
long-duration dormant systems
nonhuman legal spaces
```

Alien spacecraft should embody different assumptions about:

```text
body
self
time
environment
ownership
contact
consent
death
memory
interiority
arrival
```

Design rule:

```text
Alien craft should not merely answer "how do they travel?"
They should answer "what do they think travel is?"
```

# 2. Human Spacecraft Taxonomy

## 2.1 Earth Launch Vehicles

Role:

```text
move mass from atmosphere to orbit
```

Design language:

```text
staged systems
heat-shield scars
tile replacement records
range authority tags
worker inspection marks
abort towers or escape spines
fuel-side hazard color
```

Gameplay role:

```text
rarely player-owned
high ritual significance
spaceport-centered
Chronicle-heavy
```

Failure mode:

```text
technical success hides launch debt
```

## 2.2 Orbital Dock Shuttles

Role:

```text
short-range movement between stations, dock rings, and maintenance yards
```

Design language:

```text
blunt pressure capsules
large docking collars
patchy exterior
magnetic cargo racks
visible maneuvering thrusters
fold-out handrails
```

Gameplay role:

```text
social threshold vehicle
passenger legitimacy checks
cargo split decisions
```

Failure mode:

```text
people treated as cargo during manifest conflicts
```

## 2.3 Orbital Tugs

Role:

```text
move cargo, dead satellites, construction modules, and disabled craft
```

Design language:

```text
overbuilt trusses
grappling arms
tool nests
fuel tanks
radiator panels
many attachment points
scarred nose structures
```

Gameplay role:

```text
repair, salvage, dockside labor
```

Failure mode:

```text
tug authority becomes quiet control over orbital movement
```


# 4. Alien Spacecraft Taxonomy

## 4.1 Translation Vessels

Role:

```text
make contact possible between incompatible perception systems
```

May look like:

```text
moving lens field
folded interior theater
signal garden
living grammar organ
ship made of synchronized mirrors
```

Human misread:

```text
communication device
diplomatic craft
sensor artifact
```

Deeper truth:

```text
The vessel is not carrying a translator.
The vessel is the translation attempt.
```

Field Deck question:

```text
Is this docking, conversation, or mutual surgery on perception?
```

## 4.2 Biospheric Seed Arks

Role:

```text
carry ecosystems, not passengers
```

May look like:

```text
reef-shell
spore cocoon
frozen wetland core
seeded asteroid
migratory forest-body
```

Human misread:

```text
biological cargo
terraforming weapon
plague risk
```

Deeper truth:

```text
The craft may be a future biosphere deciding where it can safely begin.
```

Field Deck question:

```text
Is this cargo, child, embassy, or invasive species?
```

## 4.3 Gravitic Reef-Shells

# 5. Alien Design Principles

Alien craft should often violate one or more human assumptions.

## 5.1 Not Always Crewed

An alien vessel may have:

```text
no crew
distributed crew
species-stage crew
ancestral crew
future crew
symbiotic crew
crew that is also cargo
```

## 5.2 Not Always Separable From Habitat

Alien craft may not distinguish between:

```text
ship
home
organ
city
reef
womb
archive
body
```

## 5.3 Not Always Controlled From Inside

Control may be:

```text
ecological
distributed
ritual
developmental
consensus-based
remote
posthumous
boundary-triggered
```

## 5.4 Not Always Built From Dead Material

Alien craft may be:

```text
grown
cultivated
coaxed
bred
sung
crystallized
hosted
symbiotically maintained
```

## 5.5 Not Always Interested in Arrival

Some alien craft may be designed for:

```text
passing
listening
shadowing
hibernating
seeding
testing
remembering
mourning
boundary marking
```

Core line:

```text
Some ships do not travel to arrive.
They travel to remain in relation.
```

# 7. Field Deck Spacecraft Interface

## 7.1 Human Craft Legibility

Human craft should usually be Field Deck legible.

The Field Deck should read:

```text
pressure
air
water
thermal margin
radiation margin
crew list
passenger list
cargo manifest
black box status
source-chain status
repair history
authority claims
worker stop authority
quarantine seals
debris damage
```

Example:

```sh
$ read /dev/sym/spacecraft/human/vessel_mercy_12

CRAFT:
MERCY-12

FAMILY:
Refuge Transfer / Medical Cargo Hybrid

LIFE SUPPORT:
strained but stable

MANIFEST:
passengers verified
cargo manifest mismatch detected

WORKER STOP:
not active

SOURCE-CHAIN BLACK BOX:
intact / refusing cargo certification

SYSTEMIC NOTE:
The ship is protecting its people by accusing its cargo.
```

## 7.2 Alien Craft Legibility

Alien craft should produce structured uncertainty.

The Field Deck should avoid false certainty.

Possible readout:

```sh
$ read /dev/sym/spacecraft/unknown/contact_body_03

OBJECT CLASS:
agency-uncertain craft / possible habitat / possible organism

HULL:
not clearly separable from internal medium

PROPULSION:
no reaction mass detected
metric disturbance unconfirmed

CARGO:
not applicable / possible offspring / possible biome

CREW:
not detected / concept mismatch likely

CONSENT STATUS:
boundary response observed

RECOMMENDED:
Do not dock.
Do not cut.
Do not classify as dead.
Establish witness and non-invasive listening protocol.
```

Design rule:

```text
The Field Deck should say "I do not know" beautifully and usefully.
```

# 8. Spacecraft Chronicle Outcomes

Spacecraft matter because they carry events across jurisdictions.

## 8.1 Chronicle Event Classes

```rust
enum SpacecraftChronicleEventClass {
    ManifestFraudExposed,
    ShipBlackBoxRefusal,
    PeopleCargoSeparationPrecedent,
    WorkerStopInTransit,
    QuarantineSealBreach,
    LifeSupportCommonsRuling,
    SourceChainRecovered,
    DeathRecordRecoveredInTransit,
    AlienCraftBoundaryRecognized,
    VesselBecameWitness,
    SalvageAuthorityChallenged,
    RefugeTransferRightRecognized,
    CargoEnteredTheAir,
}
```

## 8.2 Core Outcomes

### The Ship Told the Truth

Triggered by:

```text
ship black box refuses to certify false manifest and public witness confirms
```

Effects:

```text
ship memory gains legal force
cargo houses lose trust
future docking authorities check black box earlier
```

### The People Were Not Cargo

Triggered by:

```text
passengers are admitted or protected while disputed cargo remains sealed
```

Effects:

```text
passenger legitimacy separated from cargo fraud
refuge transfer precedent strengthens
```

### The Cargo Entered the Air

Triggered by:

```text
sealed or fraudulent cargo enters a habitat loop
```

Effects:

```text
quarantine risk rises
dockworker trust drops
manifest protocols tighten later
```

### The Manifest Lied

Triggered by:

```text
cargo, passenger, or hazard record is proven false
```

Effects:

```text
launch origin may face Chronicle escalation
cargo chain is audited
corporate customs hostility rises
```

### The Dock Refused Certainty

Triggered by:

```text
alien-adjacent or agency-uncertain object is held under uncertainty rather than forced classification
```

Effects:


# 15. Acceptance Tests

The design bible is ready when:

```text
1. Human spacecraft feel like societies under maintenance pressure.
2. Alien spacecraft do not feel like upgraded human vehicles.
3. Every ship has legible life-support, authority, and memory consequences.
4. Cargo provenance affects ship design and gameplay.
5. Passenger legitimacy can be separated from cargo disputes.
6. Field Deck scans can show both human legibility and alien uncertainty.
7. A ship can become a Chronicle witness.
8. Alien craft preserve mystery without becoming vague.
9. Spacecraft aesthetics vary by culture and faction.
10. The player remembers that every ship is carrying obligations, not just mass.
```
