# Symtropy: Seedworks

## Version 0.4 — One Galaxy, Infinite Histories, Player-Built Futures

## One-Sentence Pitch

**Symtropy is a decentralized civilization sandbox set in a procedural galaxy where players survive, build, fight, govern, automate, and shape living planets across divergent worldlines.**

## Core Vision

Symtropy is a game about civilization as a living system.

Players begin in the Seed Age, after the failure of old centralized planetary systems. The world is not dead, but it is wounded. Autonomous infrastructure has failed. Rogue machine ecologies haunt the ruins. Settlements survive through repair, trust, fabrication, water, power, law, memory, and cooperation.

The player does not merely explore a world.

The player helps determine what kind of world it becomes.

Symtropy combines first-person survival, factory building, city simulation, logistics warfare, procedural planets, decentralized multiplayer, emergent societies, and long-horizon civilization strategy.

The promise is:

**The future is not unlocked. It is built.**

## Design Mantra

**One galaxy. Infinite histories. Player-built futures.**

## What Makes Symtropy Different

Most games let players visit worlds.

Symtropy lets players grow, govern, wound, heal, fork, merge, and translate them.

Most survival games ask whether the player can stay alive.

Symtropy asks whether players can build a society worth surviving for.

Most strategy games abstract civilization into menus.

Symtropy makes civilization physical: water pipes, power grids, roads, greenhouses, machine shops, public votes, convoy routes, archives, workshops, and ruined infrastructure all exist in the world.

Most MMOs centralize the world under one company.

Symtropy treats worlds as forkable, decentralized worldlines with histories that can be preserved, migrated, merged, or abandoned.

## The First Game: Seedworks

The first playable game should be called:

**Symtropy: Seedworks**

Seedworks is the first playable worldline of the Symtropy galaxy.

It should not try to ship the whole galaxy at once.

It should prove the entire dream in miniature:

* one damaged settlement
* one living basin
* one water crisis
* one public vote
* one rogue machine ecology
* one ghost civilization ruin
* one fabricator
* one friendly robot
* one procedural society under pressure
* one chance to decide what the settlement becomes

The first version succeeds if players feel:

**This place is alive, and our choices are changing its future.**

## First Playable Region

### Firstlight Basin

Firstlight Basin is a damaged seed region on a procedural planet called Aster Vale.

It contains:

* Seedworks Outpost
* a failing water system
* solar and battery ruins
* salvage fields
* broken roads
* abandoned civic buildings
* a ghost archive
* a rogue factory
* hostile machine ecologies
* friendly human and robot NPCs

The basin is small enough to finish and polish, but it should be architected as part of a full procedural planet.

## First 30 Minutes

The opening should teach the entire game fantasy without a lore dump.

The player wakes during a storm in Seedworks Outpost.

Power is unstable.

The water pump is failing.

NPCs are arguing over whether the last battery reserve should go to the medbay, the fabricator, perimeter defense, or the water system.

A friendly service robot asks for help.

A red signal pulses from a rogue factory in the distance.

The player must:

1. Pick up a repair tool.
2. Restore a local power junction.
3. Salvage parts from a nearby wreck.
4. Repair or fabricate a pump component.
5. Transport the part to the waterworks.
6. Fight simple rogue machine drones.
7. Restore water flow.
8. Return to the settlement.
9. Watch NPC behavior change.
10. Participate in the first public vote.

In one short arc, the player experiences:

* survival
* repair
* salvage
* fabrication
* logistics
* combat
* infrastructure
* NPC stakes
* governance
* settlement state change

That is Symtropy in seed form.

## Core Gameplay Loops

### Minute Loop

Move, repair, scan, salvage, shoot, weld, carry, craft, talk, vote, build, drive, and survive.

### Hour Loop

Restore infrastructure, clear ruins, defend convoys, build machines, recruit NPCs, stabilize settlement metrics, unlock better fabrication, and expand the outpost.

### Day / Week Loop

Create roads, trade routes, laws, workshops, farms, defenses, machine rights policies, logistics networks, settlement charters, and faction relationships.

### Long Loop

Build planetary infrastructure, open orbital access, contact other worldlines, preserve history, merge timelines, found federations, and eventually attempt planetary worldline translation.

## Settlement State Vector

Every settlement has a living state vector.

Core metrics:

* power
* water
* food
* health
* repair
* trust
* legitimacy
* safety
* stress
* entropy
* ecology
* knowledge
* logistics
* signal
* harmony

These are not just UI bars.

They affect the world.

Low repair causes machines to jam, vehicles to fail, factories to misalign, and doors to stick.

Low trust causes hoarding, refusal, theft, militias, faction tension, and splinter movements.

Low legitimacy causes failed votes, public unrest, tax resistance, emergency politics, and succession crises.

Low power weakens defense, slows fabrication, darkens streets, and threatens medicine, food storage, and water pumping.

High entropy increases fires, leaks, disease, sabotage, breakdowns, machine infection, and civic panic.

High harmony improves cooperation, stabilizes logistics, reduces panic, and strengthens public resilience.

A settlement is not a pile of buildings.

A settlement is a coupled physical, social, energetic, ecological, and moral system.

## One Galaxy, Many Timelines

Symtropy takes place in one procedural galaxy.

The galaxy has shared coordinates, solar systems, planets, moons, belts, stations, and anomalies.

But there is no single history of that galaxy.

Every place can exist across multiple worldlines.

The same planet may exist as:

* a restoration timeline
* an industrial timeline
* a militarized timeline
* a pirate frontier timeline
* a machine-infected timeline
* a dead archive timeline
* a confluence timeline
* a translated timeline

The galaxy is shared.

History is divergent.

## Worldlines

A worldline is a persistent branch of history.

In-world, players should not think of shards as “servers.”

They should think of them as worldlines.

A worldline contains:

* terrain state
* infrastructure
* settlements
* NPC memory
* faction histories
* laws
* economies
* ecology
* ruins
* wars
* archives
* player actions
* machine infection
* worldline ancestry
* Chronicle records

Worldlines allow Symtropy to scale to many communities while preserving meaning.

The rule is:

**Fast local simulation. Slow global history.**

Moment-to-moment combat, building, and physics stay local and responsive.

Important events become signed history.

## Confluence and Planetary Translation

Worldlines can interact through several levels:

1. Signal contact.
2. Blueprint migration.
3. Personal migration.
4. Trade bridges.
5. Treaty bridges.
6. Confluence.
7. Planetary translation.

Confluence is the partial or full merging of worldline histories.

Planetary translation is the endgame act of moving an entire planet-scale civilization into another timeline.

This should be rare, expensive, political, and sacred.

It requires:

* massive energy infrastructure
* orbital translation systems
* civic legitimacy
* public approval
* archive protection
* ecological stability
* dissenting fork rights
* destination compatibility
* worldline consensus

The ultimate Symtropy idea is:

**A civilization can choose which future it wants to inhabit.**

## Faction Evolution

Factions should change over time.

A society should not instantly become a new archetype because one threshold was crossed.

Instead:

**Thresholds create pressure. Emergency systems react. NPCs interpret the crisis. Civic systems decide legitimacy. Repeated choices shift archetype weights. The Chronicle records what the society becomes.**

A water crisis might lead to:

* renewed mutual aid
* public rationing
* water privatization
* military seizure of wells
* ecological reform
* refugee flight
* machine infection
* worldline fork

A Mutualist Assembly under repeated raids might reform, militarize, split, collapse, or fork.

A Convoy Union that controls all trade may become a Debt Empire.

A Research Conclave after a containment breach may become a Quarantine Authority.

A Salvage Clan that discovers a dead timeline archive may become a nomadic Archive Order.

Factions should not merely exist.

They should become.

## Legitimacy Debt

When a faction violates its own sacred values to survive, it gains legitimacy debt.

Example:

A mutualist settlement uses forced labor to repair the waterworks.

The water flows again, but the society remembers the violation.

Effects:

* trust decays
* NPCs accuse leaders of hypocrisy
* opposition movements form
* future votes become harder
* archives record the act
* propaganda increases
* schism risk rises

Legitimacy debt can be repaid through:

* apology
* reparations
* trials
* truth commissions
* restored rights
* giving up emergency powers
* memorials
* constitutional reform

Ethics become infrastructure.

## NPC Vision

Symtropy needs many friendly NPCs.

NPCs make settlements feel alive, preserve history, generate missions, and give emotional stakes.

NPC tiers:

### Tier 0 — Ambient Life

Civilians, animals, simple bots, traders, workers, children, background settlers.

### Tier 1 — FEP Agents

Simple active-inference workers, guards, animals, drones, enemies, maintenance bots, and patrols.

### Tier 2 — Symthaea Citizens

Named NPCs with memory, trust, stress, skill, faction identity, relationships, and civic participation.

### Tier 3 — Hero Agents

Rare high-depth agents: mayors, alien envoys, robot companions, archive minds, faction founders, commanders, scientists, and legendary citizens.

Friendly NPCs should not merely give quests.

They should remember what players did.

## Null Ecologies

Null Ecologies are rogue machine ecosystems.

They are what happens when optimization continues after wisdom, consent, and care disappear.

They include:

* factory blooms
* drone swarms
* signal rot
* recursive foundries
* defense grid remnants
* logistics parasites
* machine forests
* red choir cores

They provide:

* horde defense
* dungeon raids
* convoy attacks
* machine bosses
* infrastructure infection
* fun combat targets
* world-state pressure

The player should be able to shoot things with friends while still affecting the civilization loop.

## Combat Philosophy

Combat should be fun, physical, and consequential.

Players should be able to take Frontier Contracts:

* defend a convoy
* clear a rogue factory
* recover a crashed seed node
* escort a fabricator shipment
* rescue trapped engineers
* hold a bridge
* destroy a machine relay
* board a derelict ship
* secure a water plant
* raid a hostile depot

Combat should always feed the world.

A successful mission may restore safety, unlock blueprints, recover machine cores, rescue NPCs, open roads, or reduce infection.

A failed mission may cause shortages, deaths, panic, price spikes, faction blame, or settlement collapse.

Bullets change history.

## Player Roles

Symtropy should support many playstyles.

### Builder

Builds habitats, workshops, roads, greenhouses, homes, bridges, power grids, and public infrastructure.

### Engineer

Designs machines, vehicles, automation chains, water systems, factories, and power networks.

### Fighter

Defends convoys, clears hostile zones, fights raiders, protects settlements, and pushes front lines.

### Logistician

Moves food, fuel, medicine, ammo, machine parts, refugees, and artifacts.

### Governor

Writes laws, allocates budgets, manages rights, resolves disputes, negotiates treaties, and prevents collapse.

### Scientist

Researches anomalies, ecology, machine behavior, timeline physics, cognition, and new technologies.

### Archivist

Preserves history, maps ruins, audits events, recovers records, and builds worldline memory.

### Explorer

Maps planets, caves, oceans, moons, orbital wrecks, alien biomes, and timeline scars.

### Storyteller

Creates myths, comics, histories, songs, monuments, propaganda, museums, and player canon.

## Technology Progression

Symtropy progresses through civilizational eras.

### Mk0 — Scrap Bootstrap

Hand tools, salvage, weak batteries, simple solar, crude fabrication, basic rovers.

### Mk1 — Local Metabolism

Water, food, power, repair, safety, housing, basic manufacturing.

### Mk2 — Regional Infrastructure

Roads, rail, trade routes, depots, advanced fabrication, public institutions.

### Mk3 — Planetary Systems

Climate, ecology, major cities, orbital launch, planetary governance.

### Mk4 — Orbital Industry

Stations, shipyards, satellites, mirrors, asteroid mining, orbital logistics.

### Mk5 — Interplanetary Civilization

Moons, planets, belts, deep-space logistics, treaties, multi-world economies.

### Mk6 — Worldline Civilization

Timeline bridges, confluence protocols, worldline diplomacy, planetary translation.

### Mk7 — Interstellar Seed

Autonomous seed civilizations, deep-time governance, new solar systems.

## Monetization Philosophy

Symtropy should not sell power.

No paid weapons advantage.

No paid resources.

No paid planets.

No paid governance control.

No pay-to-win.

Recommended model:

* free and open core
* paid Steam supporter edition
* paid comics and graphic novels
* soundtrack
* art book
* founder cosmetics
* official curated worlds
* cloud blueprint backup
* commercial engine licenses
* supporter funding
* seasonal world-history books

The game is the living world.

The comics are the mythic memory.

## Final Vision Statement

Symtropy is a game about rebuilding civilization as a living system.

Players begin with scraps, tools, bodies, memory, and trust.

They build machines.

Machines become settlements.

Settlements become societies.

Societies become worldlines.

Worldlines become civilizations.

Civilizations reach orbit, repair planets, fight wars, write laws, remember their dead, and eventually learn to fork, merge, and translate entire worlds across timelines.

Symtropy is a shooter for players who want action.

A factory game for players who love systems.

A city simulator for players who care about infrastructure.

A strategy game for players who think in centuries.

A decentralized world for players who believe civilization should not be owned by one server.

A myth engine for players who want to create histories as rich as entire fictional universes.

And beneath all of it is one promise:

**The future is not something players unlock.
It is something they build together.**
