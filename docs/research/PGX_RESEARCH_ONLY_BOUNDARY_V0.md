# Pharmacogenomics Research-Only Boundary — v0

**Status:** research-only engineering guardrail. Not prescribing software, a clinical device, or a qualified genotype-to-phenotype implementation.

## Why this slice exists

The population-frequency model in `pgx_health_equity.rs` collapses allele functions into broad bins and applies Hardy–Weinberg approximations. The existing [HWE validation note](../../papers/applications/pgx-health-equity/HWE_VALIDATION.md) records material disagreement with published phenotype estimates, missing alleles and copy-number variants, and loss of diplotype-level information. That is useful research evidence; it is not a basis for patient-specific dosing.

The previous fallback for a drug/phenotype without a matching guideline returned `StandardDose`. Absence of support must not be represented as affirmative evidence for a standard dose. This slice changes that fallback to `Indeterminate` with no dose multiplier or alternative-drug field, and adds a regression test.

## Claim and use boundary

- Population allele frequencies and Hardy–Weinberg-derived distributions are exploratory aggregates, not an individual's genotype, diplotype, phenotype, risk, or dose.
- A person's self-reported or inferred ancestry must never be used as a proxy for their pharmacogenomic result or as a patient-level dosing rule.
- A `DosingRecommendation` value is a structured guideline-action representation for research fixtures. Its presence does not establish subject binding, guideline currency, clinical applicability, or qualification.
- Unsupported drug/phenotype combinations return `DosingAction::Indeterminate`; they must not silently become `StandardDose`.
- Known limitations and negative findings must remain visible. Do not alter assumptions, acceptance thresholds, or fixtures merely to make a result pass.
- This is a narrow guardrail. It does not implement full star-allele translation, phase/diplotype inference, copy-number/structural-variant handling, external validation, or a clinically authorized API. Clinical use remains prohibited.

## Qualification ladder

### Q0 — Software failure semantics

Required now:

1. The unknown-guideline fallback returns `Indeterminate`.
2. The fallback has no numeric dose adjustment and no alternative medication.
3. The output states that no applicable guideline was found.
4. Regression tests cover unsupported drug/phenotype combinations.
5. A `PASS` refers only to the exact tested source revision and software behavior; it is not evidence of clinical accuracy.

### Q1 — Reference-corpus correctness

Before claims about phenotype interpretation:

- Pin each input and expected result to a specific, traceable source edition and exact digest.
- Include positive cases, negative controls, unsupported variants, missing genotype, unresolved star-allele translation, ambiguous phase, uncertain or missing copy number, stale guidelines, and unsupported gene/drug pairs.
- Separate gene-specific interpretation rules and versions. Never generalize qualification from one gene/drug pair to another.
- Compare outputs against independent expert annotations, not values generated by the model under test.

### Q2 — Diplotype and phenotype validation

For a defined gene/drug/context, report exact-match agreement and a confusion matrix for phenotype assignment, plus sensitivity, specificity where applicable, and confidence intervals. Stratify by gene, phenotype, source cohort, and population actually represented in the data. Report indeterminate rates and errors caused by uncovered variants; do not count abstentions as correct predictions.

For CYP2D6, qualification must explicitly address allele translation, gene deletion, hybrid/structural alleles and copy-number variation. For other genes, define the corresponding clinically relevant coverage rather than reusing a generic completeness claim.

### Q3 — External validation and applicability

Use independent cohorts or reference sets that were not used to build the model. Evaluate calibration only where the output is a meaningful probability and the study design supports it. Document participant selection, reference-standard quality, missingness, data leakage prevention, applicability, and uncertainty. Apply TRIPOD+AI and PROBAST+AI as relevant reporting/appraisal resources; use STARD-AI when evaluating diagnostic accuracy.

### Q4 — Clinical use

Not in scope for this PR. Requires an explicitly defined context of use, appropriate expert and regulatory review, prospective evaluation where indicated, an operational monitoring/rollback plan, and evidence that expected benefits outweigh harms. Passing Q0–Q3 is not automatic authorization for Q4.

## Comparison with established practice

The baseline should be current, versioned CPIC recommendations and established genotype-interpretation tools such as PharmCAT, using supported gene/drug pairs and the same reference inputs where possible. PharmVar's allele definitions and primary source literature should be tracked explicitly. This work does not claim to outperform these systems; it first makes unsupported outputs fail visibly and establishes the evaluation conditions under which comparisons would be meaningful.

The intended research advantage is a stricter, inspectable boundary between aggregate equity analysis and individual interpretation, with source/version provenance and reproducible negative controls. Superiority is a hypothesis until independent benchmark results support it.

## Cross-project interfaces

- **Symthaea:** source-linked knowledge, versioned allele/guideline provenance, exact fixture identities, and explicit indeterminate states.
- **Mycelix:** consented and purpose-limited access to any health data used for future research; access-control correctness is not clinical validation.
- **Symtropy:** physiological or pharmacokinetic simulation may test stated mathematical assumptions, but simulation alone cannot qualify genotype interpretation or clinical outcomes.
- **Sol-Atlas:** population-level access and service coverage analysis can expose where pharmacogenetic testing/counseling is unavailable; geographic access results must not infer individual biology.
