## 1. Decision question

Which narrow pump/valve/industrial-spares product or service family has a real, source-backed need; a realizable local/partner route; acceptable technical and quality boundaries; and a credible lifecycle-economic advantage over conventional suppliers or repair services?

This panel separates:

1. Public procurement policy and technical scope.
2. Official macroeconomic context.
3. Product-level import indicators.
4. Actual public tenders/RFQs.
5. Procurement plans that may become future tenders.
6. Supplier capability declarations and independent qualification.
7. Customer/maintenance demand evidence.
8. Physical process/inspection qualification.

None of these categories is interchangeable with another.

## 2. Initial strongest source-backed observations

### 2.1 dtic designated pumps and MV motors

The dtic's official [Information on Pumps, Medium Voltage (MV) Motors and Associated Accessories Designation](https://www.thedtic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Information-on-Pumps-and-MV-Motors.pdf) provides a product-specific definition and table of designated pump families.

The retrieved document lists 70% minimum local content per unit for specific pump classes, including:
- Single-stage end-suction centrifugal pumps (DN25–DN300, 1–16 bar).
- Solid-handling end-suction centrifugal pumps (DN40–DN400, 0.5–16 bar).
- Multistage centrifugal pumps (DN32–DN350, 10–63 bar).
- Horizontal split-case pumps (DN80–DN300, 1–18 bar).
- Vertical turbine pumps (DN100–DN500, 0.3–40 bar).
- Positive-displacement and diaphragm pumps.
- Self-priming centrifugal pumps.
- Vertical cantilever and single-stage slurry pumps.
- Liquid-ring vacuum pumps.
- Listed centrifugal process and chemical pumps.

**Important scope rules in the same document:**
- Designation applies when pumps are procured, repaired, overhauled or maintained individually, as well as when procured as a system.
- The minimum applies to each designated individual pump; local content cannot be averaged across multiple pumps or imported/local combinations.
- The pump's 70% content must be supported by local/certified castings, forgings and/or fabrication plus verifiable manufacturing activity including machining, drilling, wear coating/lining, painting, assembly and testing, with full material traceability.
- The document lists upfront exemptions for certain inputs: ball and roller bearings, rubber pump lining, mechanical seals, and steel billets/bars used in shaft fabrication.
- It says the thresholds apply to new purchases, refurbishments, replacements and general overhauls.
- Product quality, safety, manufacturing process standards and accreditation may be required by the procuring entity for the actual application.

This is materially more specific than saying "pumps are 70% local content". Each candidate must be mapped to the exact pump class, size, pressure range, tender/procurement instrument, applicable edition, calculation method and required declarations. Do not assume that a pump component or any unrelated pump model is covered by the same category.

The dtic [Industrial Procurement](https://www.thedtic.gov.za/sectors-and-services-2/industrial-development/industrial-procurement/) page links the current sector circulars and refers to SANS 1286:2017 and the Annexure C/D/E local/imported content declaration schedules. A calculation must use the applicable current tender/circular and local-content guidance; this note is not legal/procurement advice.

For valves and actuators, the dtic [valve designation note](https://www.thedtic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Information-on-Valves-Products-and-Actuators-Designation.pdf) lists 70% for specified product types/ranges. Valve applicability needs its own scope mapping; do not borrow pump classifications.

## 3. Initial pilot hypothesis — compare service and manufacture separately

The evidence points to a staged hypothesis, not a selected commercial product:

### Candidate lane A — benign industrial spares and fixtures
Potential examples: brackets, housings, shafts, couplings, fixtures and other non-safety-critical parts with an exact drawing, material grade, tolerance and inspection plan.

### Candidate lane B — repair/refurbishment and requalification
Potential examples: returned pump subassemblies and selected industrial pump parts where OEM, legal and technical requirements permit independent work. Preserve exact as-received condition, identity, repair scope, replacement-part origin, measurements, tests and return-to-service decision.

### Candidate lane C — complete designated pump manufacture
A longer-term candidate only after product class/pressure/size, local-content scope, design/process qualification, manufacturing capability, testing, warranty/service and market evidence are all independently established.

Do not begin the first physical demonstrator with pressure-boundary or safety-critical products, fire pumps, high-voltage systems, chemical processing or specialist OEM-controlled equipment without appropriate technical, legal, safety and authorization pathways.

## 4. Required evidence matrix per product family

| Evidence dimension | Minimum fields |
|---|---|
| Product identity | Exact product family, OEM/model where relevant, part/serial, drawing and revision |
| Duty/application | Fluid, flow/head/pressure, temperature, environment, operating envelope and safety significance |
| Demand | Customer, tender/RFQ/maintenance work order, quantity/frequency, location, response time, evidence date |
| Local-content scope | Exact designated category/circular, effective date, calculation method, material exemptions and required schedules |
| Material | Grade/specification, lot, origin, supplier, certificates, test/assay, traceability and substitution rules |
| Supplier capability | Manufacturer/repairer/assembler/distributor/importer, exact process, equipment, scope evidence and date |
| Process route | Machining, joining, casting/forging, coating, lining, heat treatment or partner operation; current qualification |
| Inspection/test | Characteristic, instrument, calibration, measurement uncertainty, acceptance criteria and independent authority |
| Service/lifecycle | Repairability, replacement parts, warranty, overhaul interval, failure history, end-of-life and recovery |
| Economics | Dated quotations, labour, consumables, tooling, energy, inspection, logistics, scrap/rework, warranty and capital/lease cost |

A supplier's address is not proof of local manufacturing. A distributor listing is not a process qualification. A tender notice is not an award. A repair completion is not independent acceptance. A simulated cost is not a supplier quote.

## 5. Comparison design

For each exact candidate article, compare the same revision, material/quality requirements, lot size, functional test, inspection, geography and due date across:
1. OEM-authorized repair or replacement.
2. Qualified local repair specialist.
3. Conventional job-shop subcontracting.
4. Dedicated product-family line where volumes justify it.
5. Shared microfactory cell with qualified process partners.
6. Hybrid local machining/assembly plus specialist partner processes.

Report independently:
- quote and realized lead time;
- time to first conforming/accepted output;
- first-pass yield, scrap and rework;
- functional/dimensional acceptance and uncertainty;
- direct and fully burdened cost per accepted output;
- imported/secondary/virgin material and actual source;
- local-content disposition only where exact procurement rule applies;
- repair turnaround, repeat-failure and warranty burden where data exist;
- supplier concentration, single-point-of-failure and recovery;
- energy/water/material use per accepted output where measured.

A candidate must not win on raw throughput if it fails quality, OEM authorization, legal/local-content or evidence gates.
