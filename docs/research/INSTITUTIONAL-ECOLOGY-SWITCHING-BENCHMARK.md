# Institutional Ecology, Switching, and Lock-In Benchmark

**Status:** research design v0  
**Related:** #7046, #7051, #7047

## Purpose

Extend institutional evolution from a single lineage to a population of concurrently available institutions that can compete, coexist, interoperate, merge, split, gain members, lose members, and become path-dependent.

Research on institutional path dependence shows that prior institutions can alter later behavior and institutional choice. Research on competing institutional arrangements identifies early-adopter, network, and switching effects that can make one arrangement spread or become difficult to displace. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0176268015000841  https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268111000588

## Core distinction

~~~text
institutional emergence
!=
institutional ecology
!=
institutional dominance
~~~

A large installed institution is not automatically a better institution.

## State

At each time step, record:

- available institutions;
- active institutions;
- membership by institution;
- overlapping memberships;
- entry and exit rates;
- switching cost;
- interoperability cost;
- network effects;
- institutional capacity;
- adoption queue;
- authority concentration;
- institutional lineage;
- lock-in indicators;
- minority-institution persistence.

## Migration

A migration event must declare:

- source institution;
- target institution;
- member identity or population class;
- migration authority, where required;
- migration cost;
- interoperability requirements;
- implementation result;
- observation window.

Migration is not implied by institutional performance.

## Switching-cost fixture

Create two functionally equivalent institutions.

Hold all institutional performance measures constant.

Vary only switching cost.

Expected qualification property:

~~~text
same institutional performance
+ different switching cost
-> distinguishable migration dynamics
~~~

## Network-effect fixture

Create compatible and incompatible institutions with identical intrinsic performance.

Vary only network-effect strength.

Measure:

- adoption speed;
- concentration;
- minority survival;
- path dependence;
- recovery after incumbent failure.

## Early-adopter fixture

Give one institution a temporary early-membership advantage.

Later equalize intrinsic performance.

Measure whether early advantage persists after the original advantage is removed.

This separates:

~~~text
historical lead
vs
current performance
~~~

## Coexistence

The oracle must permit persistent coexistence.

Do not require convergence to one institution.

Institutional diversity can itself be an outcome of interest, especially where different institutions are suited to different contexts or provide redundancy.

Empirical work on polycentric governance notes that institutional diversity, multiple decision centers, cross-scale linkages, accountability, and conflict resolution can matter for adaptive capacity, while also emphasizing that polycentricity is not a universal panacea. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/psj.12518

## Failure and removal

After an institution becomes dominant:

1. remove it permanently;
2. suspend it temporarily;
3. degrade its performance;
4. remove its interoperability;
5. restore it after a gap.

Measure whether agents:

- migrate smoothly;
- become trapped;
- reconstruct the institution;
- fragment;
- create an interoperability bridge;
- suffer persistent coordination loss.

## Merger and fission

Allow explicit treatments for:

- two institutions merging into one;
- one institution splitting into local variants;
- one local institution becoming a higher-level shared arrangement.

Each transition requires new identity and lineage.

## Institutional quality vs market share

Do not define institutional quality as membership share.

Membership share can increase because of:

- better performance;
- switching cost;
- network effects;
- entry barriers;
- coercive authority;
- interoperability advantage;
- incumbency;
- early adoption;
- exit restrictions.

These mechanisms must remain separately observable.

## Counterfactual ecology

After an institution is adopted, compare:

- institution retained;
- institution removed;
- institution replaced by declared alternative;
- institution made interoperable with the alternative.

Hold problem schedule and physical conditions constant where possible.

Downstream divergence is interpreted through declared causal paths, not through market share alone.

## Qualification boundary

A PASS establishes only bounded migration, coexistence, network, switching, and institutional-lineage behavior under the frozen ecological profile.

It does not establish that competition, diversity, monopoly, or any institutional equilibrium is socially desirable.
