# SPECTRA historical source ledger — Puharich and the Faraday-cage claim

**Status:** initial source inventory; not a complete archive search.

**Purpose:** distinguish primary historical records, contemporaneous summaries, later recollections and unverified claims before any experiment is described as a replication.

## Source grading

Use these classes:

- **P1 — Primary record:** original patent, filed proposal, laboratory notebook, protocol, raw trial record, contemporaneous instrument record or correspondence.
- **P2 — Contemporaneous secondary account:** a contemporary report describing another researcher's work without supplying the underlying raw record.
- **S1 — Later secondary account:** biography, memoir, retrospective review or later summary.
- **U — Unverified claim:** a statement for which the underlying record has not been located or independently authenticated.

A primary record for a proposed study is not a primary record of an experiment being completed. Patent publication is primary evidence that a specification was filed/granted, not proof that all asserted effects were experimentally demonstrated.

## PH-001 — 1972 SRI / Public Health Service research proposal

**Document:** Public Health Service grant application, "Techniques To Enhance Extraordinary Human Perception," principal investigators Russell Targ and Harold E. Puthoff, Stanford Research Institute; project period stated as October 1972–October 1973.

**Archive copy:** CIA Reading Room release identifier CIA-RDP96-00787R000100150001-6 (hosted copy: https://documents2.theblackvault.com/documents/cia/stargate/STARGATE%20%231%20173/Part0001/CIA-RDP96-00787R000100150001-6.pdf)

**Classification:** P1 for the existence and content of the SRI proposal; P2 for its summary of Puharich's earlier work.

**Relevant pages:** printed pages 25–27 (PDF pages 25–27 in the hosted scan; verify page offsets against the document image).

### What the proposal records

- The proposal discusses the effect of electromagnetic shielding on ESP.
- It attributes an unusual Faraday-cage result to "privately-circulated material" from neurologist Henry K. Puharich.
- It describes two subjects in separate Faraday cages, and a variant with one subject in a solid-copper-sheet cage and another in a car about one mile from the laboratory.
- It reports a matching-test result with a claimed chance probability of 2 × 10^-19.
- It reports that breaking the shielding integrity or disconnecting the cage ground was said to reduce scores toward ordinary-room levels.
- It says the SRI proposal authors wanted to determine which physical properties of the cage were responsible and proposed to assess electromagnetic shielding and acoustic properties while running ESP tasks.
- It describes a self-powered target/feedback machine intended to avoid a power-line penetration, and planned to vary cage parameters without the subject's knowledge.

### What it does not establish

- It does not supply Puharich's raw trial data, original protocol, scoring sheets, randomization records, full target set, subject identities or contemporaneous apparatus drawings.
- It does not allow independent reconstruction of the reported 2 × 10^-19 probability.
- It does not show that the reported one-mile test was independently replicated.
- It is a proposal and a contemporaneous account of a private report; it does not, by itself, establish that all proposed SRI work was funded, executed or successful.
- It does not establish a novel electromagnetic interaction or any other new physical field.

**Research treatment:** cite the one-mile result only as a historical claim repeated in a 1972 SRI proposal, not as a verified Puharich result. Do not call a new study a direct replication until the original Puharich protocol and raw record are recovered.

## PH-002 — U.S. Patent 2,995,633, "Means for aiding hearing"

**Record:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US2995633A/en

**Classification:** P1 patent publication.

- Inventors listed: Henry K. Puharich and Joseph L. Lawrence.
- Filing / priority date listed: 25 September 1958.
- Publication / grant date listed: 8 August 1961.
- Subject: a hearing-assistance arrangement using sound-related signals coupled through dental/facial structures and the facial nervous system.

**Claim ceiling:** the patent is primary evidence of the filed/granted technical description and claims. It is not, on its own, proof of clinical effectiveness, regulatory approval, broad adoption or every physiological explanation stated in the specification.

## What we still need to recover

For the reported Puharich cage experiments, search for:

1. Original proposal and protocol, including the experiment number and exact task.
2. Full target sequence, randomization method, timing and feedback policy.
3. Trial-level responses and the exact matching/scoring algorithm.
4. Subject/participant identities under appropriate privacy rules, operator and scorer roles.
5. All runs, failed runs, exclusions, stopping decisions and deviations.
6. Cage drawings, dimensions, mesh/sheet construction, seams, door, support, grounding and electrical state.
7. Calibrated physical characterization: E-field, B-field, RF, low-frequency fields, acoustic, optical, thermal, vibration, electrical and timing channels.
8. Contemporaneous correspondence, funding reports, invoices, engineering notes, photographs and custody records.
9. Independent attempts to repeat the specific cage-state manipulation, including null and failed replications.

The historical search should include archival collections, patent records, SRI/PHS/NIMH grant files, Puharich's papers and estate/family-held records where lawful and accessible. Every item must retain its exact repository, catalogue identifier, acquisition date, file digest, page/image references and transcription uncertainty.

## Direct consequence for SPECTRA

The 1972 SRI proposal itself suggests a useful modern experimental design: characterize the enclosure's physical and acoustic properties first; then vary defined enclosure parameters under blinded conditions. But the new study must use calibrated measurements, independently held target custody, a locked scoring protocol, complete trial accounting and an independent replication.

The initial historical claim is a source for hypothesis generation, not an accepted fact.
