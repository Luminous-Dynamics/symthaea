# SPECTRA historical source ledger — Puharich and the Faraday-cage claim

**Status:** initial source inventory; not a complete archive search.

**Purpose:** distinguish primary historical records, contemporaneous summaries, later recollections and unverified claims before any experiment is described as a replication.

## Source grading

Use these classes:

- **P1 — Primary record:** original patent, filed proposal, laboratory notebook, protocol, raw trial record, contemporaneous instrument record or correspondence.
- **P2 — Contemporaneous secondary account:** a contemporary report describing another researcher's work without supplying the underlying raw record.
- **S1 — Later secondary account:** biography, memoir, retrospective review or later summary.
- **U — Unverified claim:** a statement for which the underlying record has not been located or independently authenticated.

A primary record for a proposed study is not a primary record of an experiment being completed. Patent publication is primary evidence that a specification was filed/granted, not proof that all asserted effects were experimentally demonstrated.

## PH-001 — 1972 SRI / Public Health Service research proposal

**Document:** Public Health Service grant application, "Techniques To Enhance Extraordinary Human Perception," principal investigators Russell Targ and Harold E. Puthoff, Stanford Research Institute; project period stated as October 1972–October 1973.

**Archive copy:** CIA Reading Room release identifier CIA-RDP96-00787R000100150001-6 (hosted copy: https://documents2.theblackvault.com/documents/cia/stargate/STARGATE%20%231%20173/Part0001/CIA-RDP96-00787R000100150001-6.pdf)

**Classification:** P1 for the existence and content of the SRI proposal; P2 for its summary of Puharich's earlier work.

**Relevant pages:** printed pages 25–27 (PDF pages 25–27 in the hosted scan; verify page offsets against the document image).

### What the proposal records

- The proposal discusses the effect of electromagnetic shielding on ESP.
- It attributes an unusual Faraday-cage result to "privately-circulated material" from neurologist Henry K. Puharich.
- It describes two subjects in separate Faraday cages, and a variant with one subject in a solid-copper-sheet cage and another in a car about one mile from the laboratory.
- It reports a matching-test result with a claimed chance probability of 2 × 10^-19.
- It reports that breaking the shielding integrity or disconnecting the cage ground was said to reduce scores toward ordinary-room levels.
- It says the SRI proposal authors wanted to determine which physical properties of the cage were responsible and proposed to assess electromagnetic shielding and acoustic properties while running ESP tasks.
- It describes a self-powered target/feedback machine intended to avoid a power-line penetration, and planned to vary cage parameters without the subject's knowledge.

### What it does not establish

- It does not supply Puharich's raw trial data, original protocol, scoring sheets, randomization records, full target set, subject identities or contemporaneous apparatus drawings.
- It does not allow independent reconstruction of the reported 2 × 10^-19 probability.
- It does not show that the reported one-mile test was independently replicated.
- It is a proposal and a contemporaneous account of a private report; it does not, by itself, establish that all proposed SRI work was funded, executed or successful.
- It does not establish a novel electromagnetic interaction or any other new physical field.

**Research treatment:** cite the one-mile result only as a historical claim repeated in a 1972 SRI proposal, not as a verified Puharich result. Do not call a new study a direct replication until the original Puharich protocol and raw record are recovered.

## PH-011 — *Tomorrow* magazine, Summer 1960: "TV Gilds a Lily"

**Document:** *Tomorrow*, Vol. 8, No. 3, Summer 1960, Susy Smith, "TV Gilds a Lily," printed pp. 50–53.

**Scan:** https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/tomorrow_garrett/tomorrow_2s_v8_n3_summer_1960.pdf

**Classification:** P2 contemporaneous secondary account. This is a publication from 1960, but the article is not Puharich's lab notebook or raw experimental record.

### What it reports

- The article discusses television dramatizations of Peter Hurkos and Puharich's testing at the Round Table Foundation in Glen Cove, Maine.
- It describes a Faraday-cage arrangement with a wire-mesh outer enclosure and an inner wooden compartment in which the subject sat alone.
- It reports that the outer enclosure was energized/charged using a Van de Graaff generator. The article's own wording is "25,000 volts of electrical current" and it says the generator could produce 400,000 V; the phrase "volts of electrical current" is dimensionally imprecise, and no calibrated voltage/current waveform, source impedance, field strength or exposure record is given.
- It says Puharich asked Hurkos to identify objects on a desk outside the cage and attributes claimed accuracy of 80%, sometimes up to 95%, to Puharich.
- The author explicitly criticizes the television dramatization and questions whether the program's version of events is factual, fictional or mixed. The article is therefore especially valuable as a contemporary account that warns against treating a TV reenactment as an experimental record.

### What it does not establish

- No raw trial series, target list, randomization, response sheets, scoring method, independent scorer records, instrument calibration, field measurements or full safety documentation are supplied. The article does not establish how objects were selected, whether the receiver was blind to all target metadata, how many independent trials were run, or how the percentages were calculated.
- The percentages are claims attributed to Puharich, not an independently reconstructed result.
- The article's account concerns Hurkos and object identification, not the same described protocol as Garrett's Zener-card sender–receiver task or the memoir's randomized cage-charge discrimination task.
- Do not reproduce the historical high-voltage arrangement. It is not a modern experimental recipe and is outside SPECTRA's initial safe scope.

**Research treatment:** use this as a separate historical profile lead, provisionally PH-HURKOS-OBJECT-001 (caged receiver identifies objects located outside), pending primary-source recovery. Do not pool it with Zener-card transfer or cage-state discrimination.

## PH-015 — Puharich v. Brenner, 415 F.2d 979 (D.C. Cir. 1969)

**Document:** United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, argued 21 March 1969, decided 25 June 1969, No. 22286.

**Primary opinion:** https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F2/415/415.F2d.979.22286.html
**Parallel copy:** https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/415/979/280149/

**Classification:** P1 for the judicial opinion and the evidence summarized in the litigation record. It is not the underlying trial transcript, laboratory notebook or raw data.

### Device and application

Puharich appealed the rejection of patent application Serial No. 291,096 (filed in 1963) for a device claimed to expand ESP and assist its study. The appellate opinion describes a cage-like booth with electrical installations in its screen casing for generating currents and impulses.

### Experimental record summarized by the court

The opinion says Puharich's trial presentation was largely confined to testimony and statistical data from tests on nine "sensitives" asked to identify the colors of face-down playing cards. It notes that in some instances subjects were allowed to pass a hand over the card.

The experiment was reported in three run-throughs:
1. subjects outside the cage;
2. subjects in the cage without voltage ("untreated");
3. subjects in the cage with voltage applied.

The opinion's footnote gives one subject, Mr. Marion, as an example: in a series of 520 trials he scored 390 correct outside the cage, 413 in the uncharged cage and 455 in the electrically charged cage. These correspond to raw proportions of 75.0%, about 79.4% and 87.5%, respectively. The opinion does not provide enough detail to reconstruct the full chance model, target balance, card-generation procedure, trial independence, randomization or exact analysis. Do not calculate a modern p-value from those counts without the original protocol and trial-level records.

### Why this source changes our research design

The court explicitly observes that performance improved substantially simply by entering the *uncharged* cage. It says this is consistent with environmental novelty and the experimenter's demonstrated interest accounting for a substantial part of the improvement. It also notes:
- tests were viewed solely by Puharich and his consultants;
- the programme was limited in extent;
- the applicant could not explain why the uncharged cage improved performance if electrical charge was the claimed crucial contribution;
- the results were too inconclusive to establish that the claimed results would recur in another experiment.

The court said duplication or a convincing theoretical explanation could have supported operability. It did not decide whether ESP exists: for purposes of the patent appeal it assumed, without deciding, that some people might have ESP, and held that the applicant had not shown that the device enhanced it reliably.

### Task identity

This is a distinct **face-down playing-card colour-identification task across outside / uncharged-cage / electrically charged-cage conditions**. Do not automatically identify it with:
- the earlier Peter Hurkos hidden-object identification account in *Tomorrow* (1960);
- the Garrett sender–receiver Zener-card task described in the later memoir;
- the separate randomized cage-charge guessing task described in that memoir;
- the one-mile matching-test claim repeated in the 1972 SRI proposal.

The card-colour test may be related to the 1961/1968 Faraday-cage work, but that relationship must be demonstrated from primary records rather than assumed.

### Modern design implications

A modern reconstruction must independently separate:
- ordinary practice/familiarity effects;
- participant response bias and expectancy;
- experimenter expectancy and scoring bias;
- enclosure novelty and acoustic/thermal/light changes;
- target-pool/card-order imbalance and hidden cues;
- electrical-state effects from the effects of simply being inside the enclosure.

Use randomized/counterbalanced conditions, sealed target/response records, blinded scoring, all-trial accounting and a preregistered primary endpoint. Do not reproduce the historical high-voltage apparatus. Any human-facing physical condition requires a separate electrical-safety and ethics review; initial work should be passive, low-power and instrument-characterization only.

## PH-009 — Project I details in *Memories of a Maverick* (later memoir)

**Record:** *Memories of a Maverick*, page 52 in the located scan: https://pub.deadnet.se/Books_and_manuals_on_various_stuff/Metaphysics%20%26%20Spirituality/MEMORIES%20OF%20A%20MAVERICK.pdf

**Classification:** S1 later memoir; useful for hypothesis reconstruction only.

The memoir describes a 7 × 7 × 7 ft copper-screen cage on insulating supports, with Eileen Garrett as receiver and a sender in another room using Zener cards. It says the initial cage-door-open/floating control and the door-closed condition both produced chance-expectation results. It then describes a separate cage-state discrimination procedure: a random switch applied an electrical charge to the outer cage, and Garrett was asked to guess when the charge appeared. The memoir claims 91 charge appearances over 22 experiments and 89 correct guesses. It says Puharich considered ionization of air a possible cue and contemplated a gas-tight solid copper inner cage.

**Critical qualifications:**
- These are retrospective memoir claims, not trial-level records.
- "91 times in 22 experiments" does not specify the independent randomized unit, trial sequence, missing trials, timing, stimulus generation, feedback or exact statistical analysis.
- The paired Zener-card task and the randomized cage-state task are different protocols and must not be combined.
- The memoir's proposed gas-tight inner cage is a design intention in the account; it is not evidence that a completed, characterized gas-tight apparatus was tested.

The memoir supports two separately labelled modern reconstruction profiles:
1. paired sender–receiver symbol transfer with the receiver in an enclosure;
2. blinded cage-state discrimination under a safe, independently verified apparatus state.

It does not establish a direct replication protocol.

## What we still need to recover

For the reported Puharich cage experiments, search for:

1. Original proposal and protocol, including the experiment number and exact task.
2. Full target sequence, randomization method, timing and feedback policy.
3. Trial-level responses and the exact matching/scoring algorithm.
4. Subject/participant identities under appropriate privacy rules, operator and scorer roles.
5. All runs, failed runs, exclusions, stopping decisions and deviations.
6. Cage drawings, dimensions, mesh/sheet construction, seams, door, support, grounding and electrical state.
7. Calibrated physical characterization: E-field, B-field, RF, low-frequency fields, acoustic, optical, thermal, vibration, electrical and timing channels.
8. Contemporaneous correspondence, funding reports, invoices, engineering notes, photographs and custody records.
9. Independent attempts to repeat the specific cage-state manipulation, including null and failed replications.

The historical search should include archival collections, patent records, SRI/PHS/NIMH grant files, Puharich's papers and estate/family-held records where lawful and accessible. Every item must retain its exact repository, catalogue identifier, acquisition date, file digest, page/image references and transcription uncertainty.

## Direct consequence for SPECTRA

The 1972 SRI proposal itself suggests a useful modern experimental design: characterize the enclosure's physical and acoustic properties first; then vary defined enclosure parameters under blinded conditions. But the new study must use calibrated measurements, independently held target custody, a locked scoring protocol, complete trial accounting and an independent replication.

The initial historical claim is a source for hypothesis generation, not an accepted fact.
