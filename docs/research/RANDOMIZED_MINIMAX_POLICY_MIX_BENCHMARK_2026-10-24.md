# Randomized minimax policy-tree mixtures — 2026-10-24

## Research question

The exact fixed-prior minimax solver has a Pareto frontier of deterministic adaptive intervention trees. A policy need not necessarily commit to one tree: it can draw one tree at the start of an episode and follow it. Since expected terminal cross-entropy is linear in that mixture, the best randomized policy over the finite deterministic frontier is a small linear program.

This asks whether **convexifying the policy frontier** produces a material robust-decision improvement—not whether randomized control or linear programming is new.

## Protocol

For each objective (graph, intervention-kernel assignment, and joint hypothesis), each of six learner priors, and horizons 1–2, the exact fixed-prior solver enumerates the nondominated risk vectors over six candidate true priors. Each vector contains the exact expected terminal categorical cross-entropy under all six worlds. A linear program solves

`minimize t` subject to `Rᵀw ≤ t·1`, `sum(w)=1`, `w≥0`.

The mixture selects a complete observation-contingent policy tree once per episode. The unknown true-prior identity is fixed throughout the episode and is not passed to the policy. The solver is exact over the Pareto frontier generated by the small finite model, not over prior candidates or graph/mechanism families omitted from that model.

The benchmark has **36 rows** (six learner priors × three objectives × two horizons); each row optimizes and scores against six candidate true priors. The LP returns primal policy-mixture weights and dual weights over candidate worlds. The reported minimax upper/lower certificate gap is independently checked numerically.

## Results

### Randomization gains at horizon two

| Objective | Mean improvement in worst-case CE | Maximum improvement | Learner priors with nonzero improvement | Maximum mixture support |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Graph | 0 bits | 0 bits | 0 / 6 | 1 tree |
| Kernel | 0.000192 bits | 0.000384 bits | 3 / 6 | 3 trees |
| Joint | 0 bits | 0 bits | 0 / 6 | 1 tree |

The overall mean improvement across the three objectives at horizon two is **0.000064 bits**. All horizon-one mixtures collapse to a deterministic optimal policy, with no measured gain.

For the kernel objective, the largest observed gain is for the uniform, chain-biased, and fork-biased learner priors. The deterministic worst-case cross-entropy is 2.581410 bits; the three-tree randomized mixture reduces it to 2.581025 bits, an improvement of **0.000384 bits**. The mixture weights are approximately 0.588877, 0.377643 and 0.033479 over three deterministic trees. Other learner priors obtain no gain.

Thus randomization is mathematically valid and sometimes improves the minimax point, but the practical gain is **negligible in this model**. It is not worth adding substantial runtime complexity on this evidence alone.

### Exact LP certificate

Across all 36 rows, the maximum primal/dual worst-case-loss gap is `8.88e-16` bits, consistent with floating-point solver precision. The dual scenario weights sum to one. This certifies the LP solution over the generated risk vectors; it does not certify that the candidate prior set is complete, calibrated, or representative of deployment.

### Computation

The full six-prior/two-horizon frontier enumeration used at most 1,542 memoized states and at most 450 nondominated vectors in one objective/learner configuration. Across the 36 cases, measured frontier generation summed to about 1.30 seconds and LP calls to about 0.062 seconds in this local Python 3.13.5 environment. These timings are hardware/environment-specific and are not an industry speed comparison.

## Interpretation and next step

This is a useful negative result: convexification adds flexibility, but the deterministic minimax frontier already contains a good robust policy for graph and joint scoring, while kernel score gains are tiny. Avoid claiming a breakthrough or SOTA result.

The stronger next research direction is to improve **frontier generation**, not merely randomize over the frontier: compare against exact Pareto-front methods for multi-objective MDPs, derive a valid approximation bound for epsilon pruning, and scale the number of candidate priors/horizon. Randomized mixtures should remain a low-cost optional post-processing step if a target regime later demonstrates material gains.

## External comparison and prior art

Randomized policy mixtures, LP minimax optimization, and Pareto-frontier methods are established tools. ICLR 2025 work by Li, Ju, and Shroff, *How to Find the Exact Pareto Front for Multi-Objective MDPs?*, provides an exact-frontier method based on the polytope structure of multi-objective MDPs. Its assumptions and fully observed MDP setting must be checked before porting to this adaptive causal-probing problem; our current finite partially observed belief/policy-tree model is not automatically covered by that result.

For causal-discovery evaluation, ACDB separates skeleton recovery, directed-DAG recovery, and intervention efficiency under budgeted interventions; CausalArena evaluates multiple SCM families and real-data regimes; TCD-Arena evaluates time-series causal discovery across 33 assumption violations. None was run here, and cross-entropy values are not numerically comparable to their F1/SHD metrics.
