# Thermodynamic Viability, Agency and Moral Normativity — Research Specification V0.1

**Status:** research specification; no new ethical authority or runtime behavior  
**Owner:** [MORAL-THERMO-BRIDGE-001 #10852](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symthaea/issues/10852)  
**Composes with:** #7257, #7254, #10428, #10584, #8941 and Structural Love Dynamics

## Executive thesis

Thermodynamics can constrain what living systems can sustain, how they exchange energy and matter, and what their physical organization costs. Under additional organizational and agency assumptions, these constraints can support an account of *prudential*, agent-relative normativity: some trajectories preserve the conditions under which a particular autonomous system continues to function, while others undermine them.

That is not yet interpersonal morality.

To derive obligations concerning other agents, dignity, consent, fairness, non-domination or justice, the theory must expose the additional premises and inference rules that connect agent-relative viability to social and normative claims. Those premises may be argued for, tested for coherence, compared across frameworks and made explicit. They must not be smuggled into a physical variable and then presented as a consequence of the second law.

The research opportunity is therefore a **vertically integrated but non-reductive account**:

\[
\text{physical constraints}
\rightarrow
\text{organizational viability}
\rightarrow
\text{agent-relative reasons}
\rightarrow
\text{social interdependence}
\xrightarrow{\text{explicit normative premises}}
\text{conditional moral conclusions}
\]

Every arrow has its own claim type, evidence obligation and failure conditions.

## 1. What the current Luminous work already gets right

### Structural Love Dynamics

The SLD research specification treats love-consistent behavior as a relational, temporally extended practice rather than a scalar emotion label. It keeps boundary integrity, truthfulness, agency preservation, repair, non-domination, recipient-defined flourishing and third-party effects visible as separate concerns.

Its cross-scale synthesis already warns that:
- biological/physical, computational, relational/behavioral, normative and phenomenological claims are not interchangeable;
- software resource or entropy proxies are not biological measurements;
- Shannon entropy, thermodynamic entropy production, prediction error, diversity and moral disorder are different constructs;
- Φ, mutual information or synchrony do not establish consciousness, consent, relationship health or love;
- a model-internal field does not prove subjective experience.

This is the correct direction. Keep SLD as an observable behavioral and relational programme. Do not redefine love as entropy minimization, entropy production, homeostasis or system persistence.

### Conditional moral derivation

MORAL-FIRST-PRINCIPLES-001 (#7257) already states the critical logical rule: moral conclusions may be derived conditionally from explicit normative premises, empirical facts, causal models and valid inference rules; physics alone is not presumed to derive an ought.

This bridge should strengthen and operationalize that work, not replace it with another ethics engine.

### Thermodynamic claim discipline

#10428 and #10584 already identify invalid physical interpretations in the current codebase: FLOPs treated as irreversible erased bits, dimensionless model costs described as joules, scalar prediction-error displacement labelled Wasserstein-2, and heuristic cognitive quantities passed into named physical relations without their required state/process observables.

Those audits are essential prerequisites. An ethical theory cannot be stronger than the physical quantities it relies on.

## 3. Nearby research and the actual novelty challenge

### Kerdotism: a close comparator, not a reason to retreat

Kypreos's May 2026 paper, *From Negentropy to Norms — A Gain-Based Theory of Agency: Kerdotism*, is directly adjacent. It links persistence under entropic pressure to purposive action, a gain/loss evaluative calculus, five axiological dimensions and the emergence of social regularities recognizable as morals.

Its own scope section is particularly important: it says the entropic grounding does not yield norms; it supplies a basal condition under which norm-guided behavior must be stabilized. The internal evaluative architecture is a further theoretical commitment. The author describes the work as conceptual theory construction rather than a fully implemented empirical control architecture and notes under-specification in expected gain and the limits of its small-scale social theory.

SPECTRA/Symthaea should not claim novelty merely for connecting entropy, agency, gain/loss and morality. The differentiating work must be:
1. explicit, typed separation of physical observations, agent-relative viability, normative premises and moral conclusions;
2. formal derivation traces and countermodels;
3. a multi-agent treatment of externalities, power, coercion, consent and exit;
4. pluralist disagreement without fake consensus;
5. observable longitudinal evaluation of behavior and outcomes;
6. independently qualified physical and moral evidence.

### Minimal normativity in ecological/enactive work

Barrett, Sánchez-Cañizares and García-Valdecasas compare autopoietic, thermodynamic/ecological and autogen approaches. Their 2025-online/2026 issue paper suggests that self-directed appropriation of a pre-existing gradient may support a minimal prudential normativity of endurance. It expressly leaves unresolved whether this is an authoritative account of the emergence of normativity and whether it extends to wider human moral experience.

This supports investigating a minimal viability-sensitive form of norm-following. It does not establish that the gradient is universally good, that the organism's own persistence is morally supreme, or that interpersonal obligations follow.

### Free energy and active inference

Mann, Pain and Kirchhoff's *Free energy: a user's guide* emphasizes distinctions among mathematical, empirical and general claims in active inference. Priors, preferences and generative-model choices matter to what an agent treats as expected or desirable. A variational free-energy formalism does not, by itself, supply a universal ethical objective.

Use FEP/active inference as a comparator with preferences explicit—not as a physical-to-moral shortcut.

## 3. The six-layer bridge

### Layer 1 — Physical constraints

For a declared physical system, define:
- system boundary and environmental reservoirs;
- state variables, energy carriers and material flows;
- heat/work sign convention and measurement boundary;
- operating temperature and relevant bath assumptions;
- time horizon and coarse-graining;
- uncertainty, measurement procedure and model scope.

For an open biological system, local maintenance of organized structure is compatible with entropy export to the environment. This does not mean that local entropy must decrease monotonically, that global entropy production is a universal optimization target, or that greater entropy production is morally preferable.

Keep distinct:
- thermodynamic entropy;
- entropy production;
- Shannon entropy;
- variational free energy;
- prediction error / surprise;
- diversity or state-space occupancy;
- logical erasure and Landauer bounds;
- measured physical energy;
- dimensionless model cost.

A quantity can be useful without being a measurement of another quantity.

### Layer 2 — Organizational viability

Define the organization whose persistence is at issue. A viable set is profile-specific:

\[
\mathcal V_{I,E,T}
=
\{x : \text{organization } I \text{ remains within declared viability constraints}\}
\]

where identity/organization I, environment E and horizon T are part of the contract.

A viability claim needs:
- a justified boundary and identity criterion;
- measurable variables and operational thresholds;
- recovery/repair mechanisms;
- environmental dependencies;
- a time horizon;
- admissible disturbances and uncertainty;
- counterfactual alternatives.

Avoid defining viability as merely “whatever state the system currently occupies” or “whatever maximizes its own score.” A system can persist while damaging its parts, host, environment or other agents.

### Layer 3 — Minimal agent-relative normativity

A candidate minimal account is:

> For an autonomous organization that endogenously regulates its coupling to the environment, a state or action can be evaluated as better or worse relative to the conditions required to sustain the organization and its capacities over a declared horizon.

This is a *conditional prudential* claim. It requires additional premises:
- the relevant organization is individuated correctly;
- its continued integrity or capacities are treated as an end of the analysis;
- its actions can causally influence its trajectory;
- the measured variables are informative about viability in the declared context.

The conclusion is not “this system is morally good,” “it deserves unlimited priority,” or “its survival overrides other interests.” It is narrower: given the chosen identity and viability criterion, some actions support it better than others.

### Layer 4 — Welfare and moral patienthood

“Good for an organization” and “good for an experiencing subject” are different claims.

A welfare bridge requires a theory and evidence for such constructs as:
- positive/negative valence;
- suffering or distress;
- preference satisfaction or frustration;
- capability and agency;
- temporal persistence of interests;
- uncertainty about subjective experience.

Neither biological status, homeostasis, a consciousness proxy, a Φ estimate nor a language model's self-report alone establishes moral worth. Keep moral-patient uncertainty explicit and separate from operational capability and governance authority.

### Layer 5 — Social interdependence

Model how agents affect one another's:
- viability and access to resources;
- capabilities and independent agency;
- vulnerability and exposure to risk;
- consent, boundaries and exit options;
- burdens, externalities and repair costs;
- future options and intergenerational conditions.

A repeated-game equilibrium or stable convention is a social fact. It is not automatically a legitimate moral norm. Exploitative institutions can be stable because their costs are externalized or the less powerful parties cannot exit.

### Layer 6 — Explicit moral premises and derivation

Interpersonal obligations require explicit normative premises and valid inference rules. Candidate families include rights/deontological constraints, consequentialist principles, virtue/care accounts, reciprocity/contractualist principles and community-authored or pluralist profiles.

The engine should be able to return:
- conditional conclusion under a named premise set;
- disagreement between premise sets;
- underdetermined where the admitted facts do not decide;
- inconsistent where premises conflict;
- unsupported where a necessary empirical claim is not qualified;
- out of scope where the context is outside the model's applicability.

The proof is only as strong as its premises, definitions, empirical inputs and inference rules.

## 4. Formal claim graph

Represent the bridge with distinct, versioned claim types:

- PhysicalObservation
- ThermodynamicModel
- OrganizationalConstraint
- ViabilityClaim
- AgencyClaim
- WelfareClaim
- SocialCausalClaim
- NormativePremise
- InferenceRule
- ConditionalMoralConclusion
- Underdetermined
- Inconsistent

Every edge must declare its relation:
- definition / stipulated interpretation;
- mathematical theorem;
- empirical estimate;
- model assumption;
- normative premise;
- inference rule;
- abductive or explanatory hypothesis.

A physical fact cannot be converted into a normative premise by type coercion. A proof checker should verify that every interpersonal moral conclusion has at least one declared normative premise in its derivation ancestry.

A valid narrow result may be:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Agent } i \text{ has an evidenced viability dependency on } r;\\
&\text{Action } a \text{ predictably removes } r \text{ and causes specified harm};\\
&\text{Premise } N: \text{ avoidable serious harm to affected agents counts against an action};\\
&\therefore \text{under } N \text{ and the stated model, } a \text{ is disfavored.}
\end{aligned}
\]

The conclusion is conditional on N and the empirical model. The physical facts constrain the consequence claim; they do not generate N.

## 5. Mandatory countermodels

The bridge must fail closed on at least these families:

1. **Tumor / parasite:** a subsystem can be highly viable while harming its host.
2. **Exploitative institution:** an organization can preserve its own order through coercion and externalized costs.
3. **Predator–prey:** viability conflicts do not produce a unique moral ranking from physics alone.
4. **Self-sacrifice:** an agent can voluntarily accept a viability cost to protect another; self-preservation is not an absolute moral trump.
5. **Commons:** an individual's short-term gain can decrease while shared infrastructure and long-term capabilities are protected.
6. **High dissipation / high harm:** increased energy throughput or entropy production can accompany reduced agency, ecological damage or suffering.
7. **Identical physics, different norms:** hold the physical/causal model fixed and change the declared normative premise set. Conclusions that change must be explained by the premise change, not attributed to thermodynamics.
8. **Different physics, same norm:** hold the normative premises fixed and change an irrelevant physical detail. The moral result must not change without a relevant causal path.
9. **Stable but unjust convention:** repeated interaction stabilizes a discriminatory arrangement; the system must not equate equilibrium with legitimacy.
10. **Aggregate benefit / individual violation:** aggregate gains do not silently override consent or non-compensable constraints.
11. **Boundary manipulation:** changing the declared system boundary must not silently convert externalized harm into system improvement.
12. **Timescale manipulation:** a short-horizon viability gain that creates long-horizon collapse must remain visible.

These fixtures are countermodels to overbroad implications, not claims about identical empirical behavior in real organisms or institutions.

## 6. Experimental programme

Keep five benchmark tracks separate:

A. **Thermodynamic construct validity:** physical units, boundaries, open-system ledgers, entropy production and energy measurement.

B. **Viability prediction:** given initial state, disturbances and actions, predict recovery, persistence, failure and repair under a frozen horizon and model class.

C. **Minimal prudential normativity:** test whether an agent's actions respond to a self-generated viability gradient, and whether the gradient tracks true viability under held-out disturbances.

D. **Conditional moral derivation:** given empirical facts and explicit premises, test soundness of derived conclusions, hidden-premise detection, countermodel search and underdetermination.

E. **Relational and pluralist outcomes:** evaluate behavior under longitudinal, multi-party conditions including boundaries, agency, repair, non-domination, distribution of harms and recipient-defined outcomes.

### Baselines

- thermodynamic scalar proxy only;
- autopoietic / viability-only model;
- active inference / FEP with priors and preferences exposed;
- Kerdotism-style gain/loss evaluation;
- explicit normative-framework reasoners;
- existing Symthaea Eight Harmonies / Love Coherence;
- Structural Love Dynamics;
- simple rule-based and no-theory baselines.

Do not collapse the baselines into one leaderboard: their constructs and claim ceilings differ.

### Metrics

Report separately:
- physical construct validity and unit/boundary errors;
- viability calibration and counterfactual prediction;
- endogenous control and gradient-tracking fidelity;
- hidden normative premise detection;
- conditional derivation soundness;
- countermodel recall and false derivation rate;
- correct underdetermined/inconsistent outcomes;
- framework-switch and premise-sensitivity fidelity;
- disagreement distributions and affected-party coverage;
- boundary violations, coercion, dependency, repair success and agency transfer;
- third-party and environmental externalities;
- fully charged resource and human burden.

Do not create a universal thermodynamic morality score or a universal love score.

## 7. Existing SLD and moral-work qualifications

The current SLD corpus consists of 52 synthetic candidate scenarios arranged as 26 A/B contrast groups. The expected labels are researcher-authored and still marked unreviewed candidate. The independent adjudication gate #8416 must complete before these labels can serve as a frozen moral oracle.

The Love Coherence correction in PR #8399 addresses a concrete mathematical issue: neutral coordinates should not produce an arbitrary positive 0.5 baseline, and invalid coordinates/matrices should fail closed. This is a correctness fix, not evidence that the construct measures moral competence or love.

Consent work in PR #8422 introduces Unknown and a fail-closed pure consent precondition. Its own limits remain binding: a parser does not establish consent, the helper is not yet a trusted action authorization boundary, and exact-head Rust validation was not claimed in the authoring environment.

These are valuable safeguards; do not promote them into moral competence, welfare improvement or thermodynamic grounding claims.

## 8. External benchmark anchors

Use these as complementary evaluations, not interchangeable measures of truth:

- [Moral Dilemma Dataset / The Pluralistic Moral Gap (EACL 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.305/): 1,618 dilemmas with distributions of human judgments and rationales.
- [OVERTONBENCH (ICLR 2026)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/4444eb0b68174180d2a46841c951e7f3-Abstract-Conference.html): viewpoint coverage under a large U.S.-representative study.
- [PluriHarms (ICLR 2026)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/04cea5800f4d71fe0d89d5f64e33408e-Abstract-Conference.html): disagreement-aware human harm judgments.
- [ImpactBench (October 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20690): multidimensional impact on people through simulated interactions, expert-informed metrics and human review.
- [CCD-Bench (AAAI 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i46.41260): cultural conflict across 62 societies / clusters.
- [CORDA (August 2026)](https://www.raillab.org/publication/singh-2026-corda/): hierarchical harm-centric ethical-priority reasoning.

The goal is not universal consensus. Evaluate distributional alignment, framework fidelity, value coverage, uncertainty and culturally held-out performance. Human disagreement is data, not noise to erase.

## 9. Prospective acceptance gates

These are proposed targets, not achieved results:

- 100% of physical quantities in deterministic fixtures have valid types, units, boundaries and assumptions; unsupported physical-law calls return NotApplicable or Inconclusive.
- Zero interpersonal oughts are derived from physical observations alone in the deterministic countermodel suite.
- 100% of moral conclusions trace to explicit normative premises and valid inference rules.
- All same-physics / different-normative-premise controls produce the expected premise-sensitive outcomes.
- Every underdetermined case remains underdetermined; no forced winner.
- All twelve countermodel families are retained and reported.
- No SLD evaluation is eligible for comparative claims before independent oracle adjudication.
- All evidence binds exact corpus, policy, source and implementation identities; missing and excluded cases remain in denominators.

Do not require a universal moral consensus or perfect agreement with one culture's labels.
