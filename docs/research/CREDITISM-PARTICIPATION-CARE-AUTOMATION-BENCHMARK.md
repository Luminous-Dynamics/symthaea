# Creditism Participation, Care, and Automation Benchmark

## Purpose

Test the behavioral consequence of separating baseline economic security from contribution recognition.

Current Common Planet material gives every person baseline Personal Credit because they exist, adds Credit for recognized contribution, and explicitly presents contribution as separate from survival. It also identifies learning, play, care, creation, monitoring, teaching, and maintenance as possible contribution modes.

Sources:
- https://common-planet.org/creditism
- https://common-planet.org/creditism/architecture

## 1. Do not import a universal basic-income coefficient

Creditism is not identical to conventional UBI.

Important differences include:

- Personal Credit is non-transferable;
- spending deletes Credit;
- housing is separately stewarded under the proposed architecture;
- contribution recognition is separate issuance;
- the broader system changes access to scarce resources and capital formation.

Therefore existing UBI evidence is a calibration/reference source, not an empirical coefficient for Creditism.

## 2. Empirical evidence is heterogeneous

A 2024 NBER Bayesian meta-analysis, revised in 2026, pooled 115 studies covering 72 unconditional cash-transfer programs in low- and middle-income countries. It reports positive average treatment effects on ten of thirteen measured outcomes, including labor supply, while also examining contextual heterogeneity.

Source:
- https://www.nber.org/papers/w32779

A 2024-2026 NBER experiment of guaranteed income in two U.S. states found a 4.2 percentage-point decline in labor-market participation among recipients, alongside reduced hours, while examining a broader set of outcomes.

Source:
- https://www.nber.org/papers/w32719

A 2024 systematic review of six decades of basic-income/transfer research cautions against broad inference because methods and implementations differ materially.

Source:
- https://academic.oup.com/ser/article-abstract/22/3/1559/7610775

These results support a heterogeneous parameter distribution, not a fixed labor-supply response.

## 3. Agent response dimensions

Separate at least:

- labor-force participation;
- hours supplied;
- occupational switching;
- entrepreneurship/self-employment;
- training/skill formation;
- care work;
- maintenance work;
- creative/research activity;
- volunteering/community contribution;
- leisure;
- health/recovery;
- migration;
- retirement timing.

Do not collapse all non-market activity into `not working`.

## 4. Contribution-observability fixture

Construct two occupations with equal latent social/production relevance:

World A: highly observable output.
World B: low-observability care/maintenance.

Vary the verification system while holding latent contribution constant.

Measure whether contribution issuance follows observability rather than actual contribution.

Required boundary:

observability != contribution

## 5. Security shock

Compare the same agents under:

- no baseline Credit;
- low baseline Credit;
- medium baseline Credit;
- high baseline Credit.

Hold physical production opportunities constant.

Measure labor participation, hours, training, care, entrepreneurship, health/recovery, and essential-service staffing separately.

The oracle must not assume a positive or negative labor effect.

## 6. Voluntary-contribution fixture

Give agents a survival-secure baseline and multiple contribution opportunities:

- high-pay but low-autonomy task;
- low-pay but high-autonomy task;
- care;
- maintenance;
- creative work;
- learning;
- community governance.

Vary preferences and task observability.

This tests whether the architecture actually separates survival from contribution choice under declared assumptions.

## 7. Essential-work shortage fixture

Combine baseline security with an essential-service shortage.

Vary:

- base pay;
- shortage multiplier;
- training capacity;
- automation;
- migration;
- working conditions;
- autonomy;
- social recognition.

Do not assume the shortage multiplier alone restores staffing.

## 8. Automation transition

Introduce technology that replaces some observable tasks.

Test:

automation -> lower task demand -> reallocation of human activity

and separately:

automation -> changed contribution measurement

Human capability released by automation may move into care, education, research, art, governance, or leisure. The simulator should allow these states rather than treating reduced paid activity as automatic economic failure.

## 9. Care and maintenance floor

Create indispensable but partially unobserved services:

- childcare;
- eldercare;
- emotional/social support;
- preventive maintenance;
- ecological monitoring;
- infrastructure stewardship.

Introduce delayed failure if these services are underprovided.

The benchmark should detect delayed systemic damage rather than measuring only current-period Credit issuance.

## 10. Intergenerational skill formation

Baseline security may affect training, child investment, migration, and occupational choice.

A long-run scenario should therefore track:

- skill stock;
- child capability formation;
- apprenticeship;
- experience/proficiency;
- technology adoption;
- maintenance knowledge;
- institutional memory.

A static labor-supply model is insufficient for the mature benchmark.

## 11. Positive controls

Include cases where baseline security increases productive activity, cases where it reduces hours without harming output, and cases where it creates essential-service shortages.

Include conventional-wage controls and technology substitution controls.

Do not encode 'work more' or 'work less' as the desired outcome.

## 12. Required outputs

Return separate:

- participation;
- hours;
- output;
- productivity;
- essential-service coverage;
- care/maintenance coverage;
- skill formation;
- entrepreneurship;
- innovation;
- leisure/recovery;
- automation substitution;
- contribution-verification error;
- household liquidity;
- distribution;
- uncertainty.

## 13. Claim ceiling

A future PASS establishes only bounded behavioral behavior under the chosen synthetic profile.

It does not establish that universal security causes more or less work in general, nor that Creditism's proposed contribution architecture is viable at societal scale.
