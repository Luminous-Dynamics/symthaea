# PLASMA-MEDICAL-001 — evidence-qualified translational research benchmark

**Issue:** https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symthaea/issues/12236  
**Date:** 15 November 2026  
**Status:** public research register and S0 synthetic claim audit  
**Claim ceiling:** no treatment protocol, clinical decision support, treatment dose, device safety, efficacy, authorization or Luminous medical-device capability

## 1. Executive thesis

Medical plasma should become a separate application programme sharing the general plasma physics, metrology, evidence and assurance infrastructure with manufacturing—but not inheriting industrial bond-qualification claims.

The first major opportunity is a **biological-interface evidence and dosimetry system** that reconstructs what physical exposure was delivered, what biological response was measured, what clinical claim the study can support, and what additional evidence is needed before a stronger claim is justified.

The programme distinguishes direct cold atmospheric plasma (CAP/CAPP), indirect plasma-activated liquids (PAL/PAW), intraoperative/surgical-margin applications, radiation-dermatitis prevention, dental/periodontal/endodontic research, non-clinical plasma sterilization, thermal plasma, and blood plasma. These are not interchangeable interventions.

## 2. Research evidence map

### 2.1 Chronic wounds

Strohal et al. (2025) report a multicentre randomized, single-blind, placebo-controlled study with 70 participants (35 per arm). Both groups received best-practice wound care; CAP was compared with a placebo device as an adjunct. The authors report wound-area reduction and faster healing.

Source: Journal of Wound Care, DOI 10.12968/jowc.2025.0207.

Interpretation:
- this is a human comparative study, not solely in-vitro evidence;
- the sample remains modest;
- the claim applies to the study's exact population, device, protocol and standard-care context;
- no result automatically transfers to other wound etiologies, plasma architectures, doses or healthcare settings.

### 2.2 Chronic-wound synthesis

Yuan et al. (2026), Journal of Tissue Viability, reviewed 12 RCTs involving 477 participants. The authors report improvements in wound-area and healing outcomes, while highlighting substantial technology and methodological heterogeneity. A separate March 2026 randomized, open-label multicentre trial in 60 adults with chronic venous leg ulcers provides an important counterpoint: air-based CAPJ had a greater numerical wound-area reduction at week 17 (-72.9% versus -56.7%), but the difference was not statistically significant (P=0.30); complete healing was 42.9% versus 30.4% (P=0.361). Immediate microbial burden fell, but the trial did not demonstrate statistically significant superiority on the primary wound-area endpoint. Non-significance is not evidence of equivalence or proof of no benefit. This strengthens the need for indication-specific and device-specific evidence rather than a universal positive CAP claim.

Source: DOI 10.1016/j.jtv.2026.101017.

The review is a useful comparator, but should not be reduced to one universal CAP effect size. Device types, treatment schedules, wound populations and methods differ.

### 2.3 Diabetic foot ulcers

A separate DFU-specific review/meta-analysis reports three RCTs, 107 participants and 126 ulcers. It reports an early wound-area signal and no sustained between-group bacterial-load advantage; no serious treatment-related adverse events were reported in the included trials. Larger and longer RCTs are recommended.

Source: PMID 41264397, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41264397/.

Do not treat microbial-load changes as equivalent to complete wound closure, recurrence prevention, limb preservation or survival.

### 2.4 Radiation dermatitis

NIPP-RD III is a Phase III randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study for non-invasive physical plasma to prevent radiation dermatitis during breast-cancer radiotherapy. The German Clinical Trials Register update of 8 October 2026 reports recruitment complete, study continuing and final sample size 141 (target 140); no Phase III results are posted in that record. Its primary endpoint is clinician-reported dermatitis severity at completion of radiotherapy, with patient-reported symptoms, objective skin-colour measurement, corticosteroid prescription and adverse effects as secondary measures. The registry also summarizes earlier Phase I (n=30) and Phase II (n=64) work, but those summaries are not Phase III results.

Source: DRKS00032560 (registry last updated 8 October 2026), https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00032560/details.

This is prevention of treatment-associated skin injury. It is not a tumour-treatment result. Protocol publication is not a Phase III result.

### 2.5 Dentistry

Hegde et al. (2026) systematic review: 19 studies comprising 11 in-vitro, seven animal and one human randomized clinical study. The authors report preclinical promise in antimicrobial, remineralization, bonding and tissue-modulation areas, but conclude human clinical evidence is limited and routine clinical application remains preliminary.

Source: DOI 10.1155/ijod/7100799.

### 2.6 Oncology and surgical-margin research

The published CHCP Phase I clinical study (NCT04267575) reports 20 patients treated intraoperatively after macroscopic resection of advanced or recurrent solid tumours. The main objective was safety. It is a narrow surgical-margin investigation, not a randomized efficacy trial or proof of systemic antitumour immunity.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10378184/.

Important regulatory distinction: an FDA investigational device exemption (IDE) permits a clinical investigation under the relevant conditions; it is not FDA marketing clearance or approval.

### 2.7 Plasma-activated water

A 2026 Phase I randomized-trial protocol proposes testing plasma-activated water as a wound rinse in diabetic-foot-ulcer patients.

Source: PMID 42431657, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42431657/.

It is a protocol, not an efficacy result. PAW has its own chemistry, storage, stability, container and biological-matrix questions; it is not direct CAP exposure.

### 2.8 Biological dosimetry frontier

A 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis covering more than 400 studies discusses dose heterogeneity and biphasic / hormetic CAP responses. It proposes:
- a physical index: volumetric reactive oxygen/nitrogen species flux;
- a biological index: redox-homeostatic status.

Source: DOI 10.1016/j.redox.2026.104162.

This is a proposed framework, not a universal validated clinical dose standard. It is nevertheless a valuable research direction because device settings alone cannot describe biological exposure.

### 2.9 Existing medical device reference: kINPen MED

The INPTDAT technology catalogue identifies kINPen MED as a commercially available Class IIa medical device used in clinical practice for chronic wounds and pathogen-induced skin diseases. The catalogue lists a nonthermal argon APPJ, below 3.5 W power, 1 MHz frequency, 4–6 standard L/min argon flow and 35–38 °C temperature. This is an incumbent hardware/clinical-integration reference, not a matched efficacy result or independent Luminous measurement.

Source: https://www.inptdat.de/kinpen%C2%AE-med.

Competitive implication: Luminous should not claim to invent a medical plasma jet. The opportunity is to benchmark dose reconstructability, treatment-area throughput, gas burden, serviceability and biological/clinical outcomes against existing systems under identical indications and protocols.

## 3. Paradigm shifts

### 3.1 From source settings to reconstructable biological dose

A medical CAP record should bind the source waveform, source/device revision, gas composition and purity, flow, geometry, distance, exposure time, treated area/volume, temperature, humidity, ambient environment, tissue/liquid interface, spatial dose distribution, diagnostic calibration and uncertainty.

Where scientifically and clinically appropriate, measure RONS speciation/flux and a biological response. Voltage, nominal power and time alone are not a transferable clinical dose.

### 3.2 From one-dimensional dose to a tissue-response surface

The 2026 dose literature motivates measuring both beneficial and harmful response regions. A useful model must represent possible antimicrobial effect, cytotoxicity, cytoprotection and antioxidant response, tissue repair/proliferation, inflammatory signalling, tissue injury and delayed recovery.

Do not assume more exposure means greater benefit. Do not let a model optimize patient exposure outside an independently approved research protocol.

### 3.3 From a universal plasma treatment to a specific intervention identity

The source device, electrode/discharge geometry, working gas, accessories, distance, tissue interface, liquid formulation and treatment sequence are part of the intervention. CAP and PAW are different interventions. APPJ and DBD are not inherently dose-equivalent.

### 3.4 From microbial surrogates to clinical endpoints

A lower bacterial burden does not prove complete healing. A reduction in wound area does not automatically prove fewer amputations or improved survival. In-vitro tumour-cell death does not establish patient benefit. Every clinical claim must be linked to the endpoint actually measured.

### 3.5 From retrospective claims to lifecycle safety

A medical device needs risk analysis from development through use, maintenance and post-market surveillance. Relevant hazards depend on the device and intended use and can include heat, UV, electrical leakage, ozone/NOx, gas handling, reactive chemistry, tissue injury, contamination, consumable degradation, software faults, usability and cybersecurity.

### 3.6 From autonomous therapeutic AI to clinician-governed evidence

Symthaea may organize literature, check evidence ceilings, evaluate uncertainty, generate study-design alternatives and support validated statistical analysis. This increment does not prescribe a dose, select a treatment for a patient, determine a diagnosis, or command medical hardware.

The evaluator rejects or blocks Phase I safety/feasibility promoted to clinical efficacy; trial protocol promoted to results; IDE authorization described as marketing approval; CAP claims transferred to PAW; DFU claims generalized to all chronic wounds; source evidence transferred to another indication or broad device population; a proposed dosimetry framework described as a validated universal dose standard; and blood plasma confused with ionized gas plasma.

S0 does not validate the underlying medical literature or prove any clinical outcome. It validates whether software preserves declared evidence/claim boundaries.

## 5. Benchmark programme

### S0 evidence-contract targets
- 100% of intervention records carry modality, indication, evidence class, study design and claim ceiling;
- zero false SUPPORT for human efficacy from preclinical evidence or protocol-only records;
- zero cross-indication or cross-modality claim promotion without an explicit evidence bridge;
- zero IDE-to-marketing-authorization promotion;
- deterministic replay of known-answer negative controls;
- transparent SUPPORTED / BLOCKED / REJECTED counts, no blended score that hides overclaims.

### Physical dosimetry / preclinical targets
- traceable physical exposure and spatial mapping;
- calibrated thermal and relevant chemical/species observations with uncertainty;
- device-to-device and run-to-run reproducibility;
- measured liquid/tissue-interface chemistry;
- indication-appropriate cytotoxicity and tissue-compatibility characterization;
- dose-response and time-response spanning both beneficial and harmful regions;
- independent laboratory/device-lot replication.

All physical work requires separately approved laboratory protocols, qualified operators and risk controls.

### Clinical benchmark
- exact indication and intended-use statement frozen before study;
- preregistered trial and statistical analysis plan;
- standard care maintained in relevant comparator arms;
- sham/placebo and blinding where feasible;
- adequate sample size based on a clinically meaningful endpoint and defensible variance assumptions;
- complete healing / time-to-event / recurrence for wound indications where appropriate, not only short-term area or bacterial burden;
- baseline stratification for clinically relevant etiology and severity;
- adverse events, rescue therapy, pain, infection, patient-reported outcomes and missing follow-up included;
- independent replication and external validity;
- transparent null and harmful findings.

No universal sample size, patient dose or clinical effect target is asserted. These are application- and protocol-specific clinical decisions.

## 6. Application priority

1. Chronic wounds, as an adjunct to standard care: strongest practical first evidence lane; begin with one exact wound etiology and an expert clinical partner.
2. Radiation dermatitis prevention: separate indication with a Phase III protocol precedent; do not confuse it with anti-cancer efficacy.
3. Dentistry/endodontics: evidence synthesis and preclinical dosimetry first; one human RCT is not enough for routine-use claims.
4. Oncology/surgical margins: exploratory; the Phase I safety study is a precedent, not proof of efficacy.
5. PAW/PAL: a separate formulation and stability programme; do not inherit direct CAP treatment assumptions.

Medical-equipment sterilization is a separate non-patient-exposure manufacturing/sterilization workstream. Blood plasma is not gas-discharge plasma.

## 7. Regulatory context

### South Africa

SAHPRA publishes:
- classification guidance SAHPGL-MD-04 v5 (updated 28 February 2025);
- clinical evaluation guidance SAHPGL-MD-16;
- clinical investigation guidance SAHPGL-MD-17;
- Medical Devices Reliance Guideline SAHPGL-MD-22 (25 February 2026).

See https://www.sahpra.org.za/medical-devices-and-in-vitro-diagnostics-guidelines/ and https://www.sahpra.org.za/medical-devices/.

The applicable device classification, establishment licensing, clinical-investigation route and registration requirements need a device-specific determination by the regulator and qualified regulatory counsel. Do not assume foreign IDE/CE status is a South African authorization.

### United States

FDA QMSR became effective on 2 February 2026 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference for relevant finished-device manufacturers. Source: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/quality-and-compliance-medical-devices.

An IDE for clinical research is not marketing clearance/approval.

### Risk and software

- ISO 14971:2019 remains current following its 2025 review: https://www.iso.org/standard/72704.html.
- IEC 62304:2006 with Amendment 1:2015 sets medical-device software lifecycle requirements: https://www.iso.org/standard/38421.html.

These are standards to assess for the eventual device and software role, not a declaration of compliance.
