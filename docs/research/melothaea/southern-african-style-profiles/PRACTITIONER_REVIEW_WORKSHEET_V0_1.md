# Amapiano and Gqom practitioner review worksheet v0.1

**Status:** proposed research instrument. Not a consent form, legal instrument or claim that consultation has occurred.

## Purpose

Turn the literature-grounded candidate profiles in this directory into practitioner-reviewed research hypotheses before any executable style profile or authenticity claim is made.

Run separate review cohorts for Amapiano and Gqom. Do not assume a shared panel, vocabulary, scene history or evaluation rubric is representative of both. Invite contributors according to the work they actually do and the context they know: producers, DJs, instrumentalists, vocalists, dancers/performers, historians, educators, sound engineers and other community-nominated knowledge holders.

A contributor may decline a label, use a local name, identify a substyle, challenge the framing or say that a question is outside their expertise. Do not infer community membership or expertise from audio.

## Before a session

1. Share the profile and its sources in advance, clearly marking literature-derived hypotheses as hypotheses.
2. Agree the review scope: tradition/scene, era, region, substyle, role and whether the discussion concerns composition, performance, production, dance context or history.
3. Obtain separate, purpose-specific permission for participation, recording, transcription, quotation, attribution, internal analysis, public release, derived data, model training and commercial use. One permission must not imply another.
4. Explain compensation, withdrawal routes, retention/deletion policy, who can access notes and whether any example audio will be played or stored.
5. Use only recordings and examples whose relevant composition, recording, performer and distribution rights have been checked for the proposed use. Do not record, upload or redistribute examples by default.
6. Allow anonymous or attributed contributions according to the contributor's choice. Avoid collecting unrelated personal information.

## Review record

Use one record per claim, disagreement or proposed discriminator. Preserve separate contributions rather than silently averaging them.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Profile | Amapiano or Gqom |
| Claim ID | Exact ID from the JSON profile, or a new review-local ID |
| Contributor role | Self-described role(s), optional |
| Context | Scene, era, region, substyle or repertoire scope supplied by contributor |
| Disposition | Supported in scope / contested / context-dependent / inaccurate / unknown / outside expertise |
| Explanation | Contributor's own wording; do not rewrite into a stronger claim |
| Counterexample | Example or description that would falsify or narrow the claim |
| Nearest neighbour | Which other tradition/style is being compared, and on what dimension |
| Permission | Exact permitted uses of this review record |
| Attribution | Anonymous / named / organization / restricted |
| Follow-up | Sources, collaborators or unresolved questions identified by the contributor |

Do not collapse multiple valid but conflicting accounts into a single "community consensus". Record who made each claim, the context in which it applies and whether the contributor agreed to attribution.

## Core questions — ask for each profile

### Identity and naming

- What names do musicians and producers use for this music in your context?
- Which labels are contested, outdated, too broad or too narrow?
- What are the meaningful regional, historical, scene or substyle differences?
- Which neighbouring genres are commonly confused with it, and why?
- Are there cases where two people reasonably describe the same recording differently?

### Composition and arrangement

- What makes a composition recognizably part of this practice beyond instrumentation or a sound preset?
- How do phrases, sections, repetition, development, breaks, transitions and endings work in examples you consider representative?
- Which structures are common, optional, unusual or inappropriate?
- What valid counterexamples should a composer be able to produce?

### Rhythm and groove

- Which pulse, cycle, accent, subdivision, syncopation, interlocking or timing relationships matter?
- Is a single BPM or meter an adequate description for the examples in scope?
- Which parts carry, anticipate, answer, interrupt or reinforce other parts?
- Which rhythmic changes alter the identity, and which are ordinary performance variation?

### Pitch, harmony and melody

- Which pitch materials, melodic roles, harmonic movements, ornaments or vocal practices matter in this context?
- When is harmony foregrounded, subordinate, static or absent?
- Which features are borrowed, optional or specific to one substyle?
- What would be a musically plausible variation that should still retain the identity?

### Instrumentation, performance and production

- Which instruments or electronic sounds are typical, optional, substituted or scene-specific?
- What roles do they perform, and how do those roles interact?
- Which playing techniques, articulations, microtiming or production choices matter?
- Which descriptors (for example, "raw", "dark", "warm", "deep" or "jazzy") have a specific meaning in this context, and what would make them misleading?
- How does the identity survive a different instrument, performer, renderer or production treatment?

### Creative boundaries

- What should a respectful system avoid doing when asked to compose in this style?
- Which combinations are welcomed, ordinary, experimental, controversial or misleadingly labelled?
- When should a system ask, disclose uncertainty, offer alternatives or abstain?
- What attribution or context should accompany a hybrid composition?

## Amapiano-specific prompts

Use the questions below as prompts, not as expected answers:

- How do keyboard/piano, pads, bass and log-drum roles vary across the examples and substyles you know?
- When does the log drum function as a melodic, bass, rhythmic or hybrid part?
- How do instrumental introductions and gradual layer entries vary? What are valid exceptions?
- What distinctions matter between Amapiano, deep/soulful house, Kwaito, Bacardi, Gqom and other locally named practices?
- Which historical relationships with marabi, mbaqanga, bubblegum and Kwaito are accepted, contested or too simplistic?
- Which vocal, language, DJ/producer and dance relationships should the profile preserve?

## Gqom-specific prompts

Use the questions below as prompts, not as expected answers:

- Which rhythmic characteristics distinguish the examples you identify as Gqom from generic house or techno?
- How do kick, bass, syncopation, silence, repetition and layered rhythmic events interact?
- What does "slow-burn" mean in the examples you know? Which tracks do not follow that trajectory?
- How do early and newer Gqom productions differ?
- What do descriptors such as "raw" and "dark" mean to producers and listeners? Which production shortcuts would misrepresent them?
- How should the profile distinguish Gqom from Kwaito, Amapiano and other Durban/South African electronic forms without erasing their relationships?

## Contrastive listening / composition tasks

Where rights allow, ask contributors to review matched pairs or short composition sketches:

1. Same arrangement and renderer, different proposed style grammar.
2. Same style grammar, different instrument or renderer.
3. Same groove, different arrangement trajectory.
4. Same arrangement trajectory, different groove.
5. Same identity-bearing relations, different performer/take.
6. A proposed hybrid with all contributing layers disclosed.

For each, ask: what changed, what remained, what makes the output plausible or implausible, and whether the label is appropriate? Preserve "not enough context" and "not sure" as valid answers.

Do not use automatic genre classifiers as ground truth. Do not tell contributors which system produced which output during blind comparisons. Do not use a single preference vote as a cultural-authenticity verdict.

## Analysis and exit conditions

- Keep Amapiano and Gqom analyses separate.
- Report contributor role, context, missingness and disagreement without exposing restricted identities.
- Do not treat repeated comments from one contributor as independent votes.
- Where quantitative claims are planned, define independent source-work, producer, performer and session units before recruitment; estimate power/precision from a pilot.
- Keep annotation, internal evaluation, public release and training permissions separate in the resulting manifest.
- Promote a claim only within the context and scope that the evidence supports.

**M1 exit:** a versioned, attributed review record; a rights-cleared source plan; documented counterexamples and disagreements; and a preregistered pilot protocol. This exit does not itself authorize model training, public audio release or a claim of authenticity.
