# Evidence-Grounded Art Intelligence
**Status:** research/design specification v0.1  
**System owner:** Symthaea  
**Integrations:** Mycelix CSCP Art Passport, Sol-Atlas spatial evidence, Symtropy condition/lifecycle evidence  
**Claim ceiling:** retrieval, contextual explanation and evidence triage; no automated authenticity, ownership or valuation certification

## 1. Objective

Develop a multimodal research and discovery system that helps people find, compare and understand artworks while keeping each interpretation connected to inspectable source evidence.

Symthaea should not become a general image-classification leaderboard or an “AI art authenticator.” Its differentiated task is to combine:
- visual and textual semantic representations;
- artwork identity and rights-aware metadata;
- temporal and relational evidence about creators, exhibitions, custody and scholarship;
- spatial records and capture uncertainty;
- explicit uncertainty, disagreement, and abstention.

The primary output is a ranked set of candidates and evidence explanations, not a verdict about what an artwork is or who owns it.

## 2. Paradigm shift: perception + evidence graph + temporal reasoning

Do not assume one representation must solve all art intelligence tasks.

### Perceptual retrieval layer
Use strong, reproducible vision-language and visual encoders as baselines (CLIP/OpenCLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2 and appropriate later equivalents). They support similarity and retrieval, but similarity is not attribution, cultural interpretation, or provenance.

### Structured relational layer
Represent artist, object, edition, medium, technique, period, exhibition, location (access-controlled), collection, evidence source and scholarly assertion as typed entities/relations. Use Hyperdimensional Computing to investigate compositional binding, role/filler structure, robust relation retrieval and compact context indexing. HDC is a hypothesis to benchmark—not a presumed replacement for large pretrained encoders.

### Temporal reasoning layer
Use CfC/LTC-style dynamics to investigate event-sequence reasoning, evolving claim states, and temporal context. Compare with transparent event-graph algorithms, temporal graph models, sequence transformers and gradient-boosted metadata models. A dynamic state model must not erase contradictory records or invent missing transitions.

### Evidence and explanation layer
Every surfaced statement should resolve to one or more source-backed claims, with issuer/role, date, scope, source digest, access policy, and evidence status. Symthaea can say “these records conflict” or “the claim is not supported by the available passport.” It must not upgrade a search result or similarity score into authentication.

## 3. Core tasks

1. **Semantic discovery:** retrieve artworks for natural-language and multilingual queries over authorized text/image assets.
2. **Visual similarity:** find visually related works while explaining whether the relation is composition, palette, subject, medium, geometry or learned embedding proximity.
3. **Curatorial context:** retrieve documented creator, movement, technique, period, exhibition and scholarly relationships.
4. **Provenance discrepancy triage:** identify impossible/overlapping chronology, missing event links, inconsistent names/identifiers, and unsupported assertions for human review.
5. **Attribution research assistance:** gather relevant comparison material and source histories; report alternative hypotheses and evidence limitations without declaring an attribution.
6. **Spatially informed discovery:** compare Sol-Atlas geometry/scene properties where licensed and relevant, keeping metric shape distinct from view-synthesis appearance.
7. **Condition-aware collection search:** retrieve Symtropy observations and condition records as time-bounded evidence, never as a diagnosis generated by Symthaea.
8. **Multilingual cultural access:** support language-aware discovery while respecting community-defined vocabularies, restricted descriptions and the fact that translation may not preserve cultural concepts.

## 4. Benchmark design

### Corpus composition
Start with a rights-cleared pilot corpus:
- The Met Open Access records/images where the relevant assets are explicitly marked CC0;
- other institutional collections only after field-level rights review;
- creator-contributed material with scoped permission for storage, embedding, retrieval, evaluation, publication and/or training;
- synthetic records only for logic, security and conformance tests.

A public metadata API does not mean every referenced image is freely reusable. Keep rights decisions attached to every media asset and every derived embedding.

Target at least 1,000 curator/artist-authored query-artwork relevance pairs for a pilot benchmark, then grow toward 10,000+ pairs across media, regions and languages. Document how judges were selected, the adjudication rubric, disagreement and cultural competence. Avoid treating one curator's taste as universal ground truth.

### Data splits and leakage controls
- artist-disjoint holdout;
- institution-disjoint holdout;
- time-based holdout for newly added catalogues;
- language-held-out and region-held-out slices;
- artwork-level near-duplicate and crop/derivative grouping before split;
- separate train/validation/test query sets;
- preserve collection-level source dependencies to prevent a catalogue page and its copied auction text leaking across splits.

Do not random-split image crops or catalogue duplicates as independent examples.

### Baselines
1. Metadata keyword and BM25 search.
2. CLIP/OpenCLIP text-image retrieval.
3. SigLIP-family retrieval.
4. DINOv2 visual nearest-neighbour retrieval.
5. Hybrid metadata + visual encoder reranker.
6. Typed graph retrieval / rules.
7. Gradient-boosted metadata model.
8. Temporal graph/sequence baseline for provenance chronology.
9. Human curator search and relevance judgments under a declared time budget.

Compare on identical data, candidates, filters, hardware budget, and rights permissions. Separate frozen pretrained embeddings from fine-tuned models.

### Retrieval metrics
- Recall@1/5/10/50;
- nDCG@5/10/20;
- mean average precision;
- precision at fixed curator review budget;
- query latency, index memory, embedding cost and update cost;
- calibration and selective risk at abstention coverage;
- per-region, per-language, medium, style and institution performance;
- counterfactual query robustness and spelling/translation sensitivity.

### Evidence-reasoning metrics
For provenance/chronology cases, use a frozen expert-adjudicated dataset and report:
- assertion extraction precision/recall by assertion type;
- chronology violation precision/recall;
- false contradiction rate;
- evidence-reference validity and completeness;
- calibration and abstention;
- expert review time and correction rate;
- performance on unseen institutions and new claim formats.

High-risk false alerts are costly: do not promote an ownership/custody discrepancy to a priority alert unless precision meets the pre-registered threshold. Every model-generated observation remains a candidate requiring review.

## 5. Targets and route beyond current SOTA

These are stretch hypotheses, not achieved results:
- ≥5% relative nDCG@10 improvement over the strongest matched open baseline on artist- and institution-held-out queries;
- no statistically or practically material degradation for underrepresented regions/languages/media;
- maintain or improve calibration and abstention utility, not just ranking;
- for high-risk discrepancy triage, ≥98% precision on a held-out expert-labelled set before priority routing, while reporting recall and missed-case cost separately;
- for provenance chronology, ≥90% recall on the frozen material-contradiction set at a pre-registered false-alert ceiling;
- ≥20% reduction in median expert discovery/review time in a controlled pilot without increasing missed material contradictions;
- p95 query latency and memory measured at declared corpus size and hardware.

A model does not outperform SOTA simply by exceeding one metric on a private sample. Claims require matched baseline versions, same data/splits, repeated seeds where stochastic, confidence intervals, subgroup results, external replication and model-card style limitations.

### HDC/CfC research hypotheses
Test whether HDC can improve compositional and role-sensitive retrieval (e.g., “works by maker A exhibited at venue B before date C and documented in source D”) under constrained memory, explainability or incremental-update budgets. Test whether CfC improves event-sequence generalization to new time gaps and irregularly sampled records.

Run ablations:
- encoder only;
- metadata only;
- graph rules only;
- HDC relation binding only;
- CfC temporal state only;
- encoder + graph;
- encoder + HDC;
- encoder + graph + CfC;
- full fusion.

Report quality and resource cost. If HDC/CfC do not improve the matched baseline on a task, retain the simpler method for that task.

## 6. Evidence explanation contract

Every result should expose:
- query and filters as interpreted;
- artwork passport IDs and versions;
- ranked candidate and task-specific relation explanation;
- relevant claims, source identifiers, issuer roles, timestamps and evidence digests;
- rights/access decision and excluded assets;
- uncertainty, missing fields, conflicts and out-of-domain warning;
- model, index, preprocessing, schema and benchmark digests.

Prohibit ungrounded statements such as “verified authentic,” “legally owned,” “stolen” or “forged.” Safer outputs include “passport records an attribution assertion by X, supported by Y; a conflicting source says Z; no independent authentication is inferred.”

## 7. Fairness, rights and cultural stewardship

- Do not embed/index assets without a lawful and appropriately scoped basis.
- Training permission, evaluation permission, embedding/indexing permission, display permission and commercial reuse are distinct.
- Keep sensitive cultural metadata, location and restricted imagery out of public retrieval, caches, vector indexes, previews and model logs.
- Support community-defined terms and access vocabularies where appropriate; do not normalize culturally distinct traditions into a Western canonical hierarchy.
- Record missing or withheld information as a policy state, not as an absence of cultural significance.
- Never optimize artistic value, human worth or cultural importance into one scalar score.
- Make artist and community removal/correction workflows traceable, including derived embeddings/indexes where legally and technically feasible.

## 8. System boundaries

- **Mycelix CSCP:** canonical IDs, assertions, rights, consent, provenance and disclosure policy.
- **Symthaea:** authorized retrieval, multimodal reasoning, discrepancy triage, explanations and calibrated abstention.
- **Sol-Atlas:** metric geometry, scene graphs, coordinate frames, spatial transforms and capture uncertainty.
- **Symtropy:** instrumented condition/exposure histories and validated physical-simulation receipts.

Symthaea must query only the authorized projection from Mycelix. It cannot infer missing rights or expand access because a visual model can process an asset.

## 9. Initial delivery sequence

1. Freeze an evaluation task taxonomy and rights-aware data manifest.
2. Implement BM25 + frozen CLIP/SigLIP + DINOv2 baselines.
3. Create expert/artist relevance judgments and leakage-safe splits.
4. Implement a typed claim/evidence graph adapter over Mycelix's Art Passport.
5. Add HDC compositional retrieval experiments and CfC temporal event sequence experiments as independent ablations.
6. Add discrepancy triage with abstention and source-grounded explanations.
7. Run shadow-mode pilot; no automated attribution, authentication, legal or valuation decisions.
8. Publish full benchmark card, errors, null results and external replication plan.

## 10. Sources and adjacent work

- [ArtBench-10: 60,000 images across 10 styles](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-18420-0) — a narrow style benchmark, not an art-understanding benchmark.
- [The Met Collection API](https://metmuseum.github.io/) — public-domain/CC0 records and images where designated; rights must be checked per asset.
- [CLIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020) and [SigLIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15343) — strong general-purpose vision-language retrieval baselines.
- [DINOv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193) — visual representation baseline.
- [Art Basel & UBS Global Art Market Report 2026](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026) — market context, not evidence of product impact.
- [ICOM Object ID](https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/objectid/) and [CIDOC CRM](https://cidoc-crm.org/) — complementary object identification and cultural heritage semantics.

## 11. Claim ceiling

The benchmark can establish retrieval utility and discrepancy-detection performance on defined, rights-cleared evaluation sets. It cannot establish artistic worth, universally correct cultural interpretation, legal title, authenticity, theft status, or the truth of every historical claim. HDC/CfC architectural novelty is not evidence of superior task performance until matched experiments demonstrate it.
