## 1. Research question

An observation is generated by both the scientific system and its measurement channel. Calibration offset, gain, instrument configuration, assay batch, and sensor state can introduce persistent uncertainty. A planner that treats every fidelity as a fixed accuracy may over-credit repeated observations when they share an unknown calibration error.

This reference makes scientific hypothesis (h) and persistent instrument state (c) separate coordinates of a joint latent state (p(h,c)). Calibration and scientific measurements compete for the same resource budget. The second experiment can depend on the first result, and the terminal scientific action can depend on both.

The central question is decision-focused: **when should the system spend resources learning about its measurement process before learning about the scientific system?**

## 2. Finite decision contract

A two-stage policy is (pi=(e_1,e_2(y_1),a(y_1,y_2))). Each complete ambiguity scenario (s) supplies likelihoods (p_s(ymid h,c,e)) and utility (U_s(h,a)). The reference selects:

[
max_{pi}min_{sin S}
sum_{h,c,y_1,y_2}p(h,c),
p_s(y_1mid h,c,e_1),
p_s(y_2mid h,c,e_2(y_1)),
U_s(h,a(y_1,y_2)).
]

The same contingent policy is evaluated in every scenario; it cannot inspect the hidden scenario identity. Every first-observation branch must fit the hard cumulative budget. Scenario-specific expected cost is reported separately and cannot conceal a branch-level cap violation.

A `Calibration` candidate is validated to have likelihoods independent of (h) when (c) is held fixed. It may reveal instrument state but cannot leak scientific-hypothesis information. The model permits a non-factorized joint prior.

Successive observation noise is conditionally independent given the joint latent state and scenario. A persistent shared instrument state can nevertheless make repeated observations dependent marginally. Time-varying drift, autocorrelated residuals, and instrument-state transitions are outside this v1 model.

## 3. Analytic negative control: repeatability is not calibration

The fixture has two equally likely hypotheses (h\in\{0,1\}), two equally likely persistent instrument offsets (c\in\{0,1\}), and two plausible noise scenarios. Scientific measurement reports (h\oplus c); a reference standard reports (c).

| Scenario | Calibration accuracy | Science-measurement accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nominal | 0.95 | 0.95 |
| Plausible shift | 0.90 | 0.90 |

The no-experiment decision value is 0.50. Repeating the same scientific measurement cannot identify (h): the unknown shared XOR offset remains confounded, so its best achievable decision value is 0.50.

Calibration followed by a scientific measurement allows the action to be the XOR of the two observed bits. The resulting values are:

[
V_{nominal}=0.95^2+0.05^2=0.905,\qquad
V_{shift}=0.90^2+0.10^2=0.82.
]

The exact maximin policy therefore chooses calibration first, then science measurement, with robust value 0.82 and robust gain 0.32. These are analytic fixture values, not measurements of a real instrument.

## 4. What this adds to SDCB

The existing robust multi-fidelity reference makes fidelity-specific likelihoods and resource vectors explicit. This increment introduces a distinct uncertainty axis: the measurement process has its own latent state, and a calibration action can improve the interpretability of later science observations.

It gives falsifiable tests for whether the planner:
- values calibration only when it can change a downstream decision;
- avoids treating shared-bias repeats as independent confirmation;
- chooses calibration before science when that sequence improves robust decision value;
- preserves one policy across all plausible scenarios; and
- respects every contingent resource cap.

Compare against uncalibrated EIG, calibrated EIG, R-I/R-IDeA, one-step EVSI, exact non-myopic design, finite-scenario maximin, and multi-fidelity BO under the same information, candidate, utility, and budget contracts. Do not fold these methods into a new weighted score without ablation evidence.

## 5. Research context and benchmark plan

Calibration experiment design has a long history, including designs for in-use measurement uncertainty and systematic effects; later work explicitly includes model discrepancy and surrogate uncertainty. Bayesian design for model-discrepancy calibration remains active in 2026. This reference is a decision-policy synthesis, not a claim to replace that literature.

Relevant references:
- Forbes & Minh, [Design of linear calibration experiments](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2013.04.002).
- Hu, Ao & Mahadevan, [Calibration experimental design considering field response and model uncertainty](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2017.01.007).
- [Bayesian experimental design for model discrepancy calibration](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2025.114469), *Journal of Computational Physics* (15 January 2026).
- Tang, Sloman & Kaski, [R-IDeA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/tang26d.html), AISTATS 2026.
- [MF-OSEMO](https://doi.org/10.1609/AAAI.V34I06.6560), AAAI 2020, and [MF-JESMOC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2025.131674), *Neurocomputing* (7 December 2025).

Keep external benchmark lanes distinct:
- **Petri-bench:** method integrity, experiment isolation, statistical rigor, and evidence-backed conclusions under an eight-call budget.
- **ActiveSciBench:** closed-loop enzyme-kinetics and gene-regulatory mechanism discovery.
- **Autonomous Science Bench:** lab-origin campaign workflow evaluation; the 0.1 pilot has only six tasks and three trials per task, so close leaderboard gaps are not conclusive.
- **ASI-Bench / AstaBench:** project-level research workflow autonomy, not controlled experimental-design substitutes.
- **Fresh hidden task families:** calibration leakage, measurement-channel shift, and benchmark anti-saturation.

Preserve each upstream benchmark's native score. Report SDCB's decision regret, channel-state recovery, predictive calibration, resource use, false causal claims, and evidence replay as separate metrics.

## 6. Limits and next increments

The Rust reference is an exact enumerator bounded to at most four scientific hypotheses, four instrument states, eight joint states, four scenarios, three actions, three candidate experiments, binary outcomes, and exactly two experiment stages.

Not implemented: time-varying drift; latent-state transitions; autocorrelated residuals beyond persistent shared state; continuous observations; adaptive proxy retraining; HDC/CfC policy distillation; EGBT receipt integration; external benchmark execution; physical calibration.

Next:
1. Compile and exact-head qualify the finite reference and negative controls.
2. Add instrument-state transition/drift and calibration-staleness models.
3. Add independent-instrument replication to distinguish repeatability from reproducibility.
4. Bind accepted measurements to EGBT receipts; invalid, stale, duplicate, or misbound evidence must cause zero scientific- or instrument-belief mutation.
5. Add a read-only adapter to the acquisition bake-off before changing any production selector.

**Evidence boundary:** five Rust tests are authored but not compiled or executed in the current environment. JSON and fixture shape checks passed, and the finite example was independently enumerated outside Rust. No external tournament, physical experiment, or SOTA result is claimed.
