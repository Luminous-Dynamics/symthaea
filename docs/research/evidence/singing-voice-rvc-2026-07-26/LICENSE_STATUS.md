# Licensing status

## Summary
**All audio outputs in this bundle are research-only and not commercially
cleared under project policy**, even though one layer of the pipeline
(voice timbre) is built entirely from permissively-licensed components.
The reason is the *other* layer: the singing performance itself.

## The two layers, and why they have different licensing status

### 1. Voice timbre (RVC + `af_heart`) — permissively licensed
- **Kokoro-82M** (source of the `af_heart` voice used as the RVC training
  target): Apache 2.0. Model card describes training data as
  permissive/non-copyrighted.
- **RVC** (`RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI`, pinned
  commit in `environment/source-revisions.txt`): MIT License.
- **RVC's pretrained base checkpoints** (`hubert_base`, `rmvpe.pt`,
  `pretrained_v2/f0G40k.pth` / `f0D40k.pth`, used as the fine-tuning
  starting point): hosted in `lj1995/VoiceConversionWebUI` on HuggingFace,
  itself MIT-licensed.
- This layer alone would be reasonable to describe as "permissively
  licensed with documented provenance" (a real, more careful phrasing
  than the "zero-licensing-risk" claim made earlier in this project's own
  session notes — that phrasing was an overclaim and should not be reused).

### 2. Singing performance (DiffSinger acoustic model) — research-only
- **Training data**: CSD (Children's Song Dataset, KAIST), specifically
  the English subset, sourced from the Zenodo record referenced in
  `manifests/corpus-manifest-csd.csv`. The CSD reannotation repository
  licenses its content under **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**.
- **This project's prior explicit decision** (recorded earlier in this
  session, not re-litigated here): use CSD to prove the DiffSinger
  training pipeline works, while deferring any commercial-licensing
  decision for a shippable voice. That decision still stands — it was
  never resolved, only deferred.
- **Open legal question, not resolved by this bundle**: whether a model
  *trained on* CC-BY-NC-SA data, and its *generated outputs*, are
  themselves bound by that license as "adapted material" is a real,
  jurisdiction-dependent, unsettled question this project has not sought
  legal review on. This bundle does not assert a definitive answer either
  way — it only records that the question is open and unresolved.

## Net effect
The RVC conversion step changes *who the voice sounds like*, not *what
license governs the performance it's singing*. A listener hearing
`af_heart`'s timbre does not change the fact that the pitch, rhythm, and
phrasing were generated by a model trained on non-commercial data.

**Project policy classification: research-only, not commercially cleared,
pending either (a) retraining the performance-generating model on
commercially suitable data, or (b) qualified legal review of the
model-training / generated-output licensing question above.**

This classification applies to every `.wav` file referenced by
`manifests/outputs.sha256`, without exception.
