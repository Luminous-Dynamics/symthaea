# MACE W3C PROV and SCITT Interoperability V1

**Status:** Interoperability mapping proposal and JSON-LD context published on `mace/source-origin-witness-quorum-v1`. This is a vocabulary mapping, not yet an executable exporter or SCITT client.

## 1. Design

MACE should keep its consumer-owned evidence contracts, exact digest bindings and DecisionNeed policy, while exposing provenance in interoperable vocabularies rather than making its graph schema the only way to understand the data.

The W3C PROV family models provenance through **entities, activities and agents**, and includes concepts for derivation, generation, attribution, association, versioning and provenance of provenance. MACE's mapping is:

| MACE concept | PROV representation |
|---|---|
| Raw source, candidate evidence, payload, derivation receipt, graph manifest, signed statement | `prov:Entity` |
| Fetch, archive lookup, extraction, normalization, summarization, graph replay, verification | `prov:Activity` |
| Publisher, transformer, acquisition operator, resolver, evaluator, verifier | `prov:Agent` |
| Derived candidate or payload ancestry | `prov:wasDerivedFrom` |
| Output generated by acquisition or transformation | `prov:wasGeneratedBy` |
| Activity input artifacts | `prov:used` |
| Operator responsible for an activity | `prov:wasAssociatedWith` |
| Publisher/producer attribution | `prov:wasAttributedTo` |

The MACE-specific terms in the JSON-LD context preserve digests, common-cause groups, DecisionNeed bindings, operator identities, failure domains and exact receipt commitments. These are extension attributes, not replacements for PROV relationships.

## 3. SCITT transparency integration

[IETF RFC 9943](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9943/) defines an architecture for transparency of **single-issuer signed statements**. MACE can use this architecture to publish:
- the lineage resolver's signed source-lineage statements;
- the source-origin verifier's per-node origin statements;
- each independent witness operator's per-node observation statements;
- trust-profile and revocation-related statement history where the service supports it.

Each issuer remains separately attributable. The consumer verifies the signed DSSE/in-toto statement and the transparency evidence under the relevant issuer/log policy. For multi-operator evidence, each operator's statement must remain a separate issuer assertion; a central logger must not collapse them into one purported independent attestation.

Transparency and independent acquisition solve different problems:

| Mechanism | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| DSSE/in-toto | Which key signed exact statement payload bytes | That the statement is true |
| W3C PROV | Interoperable representation of entities, activities, agents and derivation relationships | Publisher authenticity or action authorization |
| SCITT transparency | Issuer statement inclusion/history and transparency auditing | Source truth, ancestry completeness or independence of issuers |
| MACE witness quorum | Consumer-policy-constrained corroboration across pinned operators and declared failure domains | Universal ground truth or unlisted common causes |

SCITT's single-issuer transparency model should not be described as a multi-party consensus protocol. A transparency log may reveal equivocation or missing history only to the extent that independent auditors/witnesses verify the service's transparency properties and compare views. Distinct issuer statements and trust domains remain necessary.

## 4. Suggested rollout

1. Freeze the graph and receipt semantics and publish the JSON-LD context.
2. Build a deterministic Rust projection from the canonical MACE graph to PROV JSON-LD, preserving every edge and commitment.
3. Add round-trip tests proving that import/export cannot silently discard MACE-specific constraints.
4. Add one SCITT client per issuer role, each using independently provisioned issuer credentials.
5. Verify inclusion/consistency evidence separately from the issuer's DSSE signature and the MACE graph replay.
6. Benchmark log delay, proof size, verification latency, equivocation detection and outage behavior. A transparency-service outage must not be misreported as successful provenance verification.

This mapping deliberately separates provenance interoperability, cryptographic attestation, transparency and consumer decision policy.
