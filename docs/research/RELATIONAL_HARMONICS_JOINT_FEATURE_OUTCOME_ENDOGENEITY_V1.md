## Question

The existing RH-006 calibration explicitly leaves feature/outcome endogeneity uncovered.

This stress test asks whether the finite-sample equal-risk null constructed under the current conditional-independence covariance formula remains located at equal risk when a relational feature innovation shares shocks with the outcome innovation.

## Controlled construction

The marginal outcome law is preserved:

- AR coefficient = 0.5;
- same heteroskedastic scale function as the existing diagnostic;
- same 48/4/16/24/16 rolling geometry;
- same ridge = `1e-4`.

Only one feature/outcome relationship is changed.

The innovation driving relational feature channel `raw[5]` is shared with the outcome innovation:

`eta_t = sqrt(1-omega^2) z_t + omega q_t`.

Here `q_t` also drives `raw[5]`.

Thus:

- `omega = 0`: independence control;
- increasing `omega`: increasing feature/outcome innovation dependence.

The finite-sample equal-risk quadratic is intentionally still constructed with the existing independence covariance formula.

That makes any displacement a direct diagnostic of the uncovered assumption rather than a byproduct of simultaneously changing the marginal outcome covariance model.

## Completed execution

- 40 feature paths;
- 50 outcome draws per path;
- `omega = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9`;
- weak and strong predeclared directions.

At `omega=0`, the independence-based nominal roots were approximately equal-risk, as expected.

As feature/outcome dependence increased, the same nominal roots became increasingly favorable to the relational model.

| Direction | omega | Mean loss differential at gamma=0 | Mean loss differential at nominal independence root |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| weak | 0 | -0.000906 | -0.0000005 |
| weak | 0.25 | -0.000237 | -0.000312 |
| weak | 0.50 | 0.001753 | 0.000729 |
| weak | 0.75 | 0.005058 | 0.003092 |
| weak | 0.90 | **0.007700** | **0.005135** |
| strong | 0 | -0.000901 | 0.0000011 |
| strong | 0.25 | -0.000245 | 0.001778 |

| strong | 0.75 | 0.004863 | 0.009075 |
| strong | 0.90 | **0.007620** | **0.012599** |

Positive loss differential means the NonRelationalContext MSE exceeds the RelationalAugmented MSE in the existing RH-006 sign convention.

The result is therefore a potential spurious incremental-relational advantage generated by joint feature/outcome dependence.

## Interpretation

This is a genuine applicability boundary.

The present risk identity can be correct for a fixed-feature/conditionally independent design while failing to describe the risk surface under endogenous feature/outcome generation.

The missing terms are the cross-moments between the feature process and outcome disturbance.

Conceptually, the risk surface must therefore be promoted from:

`Delta(gamma; Sigma_yy)`

to something that also conditions on the joint feature/outcome law, including the relevant feature-disturbance cross-covariance terms.

This matters directly for RH-006 because the intended scientific use is not merely a fixed-design algebra exercise. If real relational features and future outcomes share latent temporal drivers, the current conditional calibration cannot be assumed to transport.

## Important distinction

This experiment does **not** show that endogenous features invalidate RH-006 in general.

It shows something narrower and more useful:

> The current independence-based finite-sample null is not invariant to a controlled feature/outcome innovation-correlation stress.

The correct next object is consequently a joint-DGP finite-sample risk surface, not another bootstrap-depth increase under the independence DGP.

## Recommended next layer

Derive the risk differential with explicit feature/outcome cross-covariance.

For fixed feature matrices and a linear forecast operator `W`, the paired squared-error expectation contains:

- mean-model terms;
- outcome covariance terms;
- forecast-operator covariance terms;
- feature/outcome cross terms when disturbances are endogenous to regressors.

The next implementation should make those cross terms explicit and test:

`independence surface`

versus

`joint endogenous surface`

for:

- root existence;
- root displacement;
- effect radius;
- branch conditioning;
- estimated-nuisance calibration.

Only then can the applicability boundary say whether endogeneity requires a new estimator/procedure bridge or merely an expanded nuisance state.
