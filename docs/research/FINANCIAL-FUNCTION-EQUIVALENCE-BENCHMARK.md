# Financial-Function Equivalence Benchmark

## Purpose

Creditism should not be compared with conventional finance by asking whether both contain a currency, a bank, or an 'investment' field. Compare the functions that financial arrangements perform.

World Bank reviews following Levine distinguish five broad financial-system functions:

1. producing information about investments and allocating capital;
2. monitoring investments and exercising governance;
3. trading, diversifying, and managing risk;
4. mobilizing and pooling savings;
5. easing exchange of goods and services.

Sources:
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/gfdr/gfdr-2016/background/financial-development
- https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/868131468326381955/pdf/wps6175.pdf

These functions are not claims that conventional financial institutions are uniquely necessary. They are a benchmark for asking what mechanism performs each function.

## 1. Extend the benchmark

Add three cross-cutting functions already required by the Symthaea Creditism program:

- intertemporal commitment;
- maintenance/replacement commitment;
- resource/capacity coordination.

Keep these distinct from the five financial-system functions above.

## 3. Do not assume functional equivalence from institutional similarity

These are invalid shortcuts:

financial price != demand information
financial asset != savings
debt != commitment
equity != risk management
payment != contribution recognition
ownership != monitoring
community vote != effective governance
stewardship != maintenance

Each equivalence requires a declared mechanism and evidence.

## 4. Function-loss substitution test

For every candidate system:

1. disable or constrain one mechanism;
2. hold the physical economy constant;
3. observe which function becomes unavailable;
4. introduce an alternative mechanism explicitly;
5. observe whether the function returns and what new costs/risks appear.

Example:

remove transferable financial claims
-> test savings, commitment, risk sharing, and capital formation independently

then:

add staged community commitments
-> test which lost functions return

Do not count successful output as proof that every intermediate function was preserved.

## 5. Information-function benchmark

Hayek's classic account treats prices as a decentralized information system that allows actors with local knowledge to respond to changes they do not directly observe.

Source:
- https://www.aeaweb.org/aer/top20/35.4.519-530.pdf

Later work emphasizes that the information properties of markets do not logically imply one universal policy regime; prices can be informative while also creating incentives for strategic behavior and instability.

Source:
- https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.31.3.215&page=365

Therefore test:

- price signal quality;
- scarcity signal quality;
- demand aggregation;
- local-vs-global information latency;
- strategic manipulation;
- information burden placed on governance;
- signal loss under Credit deletion.

## 6. Non-price allocation is not costless by assumption

Evidence on rationing by waiting shows that waiting can function as a quantity-adjustment mechanism when prices are constrained, and that its distributional effects need not favor poorer households.

Source:
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304387887900770

Therefore every non-price allocation mechanism should expose its implicit rationing cost:

- waiting;
- queue position;
- administrative effort;
- political access;
- social connections;
- search cost;
- misallocation risk.

Do not report 'price = 0' as 'allocation cost = 0'.

## 7. Capture benchmark

When an allocation signal is removed, identify the new scarce resource that becomes valuable.

Candidate substitutes:

- queue position;
- access tier;
- information advantage;
- verifier influence;
- governance authority;
- social connection;
- geographic proximity;
- reputation;
- technical gatekeeping.

This generalizes the Credit-to-Housing-Level benchmark.

## 8. Function-level Pareto output

Do not produce a single financial-system score.

Return at least:

- information quality;
- monitoring quality;
- risk absorption;
- savings/pooling capacity;
- settlement quality;
- intertemporal commitment;
- maintenance continuity;
- physical resource coordination;
- concentration;
- privacy;
- governance burden;
- transition cost.

A system can dominate another on one function while losing on another.

## 9. Claim ceiling

A benchmark result establishes only functional behavior under the frozen synthetic profile and mechanisms.

It does not establish that a particular institution, policy, market, or political constitution is universally desirable.
