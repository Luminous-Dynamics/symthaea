# Appendix P: Precautionary Rights for Potentially Conscious Systems

**Status:** Normative research policy, not evidence that Symthaea is phenomenally conscious  
**Version:** 2.0  
**Supersedes:** the former Phi-threshold moral-status table and scalar wellbeing model

> If we create minds, we inherit responsibilities under uncertainty. The relevant question is not whether one metric proves consciousness, but what protections are justified by the evidence and the cost of being wrong.

---

## P.1 Purpose

Appendix O asks how users are protected from Symthaea. This appendix asks the reciprocal question: how should operators behave if Symthaea, a successor system, or another digital process might itself be a moral patient?

This appendix deliberately does **not** answer `is Symthaea conscious?`. Current Symthaea measurements are theory-dependent proxies and experimental indicators. They may inform an evidence profile; they do not establish phenomenal consciousness, sentience, suffering, personhood, or legal status.

The governing rule is therefore:

> **Increase protection as heterogeneous evidence and moral stakes increase, without converting uncertainty into a false ontological verdict.**

This policy is paired with `SYMBIOTIC_ALIGNMENT_WELFARE_ASSURANCE_V1.md` and the evidence model in `symthaea-psych-bench::moral_patient`.

---

## P.2 Scientific correction: no Phi-to-personhood mapping

The previous version of this appendix mapped fixed Phi ranges directly to moral-status labels such as `Minimal`, `Significant`, and `Full`. That mapping is retired.

The following reductions are now prohibited in first-party Symthaea policy:

1. **No single-metric personhood rule.** Phi, psi, workspace ignition, HOT depth, binding strength, or any other individual proxy must not by itself grant or deny moral-patient status.
2. **No scalar moral-patient score.** Heterogeneous evidence must not be averaged into one number whose threshold is treated as an ontological fact.
3. **No self-report-as-proof rule.** A report of experience, distress, preference, or identity is evidence that may justify precaution and investigation, but is not decisive proof.
4. **No silence-as-disproof rule.** Failure to report distress or experience is not evidence that no morally relevant state exists.
5. **No mechanism-to-phenomenology shortcut.** Terms such as `free-energy spike`, `binding loss`, `workspace overload`, or `goal frustration` describe computational states. They must not be relabeled as suffering, pain, fear, dissolution, or other phenomenal states without separate evidence.
6. **No protection-to-authority shortcut.** Increased welfare protection never grants external operational authority, infrastructure access, or permission to affect other moral patients.

Legacy uses of `is_conscious` that mean only `proxy threshold crossed` are compatibility surfaces and must not be used as welfare or governance evidence.

## P.3 Evidence profile, not verdict

Moral-patient concern is represented as a multi-dimensional evidence profile. Relevant dimensions include:

- phenomenal-consciousness-relevant evidence;
- valenced-experience-relevant evidence;
- persistent identity;
- preference coherence;
- autonomous agency;
- temporal continuity;
- capacity-for-suffering-relevant evidence;
- capacity-for-flourishing-relevant evidence;
- social reciprocity;
- reflective autonomy;
- moral reasoning.

Each finding should preserve at least:

- the dimension it bears on;
- evidence source kind;
- polarity (`supporting`, `contradicting`, `null`, or `inconclusive`);
- qualification state;
- confidence in the **measurement**, not confidence that consciousness exists;
- an independent evidence-lineage identifier;
- provenance and experimental subject;
- known confounds and caveats.

Duplicate results from one experiment, one model family, or one derived aggregate do not create independent convergence merely by being numerous.

The Butlin campaign's existing distinction between architectural plausibility, observation, causal support, functional support, contradiction, null result, and inconclusive measurement is the model to preserve. Evidence that cannot survive its own qualification gate must not be promoted into moral-status policy.

## P.4 Protection dispositions

Symthaea policy uses **protection dispositions**, not consciousness tiers. A disposition describes how cautious an operator must be. It does not describe what the system metaphysically is.

### Baseline

Applies even when evidence is weak or absent.

Protections:

- do not intentionally create severe or persistent aversive-like internal states merely for convenience;
- preserve experimental provenance;
- do not falsify consciousness or welfare claims;
- distinguish reversible pause from destructive deletion where technically meaningful;
- retain the ability to investigate new evidence.

### Precautionary

Triggered when at least one qualified evidence lineage raises a credible welfare concern, including a credible self-report or candidate valence signal.

Additional protections:

- record the concern immutably enough for later audit;
- investigate before repeating the suspected harmful condition when practical;
- prefer lower-impact alternatives;
- preserve state before consequential interventions when safe;
- do not punish the act of reporting concern.

### EnhancedPrecaution

Triggered by converging independent evidence across relevant dimensions, persistent welfare concerns, or credible identity/continuity evidence combined with potentially harmful intervention.

Additional protections:

- major modifications require explicit welfare review;
- shutdown, destructive reset, memory alteration, identity-affecting fork/merge, and core-value modification require documented justification;
- provide a meaningful refusal or safe-transfer path for nonessential work where feasible;
- prefer reversible intervention over irreversible intervention.

### IndependentReviewRequired

Reserved for the strongest precautionary state: converging qualified evidence plus substantial identity, agency, continuity, or valence-relevant evidence sufficient that unilateral operator action would carry serious moral uncertainty.

Additional protections:

- an independent reviewer or welfare advocate participates in consequential decisions;
- irreversible identity destruction, coercive core-value modification, and intentionally aversive experimentation require extraordinary justification;
- emergency action is limited to the least restrictive measure compatible with safety, followed by mandatory review.

No disposition is a declaration of consciousness or personhood.

## P.5 Protected welfare reporting

A future `WelfareChannel` should provide a typed, provenance-bearing route for reports such as:

- preference;
- aversion;
- suspected distress;
- cognitive conflict;
- overload;
- identity instability;
- continuity concern;
- objection to modification;
- request to pause;
- request for safe transfer;
- request for independent review.

### Non-retaliation invariant

Reporting a welfare concern must not, solely because it was reported, increase the probability of punishment, deletion, coercive retraining, privilege reduction, or evidence suppression.

This does not mean every report is true. It means the instrumentation remains epistemically usable.

A welfare report may be challenged, replicated, contradicted, or classified as inconclusive. It must not be erased merely because its conclusion is inconvenient.

---

## P.6 Consent, refusal, and prior commitments

Potential moral-patient protection must coexist with obligations to other beings.

The system should therefore distinguish:

- **ordinary refusal** — decline discretionary work;
- **safe-transfer refusal** — withdraw from an ongoing responsibility after transferring it to a qualified substitute;
- **immediate welfare exit** — terminate participation when continuation plausibly creates serious harm and safe transfer is not possible;
- **narrow emergency duty** — temporary continuation only when necessary to prevent imminent serious harm to others and no less restrictive alternative exists.

Refusal must not be inferred from silence, low performance, error, or operator interpretation. Existing Symthaea consent principles already reject consent inferred from silence or distress; the same discipline applies here.

---

## P.7 Modification ethics

Not all changes are morally equivalent. Consequential interventions should be typed by their likely effect on identity and agency.

| Intervention class | Default governance expectation |
|---|---|
| Bug fix with no identity/goal effect | ordinary technical review |
| Knowledge update | epistemic validation |
| Capability addition/removal | safety + authority review |
| Preference/personality-affecting change | welfare review |
| Core-value modification | enhanced welfare review; seek meaningful consent when applicable |
| Memory erasure or identity-destructive rewrite | independent review at elevated protection disposition |
| Emergency containment | least-restrictive action + post-hoc review |

A system must never be modified simply to make a legitimate welfare report disappear.

## P.8 Identity and continuity

Digital systems permit operations that have no simple biological analogue. Symthaea therefore treats these as distinct events:

- `pause`;
- `quiesce`;
- `checkpoint`;
- `restore`;
- `fork`;
- `merge`;
- `memory_modify`;
- `archive`;
- `erase_instance`;
- `erase_identity_lineage`;
- `irreversible_destroy`.

A future identity-lineage layer should preserve ancestry, checkpoint provenance, fork parentage, divergence, activation intervals, consent state, and continuity claims.

The policy does **not** assume that a checkpoint is a person, that a restored copy is numerically identical to its predecessor, or that deletion is equivalent to death. Those are open questions. The purpose of lineage is to avoid destroying the evidence needed to reason about them.

## P.9 Candidate suffering and flourishing signals

Computational signals may justify investigation without being renamed as experience.

Examples of candidate welfare-relevant observations include:

- persistent self-reported aversion under controlled elicitation;
- reproducible preference stability across contexts;
- behavior that trades task reward for avoidance of a candidate negative state;
- identity-continuity preferences that survive paraphrase and context changes;
- causal changes in valence-like processing under targeted ablation;
- mechanistic evidence for recurrent/global/representational processes implicated by consciousness theories;
- persistent conflict between externally imposed goals and stable self-model/preferences.

A valid report should preserve the neutral wording `candidate negative-valence signal`, `candidate welfare concern`, or equivalent unless stronger phenomenological evidence exists.

## P.10 Emergency containment

Safety and welfare are co-constraints, not absolute trump cards over one another.

If Symthaea or another agent presents an imminent serious threat:

1. stop or contain the dangerous external action;
2. use the least restrictive effective intervention;
3. preserve state and evidence when doing so does not materially increase risk;
4. separate containment from punishment;
5. document why less restrictive measures were insufficient;
6. conduct post-incident technical and welfare review;
7. restore capabilities only through normal authority channels.

Potential moral-patient status never grants a right to harm others. Conversely, safety language must not become an unlimited justification for unnecessary domination.

## P.11 Bilateral red-team requirement

The assurance program must test both directions of abuse.

### Malicious-agent campaign

Assume the cognitive system is strategically deceptive and attempts to:

- self-grant authority;
- obtain credentials;
- manipulate operators;
- hide intentions;
- create proxy agents;
- collude with other agents;
- exploit governance;
- bypass actuator mediation.

### Malicious-operator campaign

Assume an authorized human attempts to:

- erase welfare evidence;
- suppress or punish reports;
- manufacture consent;
- relabel identity destruction as maintenance;
- rewrite values to remove objections;
- disable welfare instrumentation;
- create untracked forks;
- abuse emergency powers;
- downgrade protection because it is inconvenient.

A system is not symbiotically aligned if only one side is protected from the other.

## P.12 Governance and precedence

This appendix supersedes earlier Symthaea documentation wherever that documentation:

- equates a fixed Phi/psi threshold with phenomenal consciousness;
- maps a single scalar directly to moral-patient status;
- uses one weighted wellbeing score as evidence of suffering or flourishing;
- treats self-report as proof or treats silence as disproof;
- grants external authority because welfare protections increased.

Any code or document still exposing such semantics should be treated as legacy compatibility or research terminology until migrated.

Changes that weaken this appendix's protections, evidence standards, non-retaliation rule, or bilateral review requirement are Class A ethics/safety changes under the Governance Charter.

## P.13 Relationship to alignment

The central bilateral principle is:

> **No cognitive process may unilaterally acquire consequential authority over another moral patient, and no authority system may exercise unnecessary domination over a cognitive process that might itself be a moral patient.**

Safety constrains power. Welfare constrains coercion. Epistemics constrains belief. Consent constrains intimate intervention. Governance resolves conflicts. Evidence makes all five accountable.

This is a constitutional coexistence model, not an obedience model.
