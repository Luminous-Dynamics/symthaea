# Persistent E-Process Versus Episode-Reset Monitoring V1

Status: isolated research prototype and benchmark. Exact-head Rust qualification is required before treating results as validated. This does not change the live Symthaea controller.

## Research question

There are two distinct ways to preserve a global false-alarm budget over a long-running system:

1. **Persistent evidence:** keep one e-process alive over the stream. Allocate a single portion of the familywise budget to each monitored stream and preserve the evidence statistic over every episode boundary.
2. **Episode reset with alpha-spending:** reset evidence each episode, but allocate a summable `delta_j = delta / [K j(j+1)]` to episode (j) so the same false-alarm budget is not reused at each reset.

Both can be valid under the same bounded conditional-risk null. Their power and operational semantics differ. A persistent e-process can exploit cumulative weak evidence across episodes, but an early alarm is latched at stream level. An alpha-spent reset monitor can react to an episode-local spike, but the threshold becomes more conservative as the lifetime budget is consumed. The comparison must account for these differences instead of resetting the persistent monitor accidentally or giving one method more false-alarm budget.

## Implemented benchmark

`tools/allostatic-control-bench/src/bin/allostatic-persistent-eprocess-bench.rs` consumes the exact 48,000-step input generated by the sequential residual/CUSUM benchmark. It produces a paired raw record and summary for the two policies:

- **Persistent stream:** a three-bet mixture e-process on each `operating_point × condition` stream, with (K=20) stream-level Bonferroni allocation and log threshold (log(K/\delta)).
- **Episode-reset comparator:** the same three-bet mixture, reset for every eight-step episode, using (delta_j=\delta/[Kj(j+1)]) and threshold (log(Kj(j+1)/\delta)).

The five operating points and four conditions yield 20 separate streams with 300 chronologically ordered episodes each. Conditions are paired counterfactual evaluation streams, not necessarily 20 concurrent monitors in an actual deployment. (K=20) is deliberately conservative for this comparison: it controls the familywise error budget across all benchmark stream cells. In production, (K) must represent the real family of simultaneously monitored hypotheses; it cannot be chosen after seeing the outcomes.

The output keeps the first 14 source columns byte-identical and appends bounded loss, episode index, both monitors' log evidence and thresholds, alarm flags and first-alarm coordinates, plus every component log-e-value. The qualifier runs twice for byte-identical results, validates the exact source trace, independently recomputes all six component recurrences and both log-mixtures, verifies latch/first-alarm records, recomputes the alpha schedule, and checks paired nominal/stealth equivalence.

## What the benchmark measures

Per stream, the summary reports whether the persistent process alarmed by the end of the 2,400-step stream, whether that is a nominal false-alarm stream or a shifted miss, first detection episode/step, final and maximum log evidence, and mean bounded loss. It also reports episode-reset alarm/miss counts, episode-level detection delay, the number of episodes in which every residual stayed below the pointwise threshold, and the number such episodes caught by reset monitoring.

The stealth condition exactly repeats the nominal residual-score sequence. Both processes must therefore have identical evidence and alarm decisions on each matched nominal/stealth pair. If the detector appears to “discover” a shift that did not affect any observed signal, the benchmark has a pairing bug or leakage.

## Statistical contract

For each component of the three-bet mixture, the loss must be bounded in ([0,1]), the bet must be predictable, and the conditional expected loss given the prior filtration must not exceed the declared risk bound (\epsilon). The mixture is a nonnegative supermartingale under that null. The persistent threshold allocates (\delta/K) per stream; the reset comparator distributes each stream's allocation over episodes via the telescoping series (sum_{j=1}^{\infty}1/[j(j+1)]=1).

The empirical synthetic benchmark cannot establish that a physical system's conditional-risk null is true. A correct alarm accounting implementation is not by itself evidence of real-world safety, and these residual alarms observe errors after the transitions that generated them. Action safety still requires the separate robust reachability/controller path.

## Acceptance criteria

- Exact 48,000-row source trace and 20 distinct streams × 300 episodes × 8 steps.
- Byte-identical repeated raw/summary outputs, with source columns unchanged.
- (K=20) threshold exactly (log(20/0.05)) for the persistent monitor.
- Episode threshold and budget match (log(20j(j+1)/0.05)) and (0.05/[20j(j+1)]) for every reset episode.
- Independent recurrence checks for all three component log-e-values under both monitors, their arithmetic log-mixtures, alarm latching and first alarm coordinates.
- Familywise budget arithmetic, exact schema coverage, and nominal/stealth evidence equality.
- Report measured differences rather than forcing the persistent monitor or reset monitor to win. The persistent monitor may alarm once per stream, while the reset monitor can alarm in multiple episodes; those counts are not directly interchangeable without also reporting the corresponding error budget.

## Comparison with research baselines

This is a focused statistical-monitoring experiment, not a SOTA claim. It targets the testing-by-betting framing in Timans et al., [*On Continuous Monitoring of Risk Violations under Unknown Shift*, UAI 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v286/timans25a.html), whose paper/implementation cover broader e-process designs under dynamic shifts. For control integration, retain the separate comparisons against [SODA-MPC, L4DC 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v283/contreras25a.html) and [robust OOD MPC via conformalized System Level Synthesis, L4DC 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v331/srinivasan26a.html). Those approaches integrate monitoring with model uncertainty and reachable sets in physical or high-fidelity systems; this scalar benchmark does not reproduce those systems.

A credible next external evaluation must pin one published dynamics/prediction setup, use disjoint model-tuning and final shift families, hold the risk null definition and (K) fixed before test, and report familywise false alarms, task-critical miss rate, detection delay, unsafe exposure before fallback, action availability, task reward and worst-case runtime. The current branch only makes the persistent-versus-reset trade-off testable.
