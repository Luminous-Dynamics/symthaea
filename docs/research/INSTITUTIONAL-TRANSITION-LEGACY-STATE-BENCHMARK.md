# Institutional Transition and Legacy-State Benchmark

**Status:** research design v0  
**Related:** #7046, #7052, #7047, #7031, #7040

## Purpose

Test institutional evolution when the starting world contains inherited claims, contracts, property rights, organizations, currencies, and governance relationships.

A mature target institution does not exist in a vacuum. The transition mechanism itself determines which resources, obligations, authorities, and capabilities survive the change.

Current Creditism transition material explicitly describes staged conversion of existing assets/claims and continued coexistence with national-currency obligations and legacy businesses during transition. This makes transition mechanics a first-class research layer rather than an implementation detail.

Source:
- https://common-planet.org/creditism/transition

## Core causal separation

~~~text
legacy state
   + transition rule
   -> conversion / coexistence / settlement
   -> target-state starting condition
   -> institutional evolution
~~~

Do not infer:

~~~text
target institution works
-> transition succeeds
~~~

The terminal state and the path to it are separate claims.

## Legacy-state vector

Freeze, as applicable:

- debt claims;
- equity claims;
- mortgages;
- leases;
- pension obligations;
- taxes and other public obligations;
- property titles;
- contracts;
- foreign-currency liabilities;
- supplier relationships;
- firms and productive assets;
- incumbent governance authorities;
- skills and occupational assignments;
- infrastructure;
- verification systems;
- social and network relationships.

Every legacy item receives a declared disposition:

- retained;
- converted;
- settled;
- extinguished by explicit rule;
- transferred;
- phased out;
- disputed;
- stranded;
- unresolved.

Absence from the target ontology is never sufficient evidence of extinguishment.

## Transition coexistence

Permit multiple circuits to coexist when explicitly declared:

- national currency + local Credit;
- debt + Community Credit;
- private ownership + stewardship;
- legacy contracts + new contribution recognition;
- existing enterprises + community accounts.

Each circuit retains its own accounting and authority identity.

## Required fixtures

1. High legacy debt vs low legacy debt.
2. Concentrated vs diffuse housing/property ownership.
3. Solvent enterprise conversion.
4. Insolvent enterprise conversion.
5. Legacy pension obligation.
6. Foreign-currency supply contract.
7. Mortgage conversion.
8. Rental-property conversion.
9. Strategic asset with concentrated ownership.
10. Incumbent governance resistance.
11. Partial jurisdictional adoption.
12. Failed transition followed by rollback.
13. Favorable mature state reached with severe transition disruption.
14. Smooth transition into a mature state that later fails.

## Outputs

Keep separate:

- conversion gains/losses;
- stranded claims/assets;
- temporary productive-capacity loss;
- capital-formation interruption;
- migration cost;
- settlement failures;
- legal/governance burden;
- external-settlement dependency;
- distributional incidence;
- institutional continuity;
- terminal-state performance.

A terminal-state success must not erase transition failure.

## Path-dependence experiment

Freeze the target institution and target physical environment.

Vary only the inherited legacy state.

If the resulting institutional evolution diverges, report the divergence as path dependence.

This is consistent with computational work showing that prior institutional sets and paths can affect behavior and later institutional choice rather than merely current outcomes. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0176268015000841

## Reversibility

Every transition profile declares:

- reversible changes;
- irreversible changes;
- rollback authority;
- rollback cost;
- converted-asset treatment;
- transition-issued claim treatment;
- conditions required for reversal.

A one-way transition is a valid experimental design, but irreversibility is itself an observable property and must not be hidden.

## Multi-regime counterfactual

Compare:

- immediate replacement;
- phased coexistence;
- local pilots followed by expansion;
- partial adoption with interoperable boundaries;
- failed adoption followed by rollback.

Hold the mature target design fixed where possible.

## Qualification boundary

A PASS establishes only declared legacy-state and transition semantics under the synthetic profile.

It does not establish feasibility, legality, justice, political acceptability, or real-world transition success.
