# Chemical-Process Evidence Attestation Design v1

**Status:** design proposal; not implemented or verified  
**Related:** [PR #7872](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symthaea/pull/7872), [issue #7978](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symthaea/issues/7978)  
**Design revision:** 1  
**Purpose:** bind an authorized endorsement to one exact chemical-process evidence envelope without promoting authenticity into scientific validity.

## 1. Problem statement

The Tier-B chemical-process registry v1.3.0 envelope digest detects partial edits when the digest is not refreshed. Because SHA-256 is unkeyed, an editor who can change both a record and its digest can recompute a consistent pair. A detached signature can establish which key endorsed a specific, exact subject, but signer authorization and the scientific validity of the subject remain separate questions.

This design proposes a small typed attestation over the already-computed frozen envelope digest. It is not a replacement for the envelope, receipt, verifier, VVUQ process, or safety case.

## 2. Non-goals and invariants

The attestation verifier MUST NOT:

- treat signature validity as proof that a model is accurate, a simulation converged, or an invariant holds;
- upgrade `NOT_RUN`, `SKIPPED`, `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`, `UNRESOLVED`, or `OUT_OF_DOMAIN` to `PASS`;
- grant chemical-process authority from a key authorized only for fabrication manifests;
- silently treat two differently named implementations as independent if they share a relevant upstream dependency;
- accept the caller's claim of "current trust" without evidence of trust-snapshot validity and freshness;
- interpret an attester-supplied timestamp as a trusted timestamp;
- claim in-toto, SLSA, RFC 8785/JCS, or other standards conformance unless the corresponding normative requirements are implemented and tested.

Keep these independent outcomes separate: envelope integrity, statement binding, cryptographic signature, signer authorization, replay/freshness policy, evidence validity, empirical validation, and decision qualification.

## 3. Existing primitives and domain boundary

The repository already has useful building blocks:

- `crates/domains/symthaea-fabrication-kernel/src/attestation.rs`: detached signature-provider interfaces, envelope shape, signature bounds, and trust-aware verification patterns.
- `crates/domains/symthaea-fabrication-kernel/src/trust.rs`: trust snapshots, purpose-bound key usage, validity windows, lifecycle state, and sequence/freshness support.
- `crates/domains/symthaea-fabrication-kernel/src/rotation.rs`: governed trust-snapshot transitions and monotonic sequence checks.
- `crates/core/symthaea-observation-attestation/src/lib.rs`: an example of typed stage-by-stage reports and verifier/policy/environment fingerprints.

Those modules are design references, not interchangeable subject types. The fabrication attestation binds `FabricationManifest` and its usage policy; the observation adapter verifies its own receipt/envelope format. Do not claim chemical-process authority by passing one of those objects through an existing entrypoint with the wrong purpose.

Prefer reuse of signer/verifier abstractions and trust snapshot rules. Add only narrow, domain-appropriate typed glue. Do not create a second key registry or cryptographic implementation.

## 4. Subject and claim model

The subject is the exact envelope generated by the v1.3.0 registry validator. At minimum, the attestation statement binds:

- attestation schema identifier and version;
- registry `schema_id` and `schema_version`;
- exact candidate `id`;
- 32 raw bytes decoded from `frozen_evidence_envelope_sha256`;
- a fixed, versioned purpose identifier;
- signer algorithm identifier and canonical key ID;
- an attester-declared issue time, explicitly marked as an untrusted assertion unless an independent trusted timestamp mechanism is used;
- an optional policy digest identifying the intended endorsement policy, not replacing the verifier's locally trusted policy.

The verifier must first recompute and validate the envelope digest from the supplied registry and candidate. It then checks that the statement's registry schema identity, candidate ID, and subject digest match those exact values. Only then should it evaluate the cryptographic signature and signer authority. No key or policy field supplied by the candidate envelope is automatically trusted.

Suggested typed names (final names remain subject to repository conventions):

- `ChemicalProcessEvidenceAttestationV1`
- `ChemicalProcessEvidenceStatementV1`
- dedicated trust usage such as `KeyUsage::ChemicalProcessEvidence`
- `ChemicalProcessAttestationPolicy`
- `ChemicalProcessAttestationVerificationReport`

Do not reuse `KeyUsage::FabricationManifest`. A new usage requires review of all exhaustive matches, serialization compatibility and trust-snapshot migration policy.

## 5. Canonical signed payload

The signature MUST cover domain-separated, uniquely encoded bytes—not the raw JSON wrapper, whose member ordering and serialization policy may vary.

Initial proposal: a versioned binary encoding with a fixed field order, explicit length-prefixing for strings and variable-length byte fields, explicit integer encoding, and fixed tags for enumerations. Every verifier implementation must use the same test vectors. Do not include the signature byte array inside its own signed payload.

Proposed domain separator:

```text
symthaea:chemical-process-evidence-attestation:v1\0
```

The signed payload must include the attestation schema/version, registry schema ID/version, candidate ID, decoded envelope digest, fixed purpose URI, signer algorithm tag, canonical signer key ID, and any declared endorsement-policy digest. The payload encoder must reject invalid UTF-8, empty or non-canonical IDs, unsupported versions/algorithms, malformed digest lengths, and oversize fields before signing or verification.

The JSON document is only a transport/display representation. A verifier reconstructs the canonical signed bytes from validated typed fields, and signatures are verified over those bytes. If an established canonical encoding is adopted instead, its exact version and conformance vectors must be pinned before implementation; do not invent an informal mix of JSON canonicalization rules.

## 6. Authorization and lifecycle policy

A verifier takes its expected purpose, local policy, an approved trust snapshot, a signature verifier, and an explicit evaluation time. It must not let the attestation choose its own acceptable policy.

For the dedicated evidence usage, fail closed when any required condition fails:

- trust snapshot schema or invariants are invalid;
- trust snapshot is stale or expired at evaluation time;
- snapshot sequence is behind the verifier's durable checkpoint;
- the same sequence has a different digest from the accepted checkpoint;
- key identity is unknown or algorithm/key identity is ambiguous;
- key is not yet valid, expired, retired, revoked, or lacks the dedicated evidence usage;
- signer is outside the explicit allowed-key policy, if that policy is configured;
- signature provider rejects the signature, reports false, or errors;
- required quorum/algorithm policy is not met;
- statement's purpose, schema, candidate ID or envelope digest does not match the verifier's expected subject.

A snapshot can only be called monotonic relative to a remembered checkpoint. A stateless verifier must state that it has no local rollback history, or consume an externally trusted checkpoint. Never overstate this property.

## 7. Replay semantics: durable evidence vs. online authorization

These are two different products and must not be conflated.

**Durable evidence attestation.** The same immutable statement/signature may be checked repeatedly and by multiple auditors. This is not itself a replay vulnerability. The signature cannot be moved to a different case or envelope because the subject digest and identity are signed.

**Online action authorization.** If a signature grants permission for a one-time or time-bounded action, a separate authorization request must bind a verifier-generated challenge/nonce, audience, action, and expiry. The receiver tracks consumed nonces. A durable evidence attestation must never double as a bearer capability for a new action.

Historical signature verification and current authorization are also distinct. A verifier may report that a signature is mathematically valid while current policy rejects the signer due to a later revocation or lifecycle change. If compromise timing is uncertain, the result must remain unresolved or follow an explicitly reviewed incident policy; do not infer harmlessness from the signature's claimed issue time.

## 8. Structured verification report

Return per-stage results rather than one confidence score. Suggested stages:

1. **Envelope integrity:** expected envelope digest recomputed and matches.
2. **Subject binding:** candidate ID, registry schema and subject digest match.
3. **Statement/schema:** supported schema, purpose and encoding.
4. **Signature:** cryptographic verification result.
5. **Signer lifecycle:** key resolved, purpose authorized, valid and not revoked.
6. **Trust freshness:** snapshot digest, sequence, freshness, and checkpoint status.
7. **Policy:** verifier policy ID/digest and quorum requirements.
8. **Evidence status:** original scientific benchmark state copied without promotion.
9. **Decision boundary:** claim ceiling, validity domain, exclusions and validation level carried through from evidence; no added credibility implied by the signature.

Record verifier implementation/version, policy digest, trust-snapshot digest, evaluation time, subject envelope digest, resolved signer identity, and every failed or not-evaluated stage. Errors must remain specific enough to distinguish invalid signature, unresolved signer, stale trust, wrong purpose and scientific status.

## 9. Adversarial conformance matrix

These are acceptance targets, not current results. Each control must exercise the actual exported verification entrypoint, and fixtures must prove setup reaches the intended validation stage.

| Challenge | Expected outcome | Target |
|---|---|---:|
| Valid eligible signature over exact envelope | Authenticated endorsement; scientific status unchanged | 100% expected accepts |
| Single-bit change to signed payload or signature | Reject | 100% detection |
| Envelope digest changed but digest not recomputed | Envelope integrity failure | 100% detection |
| Envelope and digest both changed, old signature retained | Signature/binding failure | 100% detection |
| Valid signature transplanted to another candidate/schema/purpose | Reject | 100% detection |
| Wrong or disallowed signer purpose | Reject | 100% detection |
| Unknown, not-yet-valid, expired, retired or revoked signer | Reject under current policy | 100% detection |
| Stale trust snapshot / sequence rollback / same-sequence digest collision | Reject or explicitly unresolved; never silently trust | 100% detection |
| Duplicate signer, malformed ID/digest, unsupported schema/algorithm, oversized fields | Reject | 100% detection |
| Forged self-declared signed-at time | Must not establish trusted time | 100% detection of unsupported claim |
| Valid historic statement evaluated after revocation | Report cryptographic validity separately from current authorization | 100% correct classification |
| Durable evidence re-checked by another auditor | Accept if all integrity and policy checks pass | 100% expected accepts |
| One-time action reuses a consumed nonce | Reject online action authorization | 100% detection |
| Signed evidence says benchmark `NOT_RUN` | Endorsement may validate; benchmark remains `NOT_RUN` | 0 promotions |
| Invalid/unknown challenge hidden or omitted from the denominator | Corpus-integrity failure | 0 silent omissions |

Hard targets: zero false accepts in the seeded invalid corpus; zero invalid/not-run statuses promoted to PASS; complete report accounting; and explicit denominators. Add property-based mutations only after the deterministic fixture corpus has stable, reviewable cases.

## 10. Research and interoperability anchors

These sources inform the design, but do not imply conformance.

- [in-toto Attestation Framework v1.2 — overview](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/blob/main/spec/v1/README.md) separates authentication/envelope, typed subject statement, predicate, and bundle. Its [Statement layer](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/blob/main/spec/v1/statement.md) binds a claim to immutable subject digests and a predicate type. Symthaea may borrow that separation, but must implement and test the actual format before claiming compatibility.
- [SLSA provenance v1](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/provenance) emphasizes origin, build definition, resolved dependencies and outputs. That is useful for provenance completeness and explicit trust boundaries, but software-build provenance alone does not establish scientific model credibility.
- [NIST, *On Methods of Uncertainty Quantification in Computational Modeling and Simulation* (2026-10-05)](https://www.nist.gov/publications/methods-uncertainty-quantification-computational-modeling-and-simulation-harmonizing) discusses harmonizing ASME VVUQ and metrology and scaling credibility effort with model influence and decision consequence.
- [ASME V&V 20](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/standard-for-verification-and-validation-in-computational-fluid-dynamics-and-heat-transfer) concerns quantified accuracy at specified validation points; agreement at matched points should not silently license extrapolation to other conditions.

## 11. Implementation sequence

1. Confirm and document the signed-field schema, dedicated trust purpose and canonical bytes before writing the crypto adapter.
2. Add deterministic positive/negative fixtures plus byte-level golden vectors.
3. Compose existing signature/verifier traits and trust snapshot APIs without borrowing fabrication-manifest authorization.
4. Add stage-by-stage reports and explicit current-vs-historic authorization semantics.
5. Add challenge/nonce support only if an online action authorization use case actually exists; keep it separate from durable attestations.
6. Run focused Rust tests on an exact commit; then run the full required workflows. Skipped, queued, unavailable and unrun are not PASS.
7. Keep all five existing chemical-process candidates `NOT_RUN` until their separate solver execution and scientific evidence gates are satisfied.

## 12. Follow-on frontier after attestation

Once authenticated envelope verification is implemented and tested, the highest-value next step is a small **executable claim-conformance corpus** shared across engineering domains. Each fixture should declare: claim, subject digest, assumptions/context, required evidence level, hard invariants, seeded falsifiers, dependency-lineage controls, expected verifier stages, and expected claim ceiling. This allows chemical-process, structural, thermal and grid-physics claims to share a verification harness without pretending their physical equations or validity domains are interchangeable.
