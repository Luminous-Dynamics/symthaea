# Collective and Institutional Ontology

## Purpose

This document defines a frame-conditional way for Symthaea to reason about humans, institutions, and nation-states without either:

1. reducing every collective to a mere label over individuals, or
2. reifying a collective abstraction as if it were a simple biological organism.

The goal is epistemic hygiene: preserve the causal usefulness of collective-level concepts while keeping their constitutive substrate and maintenance conditions visible.

## Ontological layers

A Symthaea model may distinguish:

- **Physical** — bodies, terrain, buildings, machines, energy, matter.
- **Biological** — organisms and biological processes.
- **Psychological** — perception, memory, intention, emotion, belief.
- **Relational** — interactions, dependencies, networks, roles.
- **Social** — shared practices, norms, conventions, identities.
- **Institutional** — rule-governed structures with recognized roles, procedures, and powers.
- **Collective-agent** — a distributed system whose coordinated components can produce actions attributable to the collective under an explicit frame.
- **Narrative/representational** — descriptions, symbols, stories, maps, categories, and models used to represent other layers.
- **Normative** — permissions, obligations, rights, prohibitions, commitments, and other rule-dependent statuses.

These are modeling layers, not a claim that reality is literally partitioned into independent strata.

## Maintained ontology

A **MaintainedOntology** is an entity or category whose persistence depends materially on ongoing social maintenance.

Maintenance may include:

- recognition or acceptance,
- repeated practices,
- records and documents,
- rules and procedures,
- role occupancy,
- enforcement or sanction,
- physical/digital infrastructure,
- resource flows,
- collective memory,
- external recognition.

The maintenance relation must remain explicit. A frame should not infer that an institution exists independently of every mechanism that reproduces it.

## Collective agent

A **CollectiveAgent** is a frame-qualified attribution of agency to a distributed system.

Minimum decomposition:

- members/components,
- membership conditions,
- roles,
- decision procedure,
- communication channels,
- authorization rules,
- resources,
- action surface,
- persistence conditions,
- internal disagreement,
- external constraints,
- attribution rule.

The attribution rule matters. "The organization decided X" may refer to a board vote, an executive order, a delegated officer, a legal instrument, or a coordinated behavior emerging without a single matching intention.

## Nation-state as a test case

A nation-state should be modeled as a **distributed institutional/collective system**, not as a single undifferentiated agent.

A frame may include:

- population and membership rules,
- territorial jurisdiction,
- constitutional/legal rules,
- executive institutions,
- legislative institutions,
- judicial institutions,
- administrative/bureaucratic systems,
- public finances,
- infrastructure,
- security and defense institutions,
- shared symbols and historical memory,
- political organizations,
- international recognition and treaty relations,
- informal norms and social practices.

Different claims about "the state" can therefore point to different causal substrates.

For example:

> "The state changed policy."

should be decomposed into:

**referent → institution/role → authorization path → evidence → action → affected systems → observed outcome**

The model should preserve disagreement between components rather than replacing it with a single collective belief.

## Narrative agent

A **NarrativeAgent** is an abstraction that is linguistically treated as an actor even when its causal substrate is distributed.

Examples include phrases such as:

- "the country decided",
- "the economy reacted",
- "society wants",
- "the market believes",
- "history demanded".

These statements are not automatically false. They are compressed causal descriptions. The epistemic hazard occurs when the compression hides which components actually generated the observed action.

Symthaea should therefore support both:

- **compressed attribution** for useful high-level reasoning, and
- **decompressed attribution** for causal scrutiny.

## Frame-relative truth

A claim about a collective is always evaluated relative to a frame containing at least:

- evidence boundary,
- ontology,
- causal model,
- exclusions,
- known blind spots,
- attribution rule.

Thus:

> Claim C is supported by evidence E under frame F.

is preferable to:

> Claim C is simply true with confidence P.

A different frame may preserve the original claim while changing its scope, causal interpretation, or referent.

## Adversarial frame tests

Future deterministic tests should include:

1. **Same referent, different ontology**
   - collective-agent frame
   - narrative-label frame
   - verify that provenance remains distinct.

2. **Same ontology, different causal model**
   - institutional decision model
   - distributed emergent-behavior model
   - verify that conclusions are not silently conflated.

3. **Same conclusion, expanded frame**
   - frame v1 excludes an informal institution
   - frame v2 makes that institution explicit
   - verify that the historical v1 conclusion remains preserved but becomes scope-qualified.

4. **Internal disagreement**
   - component institutions disagree
   - verify that collective attribution does not erase disagreement.

5. **Maintenance failure**
   - remove recognition, procedures, records, or role occupancy
   - verify that the model can represent institutional degradation without pretending the entity simply "vanished" at a single instant.

6. **Reification trap**
   - input uses a narrative-agent phrase
   - require decomposition before causal intervention is attributed to the abstraction itself.

## Design rule

Symthaea should not be trained to answer:

> "Are nation-states real?"

with a global yes/no.

The more useful question is:

> "In what sense does this entity exist, what maintains it, what can it causally do under this frame, and what lower-level or alternative frames would change the attribution?"

This preserves ontological humility without sacrificing useful abstraction.
