# Partner brief: coupled structural and electrochemical qualification of load-bearing ZIHC

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 13 November 2026  
**Status:** research collaboration proposal. No partner, facility access, specimen, safety approval, procurement or physical result is claimed.

## Purpose

Develop a reproducible laboratory method to determine whether an integrated load-bearing zinc-ion hybrid supercapacitor can replace both a defined structural member and a separately mounted energy-storage component without losing either function under coupled mechanical/electrochemical ageing.

The first campaign should use a non-flight, non-occupied, low-voltage coupon or beam-like article. It does not establish suitability for an aircraft, vehicle chassis, building, pressure vessel or other safety-critical structure.

## Research comparator

Fu et al., *Advanced Functional Materials*, first published 19 June 2026:
“All 3D Printed Load-bearing Zn-ion Hybrid Supercapacitors”
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.76654

The publisher abstract describes:
- core–shell continuous-carbon-fibre prepreg filaments used to print a framework that includes electrodes and electrolyte matrix;
- post-print supercritical foaming and gelation to activate electrochemical function;
- supercritical-fluid-assisted laser-induced graphitization forming hierarchical porous graphene cathodes;
- 2.4× areal capacitance relative to conventional laser-induced-graphitization electrodes;
- biphasic PP/PVA semi-solid electrolyte with interconnected ion pathways and reported ionic conductivity increasing from 0.5 to 4.2 mS cm⁻¹.

The abstract does not establish full-device gravimetric/volumetric energy density, total component mass, structural design allowables, stiffness/strength/fatigue, coupled mechanical/electrochemical degradation or independent-lab reproducibility. The publisher lists supporting information and says underlying data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. Obtain and review these materials and any data rights before exact reproduction.

## Central comparison: the same assembly boundary

Compare:
1. A conventional structural member plus separately mounted commercial storage device.
2. An equivalent structural-only member.
3. A non-load-bearing electrochemical device using the same or closest feasible device chemistry/process.
4. An integrated load-bearing electrochemical storage article.

Structural credit is allowed only if the integrated article demonstrably replaces a specified structural function and meets the same load, stiffness, strength, fatigue, inspection, repair and environmental requirements. Do not remove the structural host mass from an energy-density denominator unless a matched installed assembly proves genuine substitution.

## Proposed work packages

### WP0 — source, feasibility and rights
- Review the article and supporting information.
- Determine which source conditions can be reconstructed and which remain unavailable.
- Confirm material/process capabilities, approved test methods, staff competence and facility safety authority.
- Agree background/foreground IP, raw-data access, reuse, publication, confidentiality and retention of negative results before physical work.

### WP1 — matched single-function baselines
- Establish structural-only mechanical baselines.
- Establish electrochemical-only device baselines.
- Use the same material/process lots and condition history wherever feasible.
- Freeze specimen identities, component boundaries, fixtures, calibrations and test protocols.

### WP2 — integrated coupon comparison
The participating laboratory defines the materials, geometry, loads, voltage/current limits and safe operating procedures. Candidate comparisons may include:
- mechanical-only fatigue before electrochemical measurement;
- electrochemical ageing before mechanical fatigue;
- matched sequence-reversed articles;
- combined mechanical/electrochemical duty only if separately reviewed and instrumented.

Mechanical-first and electrochemical-first effects must remain separate. Do not use an unmeasured simulation to stand in for a combined physical test.

### WP3 — metrology
Mechanical measurements, as appropriate to the article:
- mass, geometry and dimensional stability;
- tensile/flexural/shear/compression response;
- stiffness, strength, strain-to-failure and buckling;
- interlaminar strength, adhesion, delamination and crack growth;
- fatigue and residual strength/stiffness;
- impact tolerance and post-impact inspectability;
- environmental conditioning and moisture/temperature effects.

Electrochemical measurements:
- usable energy and power over the declared voltage window;
- complete-device mass/volume and component inclusion/exclusion;
- areal, gravimetric and volumetric metrics at explicit boundaries;
- ESR/EIS and evolution under load and cycling;
- capacitance, energy and power retention;
- coulombic/energy efficiency, leakage and self-discharge;
- temperature behavior, isolation integrity and failure observations.

Coupled measurements:
- electrochemical performance versus mechanical fatigue fraction and load state;
- mechanical properties versus electrochemical ageing;
- strain-dependent resistance/impedance where measurable;
- damage onset and correlated capacity/ESR changes;
- mechanical and electrical remaining-life states reported separately.

### WP4 — manufacturing and repair
Record independent material lots, print/process/toolpath revisions, post-processing, device identities, process deviations, yield, scrap/rework, process energy, inspection effort, repairability and end-of-life route.

A prototype that works once but cannot be inspected, repaired or reproduced does not establish manufacturing feasibility.

## Primary decision metrics

- Does the integrated article meet the same declared structural function?
- Does it deliver useful measured electrical energy/power at a complete-device boundary?
- Does each function persist through the coupled life exposure?
- Does the installed assembly become lighter, smaller or less costly than the matched conventional structure-plus-storage assembly?
- Are inspection, repair, replacement and recycling still practical?
- Is the process repeatable across independent lots?

Do not combine stiffness, strength, joules, watts, cycle life, cost and safety into one weighted score that can hide a hard failure.

## Initial research gates

**G0 — source/metrology feasibility:** source and rights review, matched baseline definition and facility safety review.

**G1 — single-function baselines:** calibrated mechanical and electrochemical measurements on traceable specimens.

**G2 — coupled proof of function:** the same article meets predeclared structural requirements and provides measured usable storage.

**G3 — coupled durability:** prospectively evaluate at least 90% retention of baseline mechanical stiffness and 90% of baseline usable energy after the declared exposure, while still meeting absolute design minima. These are exploratory internal targets, not standards; the load history and electrochemical duty must be application-derived and frozen before testing.

**G4 — assembly substitution:** demonstrate a lower installed mass/volume or cost against a true structure-plus-storage reference, at equivalent mechanical service, energy/power, lifetime, inspection and repair requirements.

**G5 — independent replication:** a separate facility reproduces both functions and their coupled degradation trend with unique specimens; sample size follows pilot variance and power analysis.

## Safety and authority

The host laboratory's competent personnel must approve all materials, fabrication, electrical limits, mechanical fixtures, containment, emergency response, waste handling and destructive testing. No autonomous system authorizes physical execution.

This brief authorizes no procurement, synthesis, cell assembly, charging, mechanical loading, destructive testing, certification or field deployment.
