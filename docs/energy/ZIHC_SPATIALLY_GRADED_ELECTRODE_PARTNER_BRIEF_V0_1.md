# Partner brief: spatially programmed zinc-ion hybrid capacitor electrodes

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 13 November 2026  
**Status:** collaboration proposal; no partner, facility access, funding, materials, safety approval or physical results are claimed.

## Purpose

Establish a partner-led, reproducible laboratory campaign to evaluate whether spatially programmed electrode architecture can improve high-loading zinc-ion hybrid supercapacitor (ZIHSC) performance and manufacturing robustness.

The research hypothesis is that a spatially varying distribution of lattice geometry, pore connectivity, electronic conductivity, active-site density and mechanical compliance can reduce transport polarization and reaction nonuniformity better than a uniform electrode or a fixed, manually tuned gradient.

The programme is intended to produce a measured, reproducible device comparison—not merely a simulation, literature review or synthetic software benchmark.

## Why now: two distinct research comparators

### Comparator A — high-loading carbon/Zn pouch cell (July 2026)

Wu et al., *Advanced Science*, DOI 10.1002/advs.76823, report a carbon cathode at 10 mg cm^-2, N/P ratio approximately 16.4, 38.3 mAh g^-1 at 10 A g^-1 after 3,000 cycles, and 2.82 Wh kg^-1 using a reported 0.746 g total-device denominator. Their approach combines CNT conductive networks with low-tortuosity, long-range pore channels.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.76823

### Comparator B — dual-gradient gel electrode (October 2026)

Meng et al., *Advanced Functional Materials*, DOI 10.1002/adfm.78804, report a stepwise multi-ink 3D-printed dual-gradient MXene/polypyrrole@CNT gel electrode. The abstract reports 42.7 mg cm^-2 loading, 6.2 F cm^-2 areal capacitance, 0.86 mWh cm^-2 areal energy, 71.1% capacitance retention across a 40-fold current-density increase and 98.8% retention after 15,000 cycles.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.78804

**These are different chemistries, architectures, form factors and metric boundaries.** They are not one leaderboard. The October abstract does not establish complete-device Wh kg^-1 / Wh L^-1, all inactive mass, packaging, production yield or independent-lab reproducibility. Obtain the full article and supplementary protocol before attempting strict numerical replication.

## Proposed work packages

### WP0 — protocol and rights feasibility

Before physical work:
- Obtain and review the October paper's supplementary information and any available raw data.
- Determine what can legally and practically be reproduced; record licensing, data rights and material-transfer constraints.
- Agree the target device format, safety authority, process capability, instrument access and responsibilities.
- Freeze a versioned measurement protocol and preregister the primary comparison before fabrication.

If source conditions or data required for exact reproduction are unavailable, label the paper as a literature comparator and report the gap. Do not fill missing values by assumption.

### WP1 — reference and process baseline

Under the host laboratory's approved SOPs, establish:
- a traceable uniform-electrode control;
- a repeatable device assembly and conditioning method;
- calibrated electrochemical measurement chain;
- geometry and mass/volume accounting;
- specimen, material-lot, process and fixture identity.

The laboratory selects the safe materials, formulation and fabrication procedures. This brief does not prescribe chemical recipes, synthesis parameters or equipment operation.

### WP2 — matched architecture comparison

Subject to laboratory feasibility, compare:
1. Uniform printed control.
2. Macroscopic lattice-gradient-only arm.
3. Intra-filament pore-gradient-only arm.
4. Dual-gradient reference.
5. Field-conditioned gradient designed from a frozen, pre-registered transport/reaction objective.

Keep chemistry, voltage window, loading, electrolyte, device geometry and test conditions matched unless the intervention explicitly changes one. Record every deviation as a separate arm.

Do not tune the field-conditioned architecture against held-out devices or lots.

### WP3 — structural and electrochemical metrology

Potential measurements, subject to facility capability:
- micro-CT for lattice geometry, pore structure and gradient fidelity;
- cross-sectional microscopy and composition mapping;
- porosity, tortuosity and wetting/electrolyte-uptake characterization;
- position-dependent electronic / ionic transport measurement or validated proxy;
- impedance spectroscopy and impedance evolution;
- galvanostatic charge/discharge, rate and pulse response;
- energy and coulombic efficiency, leakage and self-discharge;
- thermal response, mechanical integrity and failure analysis;
- raw data, calibration records, uncertainty and test-protocol identity.

A simulated current distribution is a model result, not a direct physical measurement. A micro-CT image establishes geometry only within the method's resolution and validation limits.

### WP4 — manufacturing and reproducibility

Record:
- independent material lots and fabrication batches;
- printer, nozzle, toolpath, ink and process revisions;
- dimensions, thickness, loading and component mass;
- print success, geometry conformance, yield, scrap and rework;
- post-processing time/energy and material utilization;
- device-to-device and batch-to-batch variation;
- failed, invalid, stopped and censored devices.

Use a pilot variance estimate and power analysis to select the confirmatory sample count. At least three fabrication lots are the initial repeatability entry gate, not a sufficient statistical sample size by themselves.

Cross-laboratory variance analysis requires at least three independent labs and at least three unique physical devices per laboratory under a common protocol as a minimum entry condition; perform a power analysis for the actual design.

## Primary outcomes

Keep separate:
- areal capacitance and areal energy;
- gravimetric and volumetric energy/power at an explicitly measured complete-device boundary;
- capacitance and energy retention over a declared current-density / power range;
- ESR / impedance and its evolution;
- efficiency, leakage and self-discharge;
- cycle count and cumulative energy throughput;
- mechanical/structural integrity;
- process yield and variability;
- accepted-good-device cost and material/process consumption.

Areal performance must not be converted to Wh kg^-1 or Wh L^-1 without a measured mass/volume boundary and a transparent calculation. Active-material metrics must never be described as complete-device performance.

## Luminous contribution

Subject to an agreed collaboration:
- benchmark design and matched-comparison protocol;
- evidence contracts for specimen, lot, process, fixture, calibration and raw-data lineage;
- immutable data manifests and source locators;
- preregistration, negative controls and independent analysis checks;
- model/data integration for spatial design hypotheses;

Symthaea supports candidate and experiment selection; Symtropy supports physical/device/system modeling; SAM supports independent evidence qualification. None substitutes for a qualified laboratory, physical measurements or independent product certification.

## Partner contribution requested

A potential partner would need to assess:
- qualified staff and approved electrochemical laboratory facilities;
- safe material handling, fabrication and cell assembly;
- suitable 3D-printing or electrode-architecture capability;
- micro-CT and microscopy access, or a qualified external provider;
- calibrated cyclers, impedance spectroscopy and environmental testing;
- quality, waste, emergency and failure-response procedures;
- agreed raw-data delivery and instrument metadata.

This is a capability request, not a claim that any named institution has agreed to provide these services.

## Data, IP and publication principles

Agree terms in writing before physical work:
1. Identify each party's background IP and pre-existing tools.
2. Define ownership and permitted use of foreground inventions, formulations, process improvements, models and device designs.
3. Agree whether raw measurements, derived data, images and metadata may be shared, archived and independently reanalysed.
4. Define publication review, attribution, embargo and confidentiality without suppressing negative results.
5. Confirm rights to use third-party papers, datasets, images, materials and proprietary equipment outputs.
6. Retain original failed/invalid observations and complete sample denominators.
7. No party receives authority to represent a research prototype as certified, safe for deployment or commercially qualified without the relevant independent process.

## Safety, authority and claim boundaries

All physical experiments, material procurement, synthesis, assembly, charging, testing and waste handling remain under the partner facility's competent-person review, approved SOPs and institutional safety controls. No autonomous agent authorizes physical execution.

This brief authorizes no purchasing, equipment actuation, hazardous-material procedure, high-current testing, product release or field deployment.

A software audit or design proposal proves no electrochemical performance. Performance, reproducibility, manufacturability, safety, certification and commercial readiness remain separate evidence gates.

| Gate | Exit evidence |

| G0 — feasibility | Facility capability, rights, safety authority and source-protocol availability reviewed |
