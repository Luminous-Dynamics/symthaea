# Atelier Microfactory — Site, Facility and Partner Survey Template v0.1

**Use:** data collection for Phase 0 partner / Phase 1 prototype microfactory feasibility  
**Status:** questionnaire and evidence checklist only; not a site approval or professional design brief  
**Parent:** ATELIER-MICROFACTORY-001 #12288

## 1. How to use this template

Complete one record per candidate property, existing workshop, CMT partner, machinery partner or utility configuration.

Keep:
- measured/observed facts;
- owner/supplier declarations;
- document claims;
- estimates;
- assumptions;
- unknowns

as separate evidence classes. Do not turn an unanswered field into a favorable default.

Record the source, date, exact asset/property, revision, measurement method, units and person/organization providing each answer. Retain source documents with permission. Do not publish sensitive commercial terms or personal data in public fixtures.

## 3. Planning, zoning and legal due diligence

Qualified local professionals/authorities must confirm applicability. Record, do not infer:
- permitted land use / zoning and any consent-use requirements;
- current occupancy/use approvals;
- existing building plans and as-built discrepancies;
- fire/life-safety documentation and inspection status;
- occupational health and safety obligations for the exact processes;
- environmental permits/consents for any proposed process or discharge;
- electrical installation certificates and inspection records;
- signage, traffic/loading/parking restrictions;
- lease covenants, operating-hour or noise restrictions;
- neighboring uses and community impacts;
- any outstanding notices, defects or nonconformities.

A listing, landlord statement, BIM model or historic approval is not proof that the proposed textile use is authorized.

## 4. Building and site physical survey

Record actual surveyed values, not inferred values:
- gross and net usable floor area by room/zone;
- floor levels, columns, walls, doors, openings and clear widths;
- clear height and overhead obstructions;
- floor condition, levelness, moisture and known load rating;
- loading dock/receiving access and delivery-vehicle envelope;
- material-roll handling path and storage envelope;
- maintenance access and machine replacement route;
- roof/wall condition, leaks and drainage;
- daylight/glare and ability to control task lighting;
- thermal conditions and ventilation observations;
- noise/vibration context;
- staff access, toilets, changing and rest facilities;
- accessibility and circulation;
- fire exits, routes, access and assembly points as assessed by qualified parties;
- future expansion and demountable-partition options.

Floor loading, structural capacity, fire compartmentation, egress and occupancy are professional-review items. Do not make them pass/fail from a visual walkthrough.

## 5. Electricity and backup

For each proposed machine and facility load, capture:
- make/model and exact machine configuration;
- nameplate electrical data and manual reference;
- voltage, phase, frequency, rated current/power and duty assumptions;
- inrush/start behaviour where relevant;
- plug/connection type and protection requirements;
- actual metered operating profile when available;
- peak/demand profile and diversity assumptions;
- existing board/installation capacity and inspection record;
- utility tariff and demand charges;
- outage history and restoration conditions supplied by the operator;
- generator/UPS/PV/battery details, if present;
- critical vs interruptible loads;
- power-quality evidence, earthing/bonding and surge protection evidence;
- electrical installer/engineer and inspection authority.

Do not open energized equipment or perform electrical measurements without appropriately qualified personnel and approved procedures. Do not size the facility from the sum of nameplate kW alone.

## 6. Water, drainage and effluent

Phase 1 is intended to be a dry textile-assembly operation. Record:
- potable/domestic water service and metering;
- sanitary and staff welfare capacity;
- any actual process-water demand and point of use;
- water quality requirements if any process needs them;
- drainage, stormwater and sewer connection;
- discharge consent/limits and available test/monitoring data if process effluent exists;
- spill containment and chemical storage requirements if later process-specific;
- outage/continuity information;
- water tariff.

Do not add dyeing, washing, scouring, bleaching, wet-finishing, coating or wet recycling to the Phase 1 scope without a separate process/water/environmental design and authorization review. Do not claim water savings when the proposed process does not use process water.

## 7. Ventilation, thermal, dust and fire

For each process and machine:
- people and machine heat loads;
- fresh-air/ventilation basis;
- task-specific local extraction needs;
- pressing/steam/thermal equipment and required separation;
- fabric lint/dust generation and collection design;
- filter/duct maintenance access;
- noise and vibration;
- combustible storage and waste handling;
- fire detection/protection, compartmentation and emergency routes;
- any adhesives, printing, solvents, chemicals or wet processes explicitly proposed.

The exact fire/dust/ventilation design must be reviewed by competent professionals for the actual facility and process. A generic HVAC system or portable vacuum does not constitute qualified local extraction or fire protection.

## 9. Equipment inventory and capability survey

For every candidate machine/tool:
- asset ID, make, model, serial (private record), manufacture date;
- controller/firmware and manual revision;
- process family and operation capability;
- supported material and thickness/stack-up envelope;
- needle/foot/tooling/fixture requirements;
- current configuration and options;
- measured or evidenced cycle/setup times;
- maintenance and service history;
- calibration status and due date where applicable;
- known faults/defects and downtime;
- safety guards, needle protection, interlocks and emergency stop condition;
- operator skills and training requirement;
- spares availability and service provider;
- supported data signals and export;
- warranty/insurance implications of retrofit;
- photographs/documents only with owner permission.

Manufacturer marketing claims, machine nameplates and observed operation are separate evidence classes. A machine declaration is not production qualification for Atelier's material/recipe/output requirements.

## 10. Workforce, skills and work design

Record role/skill coverage rather than personal ranking:
- patternmaking and grading;
- fabric inspection and conditioning;
- cutting/spreading;
- lockstitch, overlock, coverstitch, buttonhole, bartack;
- pressing/finishing;
- quality/metrology;
- repair/alteration;
- machine maintenance;
- production planning and industrial engineering;
- training and competency assessment.

For each workstation assess:
- reach, height, visibility and adjustability;
- seated/standing options;
- task lighting;
- repetitive motions and force;
- handling loads and material roll movement;
- heat, noise, dust and exposure;
- breaks, pace and rotation;
- worker participation and stop/report mechanisms.

Do not optimize the line by reducing breaks or externalizing ergonomic risk.

## 11. Production and commercial data

Where partners are willing to share, request a declared recent sample window and distinguish actual from forecast:
- product families and order mix;
- work content and operation time distributions;
- changeover and setup;
- queue and WIP;
- first-pass yield and defect categories;
- rework/scrap and returns;
- machine availability and stoppage causes;
- labor skill coverage;
- accepted output per shift;
- utility consumption boundaries;
- maintenance/calibration burden;
- price/quote basis and inclusions;
- MOQ, sample fee, setup fee, lead time and payment terms;
- confidentiality, data retention and audit rights.

No isolated throughput figure is comparable without product complexity, staffing, shifts, machine count, quality and downtime context.

## 12. Partner agreement questions

For a CMT/patternmaking/knitting/finishing partner, confirm in writing:
- one-off prototype/toile acceptance;
- sample and correction-round pricing;
- exact machine/operation capability;
- pattern/native file ownership and export formats;
- confidentiality and IP treatment;
- fabric/material responsibility and lot traceability;
- quality criteria and inspection ownership;
- who pays for rework and rejected samples;
- production MOQ and capacity reservations;
- staff skill/training and supervision;
- machine/utility downtime and business continuity;
- repair and returns capability;
- subcontracting and material substitution rules;
- data export and record retention;
- termination and work-in-progress handoff.

## 13. Evidence and disposition

For each answer attach:
- evidence ID;
- subject ID (site, facility, machine, utility, process or partner);
- evidence class;
- source/provider;
- date and revision;
- unit and method;
- scope/coverage;
- uncertainty/limitations;
- reviewer;
- disposition: accepted for this bounded purpose / conditional / unknown / rejected.

Unknowns remain visible. A site visit, quote, machine demo, digital layout or supplier declaration is not a commissioning, safety, environmental or production qualification.

## 14. Decision output

The survey should not produce one overall attractiveness score. Produce:
- hard blockers and unresolved legal/professional reviews;
- process capabilities that are evidenced, conditional or unknown;
- utility and building constraints;
- capital/lease/fit-out ranges with assumptions;
- supplier/partner concentration and resilience;
- product families/routes supported;
- required upgrades and their separate qualification;
- make/buy/partner alternatives;
- risks and data still needed;
- next reversible investigation step.

No lease, purchase, fit-out or machinery procurement decision should rely on this template alone.
