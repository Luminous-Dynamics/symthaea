# Cover Letter: AJOB Neuroscience

Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration a Target Article entitled "A Quantitative Framework for Consciousness-Gated Ethics in Neural Organoid Research."

Neural organoid complexity is advancing faster than the ethical frameworks governing their use. Cortical organoids now exhibit oscillatory waves resembling preterm neonatal EEG (Trujillo et al. 2019), yet no regulatory body provides quantitative criteria for when organoid development warrants ethical intervention. Recent reporting from STAT News (November 2025) and NPR (January 2026) confirms that effectively no legal limits exist on neural organoid use, and organoid scientists themselves recognize the regulatory gap.

This paper presents the first computational framework with quantitative consciousness thresholds gating ethical requirements for neural organoid research. We map seven measurable consciousness indicators---spontaneous activity, oscillatory patterns, synchronized bursting, integrated information (Phi), evoked responses, sleep-wake cycles, and learning capacity---to five graduated ethics tiers with specific required actions at each level. The framework operates in precautionary mode by default, lowering all thresholds by 30% on the principle that false negatives (missing emerging consciousness) carry greater moral weight than false positives.

We validate the framework against published electrophysiology data from two independent laboratories (Trujillo et al. 2019; Sharf et al. 2022), present robustness analyses across 20 independent simulation seeds, and provide sensitivity analysis for all threshold parameters. The open-source Rust implementation (~800 lines of code) is available for immediate reproducible use.

We believe AJOB Neuroscience is the ideal venue for this work. The Open Peer Commentary format is particularly well suited for a framework that is offered explicitly as a starting point for community refinement---all thresholds are adjustable parameters, not moral facts, and commentary from ethicists, neuroscientists, and regulatory scholars would strengthen the framework substantially.

We confirm this manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration at another journal. No conflicts of interest exist.
