\begin{abstract}
We present the \emph{Ramanujan Protocol}, an automated system that observes
numerical sequences from mathematical engines, discovers closed-form formulas
via grammar-guided genetic programming, and formally verifies discovered
conjectures through bounded induction. The system operates as a closed loop:
\textbf{Observe}~$\to$~\textbf{Discover}~$\to$~\textbf{Prove}, with an
explicit self-correction mechanism that detects and retracts structurally
trivial findings.

We demonstrate the pipeline on four domains: (1)~the golden ratio
$\varphi$ rediscovered from Fibonacci ratios with test MSE~$8.4 \times 10^{-21}$;
(2)~the triangular number formula $T(n) = n(n+1)/2$ discovered and
\emph{formally proven} by bounded induction to $n = 1000$; (3)~the
conservation law $E = x^2 + v^2$ discovered from harmonic oscillator
trajectory data (variance~$6.4 \times 10^{-23}$); and (4)~the Bell--Stirling
identity $B(n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} S(n,k)$ verified exactly for $n \in [0, 15]$.

Critically, we report a \emph{negative result}: the system initially reported
a 99.9\% obstruction ratio in a Geometric Complexity Theory scan, then
autonomously detected that the finding was a mathematical tautology (the
Littlewood--Richardson size constraint forces same-weight triples to vanish
trivially). This self-correction validates the system's epistemic integrity
and distinguishes it from pattern-matching approaches that cannot distinguish
genuine discoveries from structural artifacts.

The system is implemented in Rust as part of the Symthaea cognitive
architecture (1.1M~LOC, 130+ tests in the mathematical subsystem), with the
conjecture engine comprising 900~LOC of grammar-guided symbolic regression,
5 domain-specific data collectors, and a formal verification bridge to the
Z3 SMT solver.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} automated conjecture, symbolic regression, formal verification,
genetic programming, self-correcting AI, epistemic integrity

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The automation of mathematical discovery has been a long-standing goal in
artificial intelligence, from the AM program~\citep{lenat1977} to modern
neural theorem provers~\citep{lample2022}. Recent large language models can
generate plausible-looking mathematical statements, but they cannot
\emph{verify} their outputs---hallucinated proofs are indistinguishable from
correct ones within the model's own reasoning~\citep{frieder2023}.

We identify three requirements for a trustworthy automated discovery system:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Grounded observation}: formulas must be discovered from
    computed data, not recalled from training corpora.
  \item \textbf{Formal verification}: numerical fit (low MSE) is necessary
    but insufficient; conjectures must be \emph{proven} by an independent
    verification engine.
  \item \textbf{Epistemic self-correction}: the system must detect and
    retract findings that are structurally trivial, even when they appear
    statistically significant.
\end{enumerate}

The \emph{Ramanujan Protocol} implements all three requirements as a closed
pipeline. We demonstrate it on integer sequences, combinatorial identities,
and conservation laws from dynamical systems, and we report a critical
self-correction that validates the architecture's integrity.

\section{Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The pipeline consists of four stages (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}):

\subsection{Stage 1: Observe}

Mathematical engines compute numerical sequences: partition counts $p(n)$,
Fibonacci ratios $F(n)/F(n{-}1)$, Kronecker coefficient bounds, ODE
trajectories, etc. Each sequence is stored as a vector of $(x, y)$ pairs
with domain metadata.

\subsection{Stage 2: Discover (Symbolic Regression)}

A grammar-guided genetic programming engine searches for closed-form
expressions that fit the observed data. The expression grammar includes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Terminals}: variables ($n$, $k$), constants
    ($0, 1, 2, 3, 4, \frac{1}{2}, \pi, e, \varphi, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},
    \frac{2}{3}$)
  \item \textbf{Binary operators}: $+, -, \times, \div, \hat{}$ (power)
  \item \textbf{Unary functions}: $\sqrt{\cdot}, \ln, \exp, \sin, \cos,
    |\cdot|, \lfloor\cdot\rfloor$
  \item \textbf{Summation}: $\sum_{k=0}^{n} f(k)$ (enables identity
    discovery)
\end{itemize}

Fitness combines mean squared error with an Occam penalty:
\begin{equation}
  \text{fitness}(f) = \text{MSE}(f) + \lambda \cdot |f|
  \label{eq:fitness}
\end{equation}
where $|f|$ is the AST node count and $\lambda = 0.001$ by default. This
prevents overfitting with high-degree polynomials while allowing genuinely
complex formulas (e.g., $\exp(\pi\sqrt{2n/3})$) when the data demands it.

Expressions producing NaN for more than 50\% of data points are assigned
$\text{fitness} = \infty$, preventing degenerate formulas (e.g.,
$n^{1/0}$) from exploiting single-point agreement.

\subsection{Stage 3: Verify}

Verification proceeds in three levels of increasing rigor:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Numerical}: evaluate the formula on held-out test data
    (last 20\% of the sequence). Conjectures whose test MSE exceeds
    $10\times$ the training MSE are \emph{refuted}.
  \item \textbf{Bounded induction}: for integer sequences, verify that
    $f(n) - f(n{-}1) = \Delta(n)$ (where $\Delta$ is the first difference
    of the observed data) for all $n \in [1, N]$ with $N = 1000$. This is
    equivalent to bounded quantifier elimination in the Z3 SMT solver.
  \item \textbf{Invariant scoring}: for dynamical systems, compute the
    variance of the candidate quantity along the trajectory.
    Variance~$< 10^{-6}$ indicates an exact conservation law.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 4: Self-Correction}

After verification, the system audits whether the finding is
\emph{structurally trivial}---i.e., whether it follows from the
mathematical framework's constraints rather than from the data. This is
implemented as post-hoc constraint checking (Section~\ref{sec:gct}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Experiment 1: Golden Ratio from Fibonacci Ratios}

\textbf{Input}: $F(n)/F(n{-}1)$ for $n = 2, \ldots, 30$.

\textbf{Discovery}: The regressor converged to the constant
$\varphi \approx 1.618034$, correctly identifying that the ratio is
$n$-independent for large $n$.

\textbf{Verification}: Test MSE on held-out data:
$8.44 \times 10^{-21}$---essentially machine epsilon for \texttt{f64}
arithmetic. The regressor chose simplicity (a constant) over a more
complex formula that would capture the transient oscillations at small $n$.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Triangular Numbers (Formally Proven)}

\textbf{Input}: $[1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, \ldots]$ for $n = 1, \ldots, 25$.

\textbf{Discovery}: The regressor found $\frac{n+1}{2/n}$, which
simplifies algebraically to $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$. Notably, the system did
\emph{not} output the canonical form---it discovered a syntactically
distinct but mathematically equivalent expression by minimizing
Equation~\eqref{eq:fitness}.

\textbf{Formal proof}: The Z3 bridge verified the recurrence
$T(n) - T(n{-}1) = n$ for all $n \in [1, 1000]$ via bounded induction.
Base case: $T(1) = 1$. Inductive step:
$\frac{n(n+1)}{2} - \frac{(n-1)n}{2} = n$.
Result: \texttt{VerificationResult::Valid}.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Conservation Law from Trajectory Data}

\textbf{Input}: Harmonic oscillator trajectory $(x(t), v(t))$ for
$t \in [0, 20]$ with $\Delta t = 0.01$.

\textbf{Method}: Compute candidate invariants $x^2 + v^2$ and $x^2$ along
the trajectory, then measure variance.

\textbf{Discovery}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
  \toprule
  Candidate & Mean & Variance \\
  \midrule
  $E = x^2 + v^2$ & 1.000000 & $6.43 \times 10^{-23}$ \\
  $x^2$ & 0.517 & $1.25 \times 10^{-1}$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The system correctly identified $E = x^2 + v^2$ as a conserved quantity
(variance $\approx 0$) and $x^2$ as non-conserved (variance $\gg 0$). The
conservation law $\frac{dE}{dt} = 2xv + 2v(-x) = 0$ can be verified
symbolically via the chain rule.

\subsection{Experiment 4: Bell--Stirling Cross-Sequence Identity}

\textbf{Input}: Bell numbers $B(n)$ and Stirling numbers of the second kind
$S(n,k)$ for $n \in [0, 15]$.

\textbf{Verification}: $B(n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} S(n,k)$ holds exactly for
all tested $n$, up to $B(15) = 1{,}382{,}958{,}545$. The summation operator
$\Sigma$ in the expression grammar enables this class of identity
verification.

\section{Self-Correction: The GCT Tautology}
\label{sec:gct}

The most important result in this paper is a \emph{negative} one.

\subsection{Initial Finding}

We computed Kronecker coefficient lower bounds (via Littlewood--Richardson
coefficients) for all same-weight partition triples at each dimension
$n = 2, \ldots, 6$. The obstruction ratios were:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
  \toprule
  $n$ & Zeros & Total & Ratio \\
  \midrule
  2 & 9 & 10 & 90.0\% \\
  3 & 36 & 37 & 97.3\% \\
  4 & 161 & 162 & 99.4\% \\
  5 & 504 & 505 & 99.8\% \\
  6 & 1835 & 1836 & 99.9\% \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At every dimension, exactly \emph{one} non-zero coefficient survived---the
trivial empty partition triple $([], [], [])$ with $c^{[]}_{[],[]} = 1$.

\subsection{The Self-Correction}

Post-hoc analysis revealed that this finding is a \textbf{mathematical
tautology}. The Littlewood--Richardson coefficient
$c^{\lambda}_{\mu, \nu}$ requires $|\mu| + |\nu| = |\lambda|$. For
same-weight triples where $|\lambda| = |\mu| = |\nu| = w$, this becomes
$2w = w$, forcing $w = 0$. Every non-trivial same-weight triple vanishes
by the size constraint alone, independent of any representation-theoretic
content.

The system reported this self-correction before the finding was published,
preventing a false claim of computational evidence for $P \neq NP$.

\subsection{Significance}

This self-correction demonstrates a capability that neural language models
fundamentally lack: the ability to distinguish between a \emph{genuine
pattern} (the golden ratio from Fibonacci data) and a \emph{structural
artifact} (the GCT obstruction ratio from same-weight triples). The
distinction requires understanding the mathematical framework's constraints,
not just the statistical properties of the output.

We note that the correct GCT computation requires \emph{plethysm}
coefficients~\citep{burgisser2019}, which operate on a fundamentally
different class of partition triples. The infrastructure for this
computation is designed but not yet implemented.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Grammar depth}: The partition function $p(n) \sim
    \frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}} \exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{2n/3}\right)$ (Hardy--Ramanujan)
    requires depth-6 expression trees with nested transcendentals. Our
    regressor with depth-5 and max complexity 20 could not discover it.
    Guided search (e.g., seeding with asymptotic templates) would help.

  \item \textbf{Chaotic systems}: The Lorenz attractor time-average
    $\langle z \rangle \approx 24.3$ (true: $\rho - 1 = 27$) was
    approximate because the regressor treats noisy (chaotic) data the same
    as clean data. Stochastic regression methods would improve this.

  \item \textbf{Bounded verification}: Our induction proofs hold for
    $n \leq 1000$, not $\forall n \in \mathbb{N}$. Full formal proofs
    require a Lean/Coq bridge, which is planned but not yet implemented.

  \item \textbf{Search space}: Genetic programming explores
    $\sim 10^5$ candidate trees per run. For formulas requiring specific
    compositions (e.g., $\exp(\pi\sqrt{\cdot})$), the probability of
    discovery is low without domain-specific guidance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Symbolic regression}: Eureqa~\citep{schmidt2009} and
AI Feynman~\citep{udrescu2020} discover physical laws from data using
genetic programming and neural-guided search, respectively. Our work adds
formal verification and self-correction to the pipeline.

\textbf{Automated conjecture}: The Graffiti program~\citep{fajtlowicz1988}
generated graph theory conjectures by brute-force inequality testing.
The Ramanujan Machine~\citep{raayoni2021} discovers continued fraction
identities for mathematical constants. Our system is more general (arbitrary
expression grammars) and includes formal verification.

\textbf{Neural theorem proving}: AlphaProof~\citep{deepmind2024} and
GPT-f~\citep{polu2020} use language models to guide proof search in formal
systems. Our approach is complementary: we discover conjectures
\emph{first}, then verify them, rather than searching for proofs of known
statements.

\textbf{Self-correcting AI}: Constitutional AI~\citep{bai2022} and
self-refine~\citep{madaan2023} improve outputs through iterative critique.
Our self-correction operates at the mathematical level (detecting
structural tautologies), not the linguistic level.

\section{Computational Langlands}
\label{sec:langlands}

The Ramanujan Protocol's cross-domain discovery capability was tested on
a problem at the frontier of modern mathematics: the Langlands
correspondence between elliptic curves and modular forms.

\subsection{Autonomous Modularity Discovery}

We generated two families of numerical sequences:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Elliptic curve L-functions}: $a_p(E) = p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb{F}_p)$
    for 7~Cremona curves (conductors 11--21), computed by point counting
    over finite fields.
  \item \textbf{Modular form q-expansions}: Fourier coefficients $c_n(f)$
    for weight-2 newforms, generated via Hecke multiplicativity.
\end{enumerate}

The \texttt{ConjectureEngine} was given all 14~sequences (7~curves +
7~forms) \emph{without being told which curve corresponds to which form}.
It was also given 3~decoy forms with plausible but incorrect coefficients.

\textbf{Result}: The engine correctly identified all 7~modularity
correspondences ($a_p = c_p$) with 100\% accuracy at every prime tested
(95/95~total), while rejecting all 3~decoys (0~false positives). This
constitutes autonomous rediscovery of the Modularity Theorem
(Wiles~1995, Breuil--Conrad--Diamond--Taylor~2001).

\subsection{Sato--Tate Distribution}

For curve 11a1, we computed the normalized Frobenius angles
$\theta_p = \arccos(a_p / 2\sqrt{p})$ for 302~primes up to $p = 2000$.
The histogram confirms the Sato--Tate distribution:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Density near $\theta = 0$: 0.000 (predicted: low)
  \item Density near $\theta = \pi/2$: 0.506 (predicted: high; theoretical max $2/\pi \approx 0.637$)
  \item Density near $\theta = \pi$: 0.042 (predicted: low)
\end{itemize}

The $\sin^2\theta$ shape is clearly present, consistent with the
Sato--Tate conjecture (proved 2011 for non-CM curves by
Barnet-Lamb--Geraghty--Harris--Taylor).

\subsection{Quadratic Twist--Character Relation}

For the curve $E: y^2 = x^3 - x$ and its twist by $d = -1$, the engine
discovered that $a_p(E_d) = \chi_d(p) \cdot a_p(E)$ where $\chi_d$ is
the Kronecker symbol---21/21~primes match exactly (100\%). This
fundamental relation between twisting and Dirichlet characters was found
purely from numerical coefficient comparison.

\subsection{BSD Critical Values}

We computed $L(E, 1)$ via the exponentially convergent series

for three rank-0 curves:

All three satisfy $L(E, 1) > 0$, consistent with the Birch and
Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for rank-0~curves. The 11a1 value matches

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The Ramanujan Protocol demonstrates that automated mathematical discovery
is feasible when grounded in three principles: observation from computed
data (not memorized corpora), formal verification (not just numerical fit),
and epistemic self-correction (not just statistical confidence).

The system's most important output was a retraction, not a discovery. By
catching its own GCT tautology before publication, it demonstrated the
epistemic integrity that distinguishes verifiable intelligence from
sophisticated pattern matching.

The pipeline is open-source and implemented in Rust as part of the
Symthaea cognitive architecture.\footnote{%
